# Blog

The Blog is the most flexible editorial form in the Monad publication system.

It is where complex project ideas can be explained in accessible prose, engineering developments can be synthesized for readers, and broader arguments can be explored without pretending to be formal specifications or research reports.

## What belongs here

Blog posts are appropriate for:

- explanatory essays,
- technical commentary,
- project synthesis,
- accessible introductions to difficult concepts,
- architectural interpretation,
- lessons learned,
- reflective engineering writing,
- opinionated but supportable arguments,
- and narrative pieces that connect multiple parts of the project.

A Blog post can say more than a Changelog and can be more polished than a Journal entry, while remaining less formal than a Research article or White Paper.

## What does not belong here

Use another section when the primary job is different:

- current technical truth → [Documentation](/docs),
- formal engineering record → [Artifacts](/docs/artifacts),
- chronological work-in-progress → [Engineering Journal](/journal),
- release/change impact → [Changelogs](/changelogs),
- structured investigation → [Research](/articles/research),
- formal sustained thesis → [White Papers](/articles/white-papers).

## Editorial standard

A strong Blog post should usually have a clear reason to exist beyond reporting that work occurred.

It should help a reader understand something.

That may mean:

- explaining why a problem is harder than it first appears,
- connecting several engineering decisions into one coherent picture,
- making a technical concept understandable without reading every artifact,
- challenging a common assumption,
- or extracting a durable lesson from project work.

The prose may be interpretive, but it should not blur the boundary between interpretation and accepted project truth.

## Chronology

Published Blog posts are listed newest first after this landing page.

Publication date represents when the article first crossed the public editorial boundary. Material revisions keep that date and add an `updated` date.
