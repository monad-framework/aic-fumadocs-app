# How Articles Work

Articles are editorial publications.

They exist to communicate ideas deliberately: explain them, investigate them, argue for or against them, synthesize evidence, and make the project intelligible at a level that raw engineering records cannot provide by themselves.

This page defines the public authoring contract for the Articles domain.

## The editorial boundary

Articles are not the place to store every piece of project knowledge.

Use the publication domain that matches the job:

| Need | Destination |
| --- | --- |
| Current authoritative technical truth | [Documentation](/docs) |
| Formal requirements, specs, ADRs, evidence, reviews | [Artifacts](/docs/artifacts) |
| Chronological working record | [Engineering Journal](/journal) |
| Release/change communication | [Changelogs](/changelogs) |
| Editorial explanation, investigation, synthesis, or argument | **Articles** |

An Article may depend on the other records. It should link to them rather than duplicating their role.

## Publication forms

Every published Article declares exactly one `type`.

### Blog — `blog`

Blog posts are the most flexible editorial form.

Good uses include:

- explanatory essays,
- technical commentary,
- project synthesis,
- accessible introductions,
- reflective pieces,
- architectural interpretation,
- lessons learned,
- and long-form narrative writing.

A Blog post may be opinionated, but significant factual or technical claims should still be traceable to evidence or authoritative project records where appropriate.

### Research — `research`

Research articles expose an investigation rather than only its conclusion.

They should make the reader able to distinguish:

- the question,
- prior understanding,
- assumptions,
- method,
- sources or evidence,
- observations,
- interpretation,
- limitations,
- uncertainty,
- competing explanations,
- conclusions,
- and unresolved questions.

A Research article is not automatically authoritative just because it is rigorous.

Research can conclude that the evidence is insufficient.

That is a valid result.

### White Paper — `white-paper`

White Papers carry the highest editorial burden in the Articles domain.

They are appropriate for a sustained technical thesis, system proposal, conceptual model, protocol argument, or other publication intended to remain useful as a durable public reference.

A White Paper should normally establish:

1. problem and scope,
2. terminology,
3. assumptions,
4. context and prior art,
5. thesis or proposal,
6. supporting argument and evidence,
7. tradeoffs and alternatives,
8. limitations,
9. implications,
10. and references or related project records.

A White Paper can describe or argue for a system direction without replacing the accepted specifications or current System documentation.

## Public article identity

Each published article receives a durable ID:

```text
ART-YYYY-MM-DD-NNN
```

Example:

```text
ART-2026-08-30-001
```

The ID identifies the publication record.

It should not change because:

- the title changes,
- the file slug changes,
- the article is revised,
- the article becomes part of a series,
- or its editorial status changes from `published` to `revised`.

## Filenames

Published articles should use:

```text
YYYY-MM-DD-NNN-short-descriptive-slug.mdx
```

Example:

```text
2026-08-30-001-four-publication-domains.mdx
```

The date and sequence make chronology obvious in the repository while the durable `ART-*` identifier remains the semantic publication identity.

## Public frontmatter

A normal published article can look like this:

```yaml
---
title: Why Monad Has Four Publication Domains
description: Why documentation, journal entries, changelogs, and editorial articles should not be treated as interchangeable records.
article:
  id: ART-2026-08-30-001
  type: blog
  status: published
  published: 2026-08-30
  author: Thomas Carter
  audience:
    - Builders
    - Architects
  topics:
    - engineering knowledge
    - documentation architecture
  featured: false
  readingMinutes: 8
  related:
    - label: Engineering Journal
      href: /journal
    - label: Changelogs
      href: /changelogs
---
```

Only actual publication pages need the `article` block.

Landing pages and this format guide intentionally omit it.

## Metadata fields

### `id`

Required for published articles.

Format:

```text
ART-YYYY-MM-DD-NNN
```

### `type`

Required.

One of:

```text
blog
research
white-paper
```

The type should match the publication family in which the file lives.

### `status`

Required.

Public states are:

```text
published
revised
archived
```

These are deliberately different from internal editorial workflow states.

### `published`

Required.

The original public publication date in:

```text
YYYY-MM-DD
```

This date remains stable after revisions.

### `updated`

Optional.

Use it for a material revision to an already published article.

Do not change `published` to the revision date.

### `author`

Required.

The public byline.

### `audience`

Optional list of intended reader groups.

Use this to help readers understand who is most likely to benefit from the article, not to restrict who may read it.

Examples:

```text
Builders
Architects
Implementers
Historians
Researchers
AI engineers
Engineering leaders
```

### `topics`

Optional editorial taxonomy.

Topics should describe the subject matter, not repeat the publication type.

Good examples:

```text
semantic identity
provenance
AI-assisted engineering
local-first systems
engineering knowledge
system architecture
```

### `featured`

Boolean used for future editorial curation.

Default:

```text
false
```

A featured article is not more authoritative than another article. It is simply being promoted editorially.

### `readingMinutes`

Optional positive integer representing an estimated reading time.

It is intentionally stored as minutes rather than a prose string so future listing and filtering interfaces can use it consistently.

### `series`

Optional.

Example:

```yaml
series:
  name: Building Software That Understands Itself
  position: 2
  href: /articles/blog
```

A series relationship is editorial ordering, not authority or dependency.

### `canonicalUrl`

Optional absolute URL.

Use this when the same publication appears elsewhere and one URL should be treated as canonical by search engines.

### `related`

Optional links to relevant material.

Good related links include:

- another Article,
- a Journal entry,
- a Changelog record,
- System documentation,
- an ADR,
- a requirement or specification,
- an experiment,
- an external source,
- or a project phase page.

## Editorial workflow

The full authoring workflow can include states that never appear in public metadata:

```text
idea
  ↓
draft
  ↓
editorial review
  ↓
technical / factual review
  ↓
ready
  ↓
published
  ↓
revised
  ↓
archived
```

The important distinction is this:

**A file under the public `content/articles` tree is renderable.**

Therefore draft and review states should not be represented merely by committing unfinished public MDX and hoping readers do not find it.

Until a dedicated draft-publication mechanism exists, keep unpublished drafts outside the public Articles collection.

## Revision policy

Published editorial work can change, but revision should be deliberate.

### Minor corrections

Typographical corrections, broken-link fixes, formatting changes, and other changes that do not materially alter the article's meaning do not require `status: revised`.

### Material revisions

Set:

```yaml
status: revised
updated: YYYY-MM-DD
```

when a change materially affects the argument, interpretation, evidence, recommendations, or substantive explanation.

A material revision should preferably explain the revision in the article when readers would otherwise be confused by references to an earlier edition.

### Archiving

Use:

```yaml
status: archived
```

when the article should remain publicly inspectable but is no longer recommended as current editorial guidance.

Archiving is preferable to silently rewriting historical editorial context when the older publication remains meaningful as a record.

## Articles and changing project truth

A published article may become outdated because the project changes.

That does not automatically make the article defective.

The correct response depends on its role:

- revise it if the publication is intended to remain current,
- archive it if its historical editorial value remains but its guidance is obsolete,
- link readers to newer material,
- and ensure current authoritative documentation remains correct regardless of the article's state.

The Articles domain should never become the only place where current system truth is documented.

## Evidence and citations

Different forms carry different expectations.

### Blog

Cite or link significant claims when doing so helps readers verify or investigate them.

### Research

Evidence and source traceability are part of the form itself.

A Research article should make clear which conclusions come from evidence and which remain interpretation or hypothesis.

### White Paper

A formal argument should provide enough sourcing and cross-reference structure for a critical reader to evaluate the thesis.

Project-internal claims should link to formal project artifacts where available.

## Relationship to the Engineering Journal

Journal entries preserve work close to the moment it happens.

Articles are written with more editorial distance.

A common path is:

```text
Journal observations
        ↓
engineering work + evidence
        ↓
accepted artifacts / current docs
        ↓
editorial synthesis
        ↓
Article
```

An Article should not rewrite the Journal into a cleaner fictional history.

If uncertainty, failure, or reversal mattered to the argument, preserve it.

## Relationship to Changelogs

Changelogs communicate externally meaningful deltas at publication or release boundaries.

Articles communicate understanding.

A new feature may need both:

- a Changelog entry saying what changed and whether migration is required,
- and an Article explaining why the feature matters or how to think about it.

The two records should link rather than duplicate each other.

## Relationship to Building Monad

Building Monad is the curated narrative of the project's evolution.

Articles can participate in that narrative without being nested inside the Documentation tree.

A Building Monad installment may point to:

- a Blog essay for deeper interpretation,
- a Research article for a specific investigation,
- or a White Paper for a formal thesis.

This keeps narrative organization and publication type separate.

## Editorial Definition of Done

A published Article is done when:

- its publication type is correct,
- its title and description accurately represent the content,
- its durable `ART-*` ID is unique,
- `published` reflects the original publication date,
- the author/byline is correct,
- intended audiences and topics are useful rather than decorative,
- substantial factual or technical claims are supportable,
- uncertainty is represented honestly,
- links to authoritative project records are included where useful,
- it does not masquerade as current authority,
- it does not duplicate a Journal or Changelog record unnecessarily,
- series information is correct when applicable,
- canonical URL is set when cross-publication requires it,
- the article renders successfully,
- typecheck/build pass,
- and the publication provides durable value beyond the immediate conversation or work session that produced it.

## Recommended body structures

These are defaults, not mandatory templates.

### Blog

```text
Opening / problem
Context
Main argument or explanation
Examples / implications
Tradeoffs or caveats
What this means
Related reading
```

### Research

```text
Research question
Context / prior understanding
Method
Evidence / observations
Analysis
Competing explanations
Limitations
Conclusion
Open questions
Related records / sources
```

### White Paper

```text
Abstract
Problem
Scope
Terminology
Assumptions
Background / prior art
Thesis or proposal
Model / architecture / mechanism
Evidence and argument
Alternatives and tradeoffs
Limitations
Implications
Conclusion
References
```

The structure should serve the publication's purpose rather than becoming ceremony for its own sake.
