# Research

Research articles document investigations in a form that makes the path from question to conclusion inspectable.

They are not merely polished opinions with citations added afterward. The investigation itself is part of the publication.

## What belongs here

Research is appropriate when the publication needs to expose:

- a concrete research question,
- prior understanding,
- assumptions,
- method,
- external and internal evidence,
- observations,
- interpretation,
- competing explanations,
- uncertainty,
- limitations,
- conclusions,
- and unresolved questions.

Examples might include investigations into:

- how AI-assisted engineering changes knowledge-management requirements,
- whether semantic system models provide measurable value over simpler retrieval,
- how other engineering systems model authority or provenance,
- local-first architecture tradeoffs,
- reproducibility strategies,
- agent governance models,
- or empirical behavior observed during Monad experiments.

## Research is allowed to be inconclusive

A good investigation can end with:

> The evidence is insufficient to justify the original hypothesis.

or:

> The simpler explanation currently fits the evidence better.

or:

> We identified the relevant variables but do not yet have enough evidence to choose between the competing approaches.

Those are legitimate research results.

The purpose is to increase what the project knows, not to manufacture certainty.

## Research and authority

A Research article may influence requirements, architecture, specifications, or product strategy, but publication does not make its conclusions authoritative by itself.

When research produces an accepted engineering consequence, the consequence should appear in the appropriate authoritative artifact:

```text
Research
   ↓
evidence / conclusion
   ↓
review or decision
   ↓
requirement / ADR / specification
   ↓
System documentation
```

This keeps the evidence trail visible without confusing investigation with governance.

## Research and the Journal

The Engineering Journal can capture investigation as it happens:

- questions,
- failed approaches,
- early observations,
- source discoveries,
- and evolving hypotheses.

A Research article is a more deliberate synthesis of that work.

It should not rewrite uncertainty out of the history simply because the final publication has a clearer structure.

## Research and external sources

Research articles should identify externally sourced evidence clearly enough that a critical reader can inspect it.

Where the research depends on current external facts, source freshness and publication dates matter.

Where the work depends on project-internal evidence, link to the relevant experiment, review, artifact, Journal entry, implementation, or verification record when available.

## Recommended structure

A normal Research article should consider:

1. Research question
2. Why the question matters
3. Prior understanding
4. Assumptions
5. Method
6. Evidence and observations
7. Analysis
8. Competing explanations
9. Limitations
10. Conclusion
11. Open questions
12. Related sources and project records

The exact structure may vary, but evidence and uncertainty should remain visible.
