---
title: How Building Monad Works
description: The curation contract for Building Monad reading-path types, source ownership, authority boundaries, linking rules, maintenance, and completion criteria.
---

Building Monad is a **curated view over project records**, not another source-of-truth repository.

Its job is to make the evolution of Monad understandable by selecting, sequencing, and contextualizing records that remain owned by their native publication domains.

<Callout type="info" title="Core invariant">

**Curation may add context and sequence. It must not silently acquire authority that belongs to the records being curated.**

</Callout>

## Curation objects

### Series

A Series is a stable thematic reading path. Use it when a recurring question is best answered by an ordered sequence of records that may span Docs, Artifacts, Journal, Changelogs, and Articles.

A Series is not a tag archive and does not require new source content.

### Installment

An Installment is a bounded historical snapshot of one meaningful project moment.

It should explain the before-state, the pressure or question, the important source records, the resulting state, and where the story continues.

Installments use stable sequential slugs such as:

```text
001-publication-domains
002-...
```

The number identifies the installment sequence, not an authority or release version.

### Project Phase

A Project Phase is a lifecycle-oriented reading path. The phase pages are stable orientation surfaces rather than mutable project-status records.

Current status belongs in [Project](/docs/project); chronological work belongs in [Journal](/journal).

### Essays & Editorial Reading

This surface curates [Articles](/articles) that matter to the Building Monad narrative. It must not become a second location for Blog posts, Research, or White Papers.

## Source ownership

Building Monad can point to all major public knowledge domains, but ownership remains explicit:

| Record | Owning domain |
| --- | --- |
| current system explanation | `/docs/system` |
| requirement, specification, ADR, evidence, review, verification | `/docs/artifacts` |
| chronological engineering event | `/journal` |
| release or meaningful change record | `/changelogs` |
| essay, research publication, white paper | `/articles` |
| curated sequence or interpretation of those records | `/building-monad` |

## What curation may add

A Building Monad page may add:

- historical framing,
- ordering,
- transitions between records,
- explanation of why records belong together,
- reader-specific paths,
- cross-domain context,
- bounded retrospective interpretation,
- pointers to unresolved questions.

## What curation should not duplicate

Do not copy the body of an ADR, Article, Journal entry, requirement, specification, or Changelog record into Building Monad merely to make a reading path self-contained.

Brief summaries are appropriate. The canonical record should remain one click away.

## Historical versus current statements

If a curation page describes an earlier understanding, it should make that historical position clear.

When current truth matters, link to the current authoritative System or Artifact record rather than relying on a historical summary.

## Link durability

Prefer stable semantic routes and durable record pages over ephemeral anchors, search-result URLs, or branch-specific repository links.

When a source record moves, preserve redirects where practical and update the curated link when the new canonical route is known.

## Revision behavior

Building Monad pages may be revised as the project develops, but revisions should preserve historical meaning.

A later discovery may justify adding context such as:

> This interpretation was later superseded by ADR-00XX.

It should not justify rewriting the page to pretend the earlier interpretation never existed.

## Creation test

Create or materially expand a Building Monad page only when it improves at least one of these:

- sequence,
- orientation,
- cross-domain comprehension,
- historical reconstruction,
- reader navigation.

If the proposed page is primarily new technical truth, a work log, a release note, or an essay, it belongs elsewhere.

## Definition of Done

A curated page is ready when:

- its purpose as Series, Installment, Phase, or editorial pathway is clear,
- the important source records are linked in their native domains,
- authority boundaries are not blurred,
- historical claims are framed as historical where necessary,
- the page adds context rather than duplicating source bodies,
- links resolve to durable public routes,
- the reader knows where to continue.

## Governing principle

> **Keep the records where they belong; make the relationships between them understandable here.**
