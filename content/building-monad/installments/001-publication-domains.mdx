The public knowledge system originally placed too many kinds of information inside one documentation-shaped hierarchy.

That worked mechanically, but the semantics were wrong. A reader could not reliably tell whether a page represented current authority, historical working notes, editorial explanation, or a release-oriented change record.

The solution was not another naming convention inside `/docs`. The solution was to give the major publication roles separate homes.

## The resulting model

```text
/docs             current authoritative documentation and engineering artifacts
/journal          chronological working history
/changelogs       release and meaningful change communication
/articles         editorial publishing
/building-monad   curated narrative and reading paths
```

## Why this mattered

The change made authority visible in the information architecture.

A Journal entry can now be historically valuable without pretending to be current technical truth. A Changelog can focus on impact without carrying the whole engineering narrative. An Article can interpret the work without becoming a normative specification. Building Monad can connect all of those records without owning duplicate copies.

That separation also makes the site itself a useful example of the broader Monad idea: **records with different semantic roles should remain distinguishable even when they describe the same underlying subject**.

## What this installment changes about Building Monad

Building Monad is no longer described as "part engineering journal, part essay series, part research record."

Those publication forms now have their own homes.

Building Monad becomes the composition layer above them: a place for sequence, context, project phases, thematic series, and bounded historical snapshots.
