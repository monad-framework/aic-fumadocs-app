In our last article we covered the difference between `idea.md`, `paoject.md`, and `workspace.md`.

Now that the repository has the right conceptual split, we will write `idea.md` from our raw project concept, and derive `project.md` from it.

The next step is to populate `docs/idea.md` before touching architecture or tooling.

We will use this structure:

```md
# Project Idea

## Origin

What prompted this project?

## Problem

What problem, limitation, inefficiency, or opportunity have you observed?

## Core Idea

What is the central idea for addressing it?

## Vision

What could this become if developed successfully?

## Users

Who might benefit from it?

## Desired Capabilities

What should the system eventually be able to do?

## Principles

What qualities should guide the project?

Examples:

- simple before complex
- local-first
- deterministic
- extensible
- secure by default
- automation-friendly
- human-understandable

## Possible Directions

What ideas are worth preserving even though they are not yet commitments?

## Unknowns

What important things do we not yet know?

## Non-Goals

What do we currently believe this project should not become?
```

We won't worry about making it polished. `idea.md` is allowed to contain speculation, alternatives, ambitions, and uncertainty.

The important distinction is:

```text
idea.md is exploratory truth

project.md is committed truth
```

So at this stage, I'm **not** going to derive requirements, choose Bun/Rust/Python, create `package.json`, select a framework, or decide whether this is a monorepo.
