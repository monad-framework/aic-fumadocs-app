# 1. Purpose

The AI-Native Engineering Operating System (AIEOS) defines a governed software-engineering system in which humans, AI agents, deterministic automation, development tools, repositories, CI/CD systems, and production environments cooperate to transform **intent into verified changes to operational reality**.

The system SHALL treat software engineering as a closed-loop control process:

```text
Intent
  ↓
Specification
  ↓
Architecture / Plan
  ↓
Execution
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Review
  ↓
Authorization
  ↓
Deployment
  ↓
Observation
  ↓
Learning
  ↓
New Intent
```

The AIEOS is not merely an AI coding assistant.

It is the system responsible for:

* engineering workflow,
* artifact lifecycle,
* agent orchestration,
* policy enforcement,
* authority management,
* evidence collection,
* verification,
* provenance,
* human approvals,
* deployment governance,
* operational feedback,
* organizational learning.

Its objective is:

> **Continuously transform human intent into safe, verified, traceable changes while maximizing useful automation without surrendering human authority where judgment or accountability is required.**

# 2. Normative Language

The terms **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHALL**, **SHALL NOT**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**, **MAY**, and **OPTIONAL** are normative.

* **MUST / SHALL** — mandatory requirement.
* **SHOULD** — expected unless a documented reason exists otherwise.
* **MAY** — optional.
* **MUST NOT / SHALL NOT** — prohibited behavior.

# 4. Primary Architectural Principle

The AIEOS SHALL distinguish:

```text
Capability
    ≠
Authority
    ≠
Execution
    ≠
Verification
    ≠
Approval
```

An entity capable of performing an action MUST NOT automatically be considered authorized to perform that action.

An entity authorized to execute a change MUST NOT automatically be authorized to approve the correctness of that change.

An entity capable of reviewing a change MUST NOT automatically be authorized to release that change.

This separation is one of the foundational invariants of the system.

# 5. Core Principles

The AIEOS SHALL implement the following principles.

## P-001 — Intent before implementation

Engineering work MUST originate from an identifiable desired outcome, problem, incident, obligation, or hypothesis.

Implementation SHALL NOT be treated as the initial authoritative statement of intent.

---

## P-002 — Explicit artifacts

Important engineering knowledge SHALL be represented by identifiable artifacts rather than existing solely in transient conversation or human memory.

---

## P-003 — Machine-operable knowledge

Artifacts SHOULD be understandable by both humans and machines.

Structured metadata SHALL be used where automation depends upon the information.

Human-readable representation SHOULD remain available wherever practical.

---

## P-004 — One authority per assertion

For each authoritative engineering assertion, the system SHALL identify its source of authority.

Multiple representations MAY exist.

Multiple simultaneous authoritative sources for the same assertion SHOULD be avoided.

Where multiple authoritative claims conflict, the conflict MUST be surfaced rather than silently resolved.

---

## P-005 — Plan before mutation

Non-trivial implementation work SHOULD have an accepted execution plan before mutable execution begins.

Higher-risk work MUST have an accepted plan.

---

## P-006 — Evidence over assertion

An agent asserting that work is correct SHALL NOT constitute sufficient evidence of correctness.

Correctness SHALL be established using independently observable evidence.

---

## P-007 — Deterministic enforcement for invariants

Policies that MUST always hold SHALL NOT depend solely on model instructions.

They SHALL be enforced through deterministic mechanisms where technically possible.

---

## P-008 — Risk-adaptive autonomy

Automation SHALL be proportional to risk.

Low-risk, reversible changes MAY progress autonomously.

High-impact, irreversible, security-sensitive, destructive, or production-critical changes SHALL require stronger controls.

---

## P-009 — Separation of duties

Where risk warrants it:

```text
Executor ≠ Reviewer ≠ Approver
```

The same agent SHOULD NOT independently implement and certify its own work as sufficient for consequential deployment.

---

## P-010 — Everything consequential is traceable

The system SHALL maintain provenance for consequential:

* artifacts,
* decisions,
* executions,
* reviews,
* approvals,
* deployments,
* observations,
* policy evaluations.

---

## P-011 — Production closes the loop

Production behavior SHALL be considered engineering evidence.

Operational failures, regressions, and meaningful discoveries SHOULD feed back into requirements, tests, evaluations, policies, or future intent.

---

## P-012 — Improve the harness

Recurring agent failures SHOULD trigger improvement of:

```text
instructions
context
tools
tests
policies
architecture
evaluations
workflows
```

rather than relying indefinitely on repeated human correction.

# 10. Risk Classification

Every work unit SHOULD have a risk classification.

Baseline:

```text
R0 — Minimal
R1 — Routine
R2 — Significant
R3 — Critical
```

## R0 — Minimal

Examples:

* documentation,
* comments,
* formatting,
* generated metadata,
* reversible internal changes.

May permit high autonomy.

---

## R1 — Routine

Examples:

* normal feature development,
* ordinary bug fixes,
* non-critical refactoring.

Typically permits agent execution with automated verification and human or policy-controlled approval.

---

## R2 — Significant

Examples:

* authentication,
* authorization,
* persistent data migrations,
* security controls,
* externally visible API changes,
* significant infrastructure modifications.

Requires explicit additional review and authorization.

---

## R3 — Critical

Examples:

* destructive production operations,
* credential or identity infrastructure,
* irreversible migrations,
* safety-critical systems,
* critical security infrastructure,
* major production architecture changes.

Requires strong human authority boundaries.

Organizations MAY define additional risk levels.

# 11. Human Authority Model

Human participation SHALL be concentrated where human judgment has the highest value.

Humans SHOULD own:

### Intent acceptance

Does this work actually need to happen?

### Strategic prioritization

Should this work happen now?

### Ambiguous requirements

What does the organization actually want?

### Architectural trade-offs

Which competing qualities matter most?

### Policy exceptions

Should a rule be overridden?

### Risk acceptance

Is remaining risk acceptable?

### High-impact release authorization

Should the change affect operational reality?

Humans SHOULD NOT be required to manually perform deterministic clerical work merely because historic processes required it.

* task-specific,
* environment-specific,

# 13. Policy Model

Policies SHALL be divided into at least three categories.

## Advisory policy

Provides reasoning guidance.

```text
Prefer existing abstractions.
```

## Testable policy

Can be automatically evaluated.

```text
Every public API endpoint must have an integration test.
```

## Enforced invariant

Cannot be violated through the permitted execution environment.

```text
Agent execution credentials cannot deploy to production.
```

The system SHOULD progressively convert important advisory rules into testable or enforced policies.



# 21. Deployment Stage

Deployment SHOULD proceed automatically once all required gates have passed, unless policy requires further human intervention.

A mature pipeline may therefore look like:

```text
Verified change
      ↓
Review accepted
      ↓
Authorization
      ↓
Deploy staging
      ↓
Health checks
      ↓
Canary
      ↓
Observe
      ↓
Production promotion
```

Contemporary CI/CD platforms already support this model. For example, GitHub environments can require human reviewers, prevent self-approval, restrict branches, delay deployment, withhold environment secrets until approval, and use external automated protection rules.

# 26. Workflow Engine

The workflow engine SHALL be deterministic.

It SHALL own lifecycle state.

Agents MAY recommend transitions.

Agents MUST NOT directly mutate authoritative lifecycle state unless explicitly permitted by policy.

The workflow engine SHALL determine whether:

```text
transition allowed?
required artifacts present?
required evidence valid?
policy satisfied?
authorization present?
```

# 28. Policy Engine

The policy engine SHALL answer questions such as:

```text
May this work enter execution?

What evidence is mandatory?

Does this require security review?

May this agent modify these files?

Can this change auto-merge?

Who may approve this?

Can this release enter staging?

Can this release enter production?
```

Policies SHOULD be declarative.

Example:

```yaml
policy: production-deployment

when:
  risk: R2

requires:
  review:
    security: accepted

  evidence:
    integration-tests: passed
    security-scan: passed

  authorization:
    human:
      role: release-owner

deny:
  self_approval: true
```

# 33. What MUST NOT Be Fully Automated by Default

The AIEOS SHOULD NOT initially automate away:

```text
strategic intent
organizational priorities
ethical judgment
ambiguous product decisions
policy exceptions
risk acceptance
high-consequence irreversible actions
critical production authorization
```

These may be AI-assisted.

They should remain explicitly attributable to accountable human authorities unless an organization deliberately adopts another governance model.

---

# 34. Why Full Automation Is Not the Objective

The optimization objective is not:

```text
maximize number of automated steps
```

It is:

```text
maximize safe autonomous throughput
subject to:
    correctness
    governance
    traceability
    security
    human authority
```

A workflow with 95% automation and excellent authority boundaries is preferable to 100% automation with unclear accountability.

Likewise, a workflow with unnecessary human approvals is not inherently safer.

Excessive human gates produce:

```text
rubber-stamping
review fatigue
queue buildup
loss of attention
slow feedback
false sense of safety
```

Humans should appear where judgment creates substantial value.

No high-risk implementation without accepted plan.

### INV-004

No claim of correctness without evidence.

# 38. Failure Modes

The system SHALL explicitly protect against:

## Automation theater

The workflow looks automated but humans still perform hidden manual reconciliation.

## Agent self-certification

The same agent generates work and declares it correct without external evidence.

## Authority leakage

An agent receives credentials broader than its authorized role.

## Stale institutional knowledge

Agents follow outdated architecture or policy.

## Spec drift

Implementation no longer corresponds to accepted intent.

## Evidence gaming

Tests demonstrate something passed without proving the actual requirement.

## Human rubber-stamping

Approval volume exceeds meaningful human attention.

## Runaway execution

Agents continue retries or modifications without bounded execution policy.

## Silent policy conflict

Contradictory requirements are arbitrarily resolved.

## Duplicate authority

Two systems claim authoritative ownership of the same engineering fact.

## Lost provenance

No one can reconstruct why a change occurred.

# 44. Definition of Ready

A work unit is **Ready for Autonomous Execution** when:

* intent is accepted;
* applicable requirements are explicit;
* contradictions are resolved or surfaced;
* risk is classified;
* required architecture decisions exist;
* execution plan exists;
* acceptance criteria are testable;
* required tools are available;
* required execution capabilities are authorized;
* applicable policies are known;
* execution can occur in a bounded environment.

---

# 45. Definition of Done

A work unit is **Done** when:

* accepted intent is satisfied;
* requirements are traceably implemented;
* verification criteria pass;
* required evidence exists;
* required reviews are accepted;
* required authorization exists;
* deployment has reached its target state;
* operational verification has completed;
* documentation and knowledge artifacts are updated;
* provenance is complete;
* unresolved residual risks are explicitly recorded;
* follow-up work has been created where necessary.

# 49. Central Thesis

The purpose of an AI-native engineering system is not to remove humans from software engineering.

It is to remove humans from the parts of software engineering where human attention is unnecessary while making human involvement **more explicit, more informed, and more authoritative** where judgment matters.

The desired evolution is:

```text
Human performs workflow
        ↓
Human assisted by AI
        ↓
AI performs tasks
        ↓
Application orchestrates AI tasks
        ↓
Application operates engineering lifecycle
        ↓
Humans govern the lifecycle
```

The optimal destination is therefore not:

```text
Fully Autonomous Software Factory
```

but:

```text
Governed Autonomous Engineering System
```

—a persistent engineering control plane capable of moving software from intent through implementation into verified operational reality while preserving human judgment, explicit authority, evidence, provenance, and control.
