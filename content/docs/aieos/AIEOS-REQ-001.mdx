# AIEOS System Requirements Specification

**Specification ID:** AIEOS-REQ-001
**Title:** AI-Native Engineering Operating System — System Requirements Specification
**Version:** 0.2.0
**Status:** Draft
**Classification:** Normative Requirements Specification
**Parent:** AIEOS-FOUND-001 — AI-Native Engineering Operating System Foundation Specification

---

# 1. Purpose

This specification defines the normative system requirements for the **AI-Native Engineering Operating System (AIEOS)**.

AIEOS is a governed engineering control system in which humans, AI agents, deterministic automation, repositories, engineering tools, delivery systems, and operational environments cooperate to transform intent into verified changes to operational reality.

This document defines **what AIEOS must do and guarantee**.

It intentionally does not prescribe:

* concrete implementation technologies,
* database products,
* programming languages,
* deployment platforms,
* UI frameworks,
* agent vendors,
* model providers,
* repository hosting providers,
* workflow-engine implementations,
* physical service boundaries.

Those concerns belong to subordinate architecture and implementation specifications.

---

# 2. Relationship to the Foundation

`AIEOS-FOUND-001` establishes the governing principles and conceptual operating model of AIEOS.

This document translates those principles into individually identifiable and testable requirements.

The dependency is:

```text
AIEOS-FOUND-001
      ↓
AIEOS-REQ-001
      ↓
AIEOS-DOM-001
      ↓
AIEOS-ARCH-001
      ↓
subordinate specifications
      ↓
implementation
      ↓
verification
```

Where a conflict exists between this specification and `AIEOS-FOUND-001`, the conflict MUST be surfaced and resolved through an explicit specification change.

It MUST NOT be silently interpreted away.

# 4. Out of Scope

The following are not defined by this specification:

## 4.1 Project-management methodology

AIEOS MUST NOT require Scrum, Kanban, SAFe, Shape Up, Waterfall, or another specific planning framework.

These MAY be implemented as workflow projections or integrations.

## 4.2 Source-control vendor

AIEOS MUST NOT fundamentally depend upon GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, or another specific provider.

## 4.3 AI provider

AIEOS MUST NOT require one specific AI model or provider.

## 4.4 CI/CD vendor

AIEOS MUST NOT require one specific CI/CD system.

## 4.5 Cloud platform

AIEOS MUST NOT require one specific deployment environment or cloud provider.

## 4.6 Complete organizational governance

AIEOS governs engineering activities within its configured authority boundary.

It does not attempt to replace corporate, legal, financial, personnel, or executive governance systems.

# 5. Normative Language

The terms **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHALL**, **SHALL NOT**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**, and **MAY** are normative.

Requirements identified with a `REQ-*` identifier are normative unless explicitly marked informational.

# 6. Requirement Structure

Each requirement has a stable identifier.

Requirement identifiers SHALL use the form:

```text
REQ-<FAMILY>-<NUMBER>
```

For example:

```text
REQ-WF-001
REQ-AUTHZ-004
REQ-VER-012
```

Requirement identifiers MUST remain stable once accepted.

If a requirement is removed, its identifier MUST NOT be reassigned to an unrelated requirement.

# 8. System-Wide Requirements

## REQ-SYS-001 — Governed engineering lifecycle

AIEOS MUST support a governed engineering lifecycle from identifiable intent through operational observation.

---

## REQ-SYS-002 — Closed-loop operation

AIEOS MUST permit operational observations, incidents, discoveries, and evaluation results to produce new engineering input.

---

## REQ-SYS-003 — Human-agent-system distinction

AIEOS MUST preserve a conceptual distinction between:

* human actors,
* AI agents,
* deterministic automation,
* external systems.

---

## REQ-SYS-004 — Capability/authority separation

AIEOS MUST distinguish technical capability from granted authority.

Possession of a capability MUST NOT by itself imply authorization to use it.

---

## REQ-SYS-005 — Execution/review/approval separation

AIEOS MUST be capable of assigning execution, review, and approval to distinct actors.

---

## REQ-SYS-006 — Deterministic governance

Authoritative lifecycle state changes and policy-enforced governance decisions MUST be determined through deterministic system rules rather than unverified model judgment alone.

---

## REQ-SYS-007 — Agent recommendations are non-authoritative by default

An AI agent MAY recommend decisions, transitions, risk levels, approvals, or exceptions.

Such recommendations MUST NOT become authoritative solely because the agent generated them.

---

## REQ-SYS-008 — Explicit exceptional paths

AIEOS MUST represent exceptions, overrides, bypasses, and emergency actions explicitly.

They MUST NOT occur as invisible deviations from normal workflow.

---

## REQ-SYS-009 — Inspectability

The current engineering state of a governed work unit MUST be inspectable.

---

## REQ-SYS-010 — Reconstructability

AIEOS SHOULD make it possible to reconstruct how a consequential engineering outcome was reached from retained artifacts, events, evidence, and decisions.

---

# 9. Identity Requirements

## REQ-ID-001 — Stable subject identity

Every first-class governed Subject MUST possess a stable semantic identity appropriate to its lifecycle and traceability requirements.

---

## REQ-ID-002 — Location-independent identity

The semantic identity of a governed Subject MUST NOT depend exclusively upon a mutable representation or physical location such as:

* filename,
* repository path,
* issue number,
* URL,
* database row position,
* temporary execution location,
* infrastructure address.

---

## REQ-ID-003 — External identity bindings

AIEOS MUST permit an internal subject to be bound to one or more native identifiers in external systems.

---

## REQ-ID-004 — Identity preservation

Moving, renaming, reformatting, regenerating, or projecting an artifact MUST NOT automatically change its semantic identity.

---

## REQ-ID-005 — Identity history

Changes to identity bindings SHOULD preserve historical traceability.

---

## REQ-ID-006 — No silent identity merge

AIEOS MUST NOT silently merge semantically distinct subjects because external references appear equivalent.

---

## REQ-ID-007 — Split and merge support

The identity model MUST be capable of representing semantic split and merge events without destroying historical provenance.

---

## REQ-ID-008 — Human-readable references

The system SHOULD support human-readable aliases in addition to stable canonical identifiers.

---

# 10. Work Unit Requirements

## REQ-WU-001 — Governed work unit

AIEOS MUST provide a persistent concept representing one governed unit of engineering work.

---

## REQ-WU-002 — Intent association

Every active governed work unit MUST be associated with identifiable intent.

---

## REQ-WU-003 — Work-unit coordination status

A Work Unit MUST expose an explicit coordination status sufficient to communicate its current engineering condition.

That status MAY be derived from one or more governing Workflow Instances.

It MUST NOT replace or ambiguously overwrite the authoritative Lifecycle State of a Workflow Instance.

---

## REQ-WU-004 — Work-unit ownership context

A work unit MUST be capable of recording accountable human ownership or governing authority where applicable.

---

## REQ-WU-005 — Parent/child decomposition

A work unit MUST be capable of being decomposed into subordinate work units without losing traceability to originating intent.

---

## REQ-WU-006 — Dependencies

AIEOS MUST support explicit dependencies between work units.

---

## REQ-WU-007 — Blocking conditions

A work unit MUST be capable of entering an explicit blocked state with one or more identified blocking conditions.

---

## REQ-WU-008 — Cancellation

Cancellation of work MUST be explicit and recorded.

---

## REQ-WU-009 — Supersession

A work unit MAY supersede another work unit while preserving the superseded work's historical record.

---

---

Consequential Assertions MUST be capable of retaining provenance.

---

## REQ-ASSERT-006 — Uncertain propositions

## REQ-ASSERT-008 — No automatic authority promotion

# 14. Semantic Authority Requirements

## REQ-AUTHORITY-001 — Authority attribution

AIEOS MUST be capable of identifying the authority source for authoritative engineering assertions.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-002 — Authority scope

Authority MUST be capable of applying at finer granularity than an entire artifact.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-003 — Multiple representations

Multiple representations of the same subject MAY coexist.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-004 — Canonical authority

Where multiple representations exist, AIEOS MUST be capable of determining which source is authoritative for a governed assertion.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-005 — Authority conflict

Conflicting authoritative claims MUST be surfaced.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-006 — No silent conflict resolution

AIEOS MUST NOT silently choose between conflicting authoritative claims unless an explicit deterministic resolution policy authorizes that choice.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-007 — Derived information

Derived information MUST remain distinguishable from its authoritative source information.

---

## REQ-AUTHORITY-008 — Projection status

Generated or projected views MUST NOT automatically become authoritative merely because they are convenient representations.

---

# 15. Workflow Requirements

## REQ-WF-001 — Explicit lifecycle

Every governed workflow MUST define explicit states and permitted transitions.

---

## REQ-WF-002 — Deterministic transition evaluation

Transition eligibility MUST be evaluated deterministically against workflow and policy state.

---

## REQ-WF-003 — Transition prerequisites

A transition MUST be capable of requiring:

* artifacts,
* evidence,
* policy satisfaction,
* authorization,
* actor qualification,
* risk conditions.

---

## REQ-WF-004 — Transition provenance

Every authoritative lifecycle transition MUST produce an auditable record.

---

## REQ-WF-005 — Agent transition recommendation

Agents MAY recommend state transitions.

AIEOS MUST independently determine whether a proposed transition is permissible under the applicable Workflow Definition, Policy, Evidence, Risk, and Operational Authorization state.

---

## REQ-WF-006 — Blocking

A workflow MUST support explicit blocked states.

---

## REQ-WF-007 — Failure

A workflow MUST support explicit failure states.

---

## REQ-WF-008 — Retry

Retry behavior MUST be bounded and policy-controlled.

---

## REQ-WF-009 — Cancellation

Workflow execution MUST support cancellation subject to applicable safety constraints.

---

## REQ-WF-010 — Rollback

Workflows involving reversible operational change SHOULD support explicit rollback paths.

---

## REQ-WF-011 — Workflow specialization

AIEOS MUST support multiple engineering workflows on the same underlying control plane.

Examples MAY include:

* feature development,
* defect remediation,
* incident response,
* security remediation,
* dependency upgrades,
* architecture evolution,
* research experiments.

---

## REQ-WF-012 — Workflow versioning

Workflow definitions MUST be versionable.

---

## REQ-WF-013 — In-flight workflow stability

Changes to workflow definitions MUST NOT ambiguously reinterpret already-recorded historical transitions.

---

## REQ-WF-014 — Workflow definition distinction

AIEOS MUST distinguish a versioned Workflow Definition from a concrete Workflow Instance.

---

## REQ-WF-015 — Workflow-instance binding

Each Workflow Instance MUST identify the Workflow Definition version governing it.

---

## REQ-WF-016 — Historical definition stability

Later modification of a Workflow Definition MUST NOT reinterpret historical transitions previously executed by existing Workflow Instances.

---

## REQ-WF-017 — Workflow-instance state

Authoritative lifecycle state MUST belong to a specific Workflow Instance.

---

# 16. Human Participation Requirements

## REQ-HUM-001 — Human authority boundaries

AIEOS MUST support workflow gates requiring explicit human action.

---

## REQ-HUM-002 — Human identity

Human decisions MUST be attributable to an authenticated actor.

---

## REQ-HUM-003 — Human judgment concentration

The system SHOULD minimize human involvement in deterministic clerical actions that can be safely automated.

---

## REQ-HUM-004 — Decision context

When human judgment is requested, AIEOS SHOULD provide sufficient context to make the decision meaningfully.

---

## REQ-HUM-005 — Decision rationale

Policy MAY require a human decision to include rationale.

---

## REQ-HUM-006 — Decision alternatives

A human approval interface SHOULD support at least:

```text
Approve
Reject
Request changes
Escalate
```

where applicable.

---

## REQ-HUM-007 — No assumed approval

Lack of human response MUST NOT be interpreted as approval unless an explicit policy states otherwise.

---

## REQ-HUM-008 — Human override

Human override of automated outcomes MUST be explicit and governed by authority policy.

---

---

## REQ-AGENT-002 — Provider abstraction

The core system MUST NOT require one particular model provider.

---

## REQ-AGENT-003 — Role specialization

AIEOS MUST permit different logical agent roles.

Different agent roles MAY receive different:

* tools,

* authority.

---

## REQ-AGENT-005 — Agent context

AIEOS MUST be capable of supplying an agent with task-relevant engineering context.

---

## REQ-AGENT-006 — Bounded execution

Agent execution MUST be bounded by configurable limits.

Limits MAY include:

```text

tool calls
cost
tokens
file scope
network scope
retry count
resource consumption
```

---

## REQ-AGENT-007 — Agent failure handling

Agent failure MUST NOT cause silent workflow advancement.

---

## REQ-AGENT-008 — Agent output status

AIEOS MUST distinguish an agent proposal from an accepted authoritative artifact.

---

## REQ-AGENT-009 — Agent replacement

A work unit SHOULD remain valid if the underlying agent model or provider changes.

---

## REQ-AGENT-010 — Agent self-modification boundary

An agent MUST NOT be permitted to silently expand its own authority, capability set, or governing policies.

---

## REQ-AGENT-011 — Agent role/execution distinction

AIEOS MUST be capable of distinguishing a logical Agent role or profile from a concrete Agent Execution.

---

## REQ-AGENT-012 — Model identity distinction

---

# 18. Capability Requirements

## REQ-CAP-001 — Explicit capability model

AIEOS MUST represent the capabilities available to execution actors.

---

## REQ-CAP-002 — Least privilege

Actors SHOULD receive only capabilities necessary for their assigned operation.

---

## REQ-CAP-003 — Scoped capabilities

Capabilities MUST be capable of being constrained by:

* action,
* target,
* environment,
* duration,
* work unit.

---

## REQ-CAP-004 — Revocation

Capabilities MUST be revocable where the underlying system permits revocation.

---

## REQ-CAP-005 — Capability expiration

Sensitive capabilities SHOULD expire automatically.

---

## REQ-CAP-006 — Capability provenance

Capability issuance SHOULD be auditable.

---

## REQ-CAP-007 — External credential isolation

Raw long-lived external credentials SHOULD NOT need to be exposed directly to agent reasoning contexts.

---

## REQ-CAP-008 — Capability denial

A denied capability request MUST NOT be silently converted into an alternative privileged action.

---

## REQ-CAP-009 — Capability definition

AIEOS MUST be capable of representing a defined class of technical operation independently of any Actor currently possessing it.

---

## REQ-CAP-010 — Capability grant

AIEOS MUST be capable of representing a scoped grant making a Capability available to a particular Actor or Execution context.

---

# 47. Requirements North Star

A conforming AIEOS implementation should eventually make it possible to answer, from governed system state:

> **What are we trying to make true?**

> **Why are we trying to make it true?**

> **What requirements constrain the change?**

> **What plan was accepted?**

> **Who or what performed the work?**

> **What authority did they possess?**

> **What actually changed?**

> **What evidence shows the change is correct?**

> **Who or what reviewed it?**

> **Who authorized consequential actions?**

> **What was deployed?**

> **What happened afterward?**

> **What did the organization learn?**

> **What would be affected if this changed again?**

If the engineering system cannot answer these questions for consequential work, then the system does not yet provide the level of governance, traceability, and machine-operability required by AIEOS.
