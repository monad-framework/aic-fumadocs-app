# What is evidence?

**Evidence** is inspectable information used to support, weaken, verify, falsify, or otherwise evaluate a Claim about an engineering system.

Evidence may include:

```text
test results

benchmark measurements

build output

static-analysis findings

runtime observations

repository state

execution records

experiment results

review findings

security scans

migration reports

compatibility checks

human observations

native-system records
```

Evidence is not synonymous with documentation.

It is not merely a statement that something happened.

It is the supporting material that gives such a statement credibility.

> **A Claim tells us what is asserted. Evidence gives us reason to believe, reject, or qualify it.**

---

# Evidence is not a claim

Suppose an artifact states:

```text
The parser handles nested expressions correctly.
```

That is a Claim.

A test report showing all nested-expression cases passing may be Evidence supporting it.

Likewise:

```text
The migration completed successfully.
```

is a Claim.

Evidence might include:

```text
migration execution record

schema version before and after

row-count reconciliation

integrity checks

verification results
```

Keeping these concepts distinct allows Monad to ask:

```text
What is being asserted?

What Evidence supports it?

How strong is that Evidence?

Is the Evidence still relevant?

Does contradictory Evidence exist?
```

---

# Evidence is not proof

Engineering Evidence rarely establishes absolute truth.

A passing test proves only what that test actually establishes under the conditions in which it was run.

For example:

```text
10 tests passed
```

does not automatically imply:

```text
the component is correct
```

It may imply:

```text
the tested behavior satisfied
the tested expectations
under the recorded conditions
```

That distinction matters.

<Callout type="info" title="Evidence has scope">

Every piece of Evidence supports particular Claims under particular conditions. Its meaning should not be extended beyond those conditions without justification.

</Callout>

---

# Evidence is contextual

The same result can have different significance in different contexts.

For example:

```text
response time: 90 ms
```

could mean:

```text
excellent
```

for an interactive requirement of:

```text
≤ 250 ms
```

but:

```text
failure
```

for a requirement of:

```text
≤ 50 ms
```

Evidence does not interpret itself.

Its relationship to a Claim, Requirement, Specification, Policy, or Hypothesis provides meaning.

---

# Evidence is part of an engineering argument

A useful engineering chain is:

```text
Requirement
    ↓
Implementation
    ↓
Verification Method
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Verification Result
```

Another is:

```text
Hypothesis
    ↓
Experiment
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Conclusion
    ↓
Decision
```

Another:

```text
Policy
    ↓
Operation
    ↓
Audit / Execution Evidence
    ↓
Governance Determination
```

Evidence connects engineering reasoning to observed reality.

---

# Evidence and authority

Evidence and Authority answer different questions.

**Evidence asks:**

> What supports this Claim?

**Authority asks:**

> Who or what is entitled to establish, approve, modify, or govern it?

An authoritative person can still make an unsupported Claim.

Strong Evidence can also exist before anyone with decision Authority has reviewed it.

Therefore:

```text
Authority ≠ Evidence
```

Both may be required for consequential engineering decisions.

---

# Evidence and approval

Approval is a governance act.

Evidence is support for that act.

For example:

```text
security scan passed
```

may be Evidence.

```text
security reviewer approved release
```

is an Approval.

The Approval may depend on the Evidence.

But the two should remain distinguishable.

---

# Evidence and provenance

Evidence should normally carry enough [Provenance](/docs/system/concepts) to answer questions such as:

```text
Who produced it?

What produced it?

When?

Against which system revision?

Using which inputs?

Under which environment?

From which native system?

Was it transformed?

Has it been superseded?
```

Evidence without provenance may be difficult to trust or reproduce.

---

# Evidence and observation

An Observation records something encountered in reality.

Evidence is an Observation used in support of evaluating a Claim.

For example:

```text
Observation:
process exited with status 1
```

may become Evidence for:

```text
Claim:
the build failed
```

Not every Observation needs to become durable Evidence.

Only information that matters to engineering understanding, governance, verification, or future reconstruction should incur that retention cost.

---

# Evidence and inference

Evidence may support an Inference.

For example:

```text
Evidence:
three independent tests show the same regression
after commit abc123
```

Inference:

```text
commit abc123 likely introduced the regression
```

The Evidence may be strong.

But unless causal attribution is established, the Inference should remain an Inference.

Monad should not silently promote inferred conclusions into confirmed facts.

---

# Evidence and verification

[Verification](/docs/artifacts/verification) evaluates whether something satisfies an established expectation.

Evidence supports that evaluation.

Conceptually:

```text
Expectation
     ↓
Verification Method
     ↓
Observation
     ↓
Evidence
     ↓
Verification Result
```

The result might be:

```text
passed

failed

incomplete

blocked

inconclusive
```

The Evidence should remain available to explain why.

---

# Evidence and experiments

[Experiments](/docs/artifacts/experiments) generate Evidence in order to reduce uncertainty.

For example:

```text
Hypothesis:
semantic identity survives file movement
```

Experiment:

```text
move 500 representative files
and reconcile identity
```

Evidence:

```text
492 preserved automatically

8 required review

0 false merges
```

Conclusion:

```text
hypothesis moderately supported
under tested conditions
```

The Evidence is the measured result.

The conclusion is the interpretation.

---

# Evidence and reviews

A Review may produce Evidence such as:

```text
identified requirement mismatch

architecture violation

security concern

missing migration

incorrect dependency

unverified assumption
```

A reviewer statement can itself be Evidence when human judgment is the relevant evaluation method.

But human Evidence should be recorded as human judgment, not disguised as machine measurement.

---

# Evidence and native systems

Native engineering systems are important Evidence sources.

Examples include:

```text
Git

filesystem

compiler

test runner

package manager

CI system

GitHub

database

deployment platform

runtime telemetry

security scanner
```

Monad should not replace authoritative native records merely to centralize Evidence.

Where possible:

```text
preserve native identity

record native reference

capture relevant metadata

link from Monad
```

rather than duplicating entire systems unnecessarily.

---

# Evidence identity

Important Evidence should have durable identity.

Conceptually:

```text
Evidence
   │
   ├── identity
   ├── type
   ├── source
   ├── producer
   ├── subject
   ├── revision
   ├── time
   ├── result
   ├── provenance
   └── integrity information
```

The final identifier format belongs in the relevant [Schema](/docs/artifacts/schemas).

As elsewhere:

```text
Evidence identity ≠ file path
```

# Evidence types

Evidence may be categorized by what produced it.

Useful families include:

```text
test evidence

build evidence

analysis evidence

benchmark evidence

experiment evidence

execution evidence

review evidence

security evidence

migration evidence

release evidence

operational evidence

human evidence

native-system evidence
```

The taxonomy should remain small enough to be useful.

---

# Test evidence

Examples:

```text
unit test results

integration test results

end-to-end test results

property tests

contract tests

acceptance tests

regression tests
```

Useful metadata may include:

```text
test suite

revision

environment

test count

passed

failed

skipped

duration

report location
```

---

# Build evidence

Examples:

```text
compiler output

build success

artifact checksum

package contents

dependency resolution

reproducibility result
```

A successful build is Evidence that the project could be built under the recorded conditions.

It is not Evidence that the resulting software is correct.

---

# Static-analysis evidence

Examples:

```text
type checker results

lint findings

security analysis

dependency analysis

schema validation

policy validation
```

Static analysis supports particular Claims.

For example:

```text
no type errors detected
```

does not imply:

```text
no runtime defects exist
```

---

# Benchmark evidence

Examples:

```text
latency

throughput

memory usage

CPU usage

disk usage

startup time

index rebuild duration
```

Performance Evidence should preserve:

```text
workload

dataset

hardware class

software revision

tool version

number of runs

measurement method
```

where material.

---

# Experiment evidence

Experiment Evidence should be linked to:

```text
Experiment

Hypothesis

method

fixture

environment

run

result
```

This lets future engineers understand not merely the measurement, but why it was produced.

---

# Execution evidence

Governed operations may produce Evidence such as:

```text
operation identity

Actor

target

authorization

command or adapter invocation

native result

observed side effects

postconditions

timestamps

resulting revision
```

Execution Evidence is especially important when a side effect cannot be reconstructed reliably from final state alone.

---

# Verification evidence

Verification Evidence supports a specific conformance determination.

For example:

```text
Requirement:
REQ-017
```

Verification:

```text
run acceptance suite
```

Evidence:

```text
report artifact
```

Result:

```text
passed
```

Traceability should make the chain discoverable.

---

# Security evidence

Examples:

```text
threat-model review

dependency scan

SAST result

DAST result

credential scan

permission audit

penetration-test finding

security test

signed artifact verification
```

Security Evidence often has stronger retention and access requirements.

---

# Migration evidence

Migration Evidence may include:

```text
source schema version

target schema version

migration identifier

pre-migration counts

post-migration counts

integrity checks

warnings

failed records

rollback or compensation status
```

This makes later investigation possible if migrated state proves incorrect.

---

# Release evidence

A Release may collect Evidence such as:

```text
required tests

build artifacts

checksums

compatibility verification

security verification

migration verification

approval record

release manifest
```

The Evidence supports the Claim:

```text
this version satisfied the release requirements
at the time it was released
```

---

# Operational evidence

Production or real-world operation may produce Evidence about:

```text
reliability

error rate

latency

resource usage

failure modes

user-visible defects

upgrade behavior
```

Operational Evidence can contradict assumptions established before release.

That feedback should be allowed to revise the system's knowledge.

---

# Human evidence

Some questions require judgment.

Examples:

```text
architecture review

usability evaluation

code review

design critique

incident analysis

manual compatibility check
```

Human Evidence should record:

```text
reviewer

scope

method or rubric

observation

conclusion

time
```

where appropriate.

---

# Documentary evidence

Existing engineering records may themselves support Claims.

Examples:

```text
vendor documentation

protocol specification

standards document

repository history

issue discussion

native audit record
```

When external Evidence is used, preserve enough reference information to retrieve or identify the source later.

---

# Direct evidence

Direct Evidence observes the thing being claimed.

Example:

```text
Claim:
API returns 200 for valid request.

Evidence:
captured execution of valid request
returning 200.
```

Direct Evidence is generally stronger than indirect Evidence for the same Claim.

---

# Indirect evidence

Indirect Evidence supports a Claim through another fact.

Example:

```text
Claim:
deployment used artifact X.

Evidence:
release manifest references checksum Y,
and artifact X has checksum Y.
```

Indirect Evidence can be strong.

Its inferential chain should remain inspectable.

---

# Primary and derived evidence

**Primary Evidence** is captured directly from the relevant source.

For example:

```text
raw test report
```

**Derived Evidence** is calculated or summarized from other Evidence.

For example:

```text
test success rate: 97.8%
```

The derived result should preserve lineage to its inputs.

---

# Raw evidence

Raw Evidence may include:

```text
JSON report

JUnit XML

command output

trace

benchmark data

database query result

native audit event
```

Raw Evidence is valuable because it preserves what the producing system actually reported.

---

# Summarized evidence

Humans often need concise summaries.

For example:

```text
1,428 tests passed

0 failed

6 skipped
```

A summary should link to the underlying Evidence when the detail matters.

---

# Evidence bundles

A single Claim may require multiple Evidence items.

For example:

```text
Release Evidence Bundle
   ├── build result
   ├── unit tests
   ├── integration tests
   ├── security scan
   ├── migration verification
   ├── compatibility test
   └── approval record
```

A bundle can make a verification argument easier to inspect.

The individual Evidence items should remain identifiable.

---

# Evidence relationships

Useful relationships include:

```text
supports

contradicts

qualifies

derived-from

produced-by

verifies

invalidates

supersedes
```

These relationships should be explicit when they affect interpretation.

---

# Supporting evidence

Supporting Evidence increases confidence in a Claim.

Example:

```text
Claim:
read model rebuild is deterministic.
```

Evidence:

```text
20 independent rebuilds
produced identical checksums.
```

---

# Contradictory evidence

Monad must preserve Evidence that challenges established Claims.

For example:

```text
Claim:
all identity-preserving moves are reconciled automatically.
```

Later Evidence:

```text
production case required manual rebinding.
```

That contradiction should not disappear because the earlier Claim was authoritative.

Instead:

```text
Evidence challenges Claim
        ↓
Claim re-evaluated
        ↓
possible correction
```

---

# Negative evidence

Absence of an expected observation may itself be meaningful.

For example:

```text
expected:
migration writes 12,410 records

observed:
only 12,389 records exist
```

The missing records are Evidence of incomplete migration.

---

# Absence of evidence

Be careful with:

```text
we found no failures
```

versus:

```text
there are no failures
```

These are not equivalent.

> **Absence of Evidence is only informative when the observation method had a meaningful opportunity to detect the condition.**

---

# Evidence strength

Evidence strength can depend on:

```text
directness

coverage

method quality

reproducibility

independence

source reliability

freshness

representativeness

integrity

provenance
```

Monad should avoid pretending all Evidence has equal weight.

---

# Evidence quality

A useful Evidence record may answer:

```text
What was observed?

How was it observed?

By whom or what?

Against which Subject?

Against which revision?

Under what conditions?

How complete was the observation?

Can it be reproduced?

Can its integrity be checked?

What Claim does it support?
```

---

# Evidence validity

Evidence can exist but be invalid for a particular purpose.

For example:

```text
benchmark data
```

may be authentic, yet invalid for deciding production latency because:

```text
dataset was too small
```

Therefore:

```text
Evidence existence ≠ Evidence validity
```

---

# Evidence relevance

Evidence may be valid but irrelevant.

Example:

```text
unit tests passed
```

is valid Evidence for tested unit behavior.

It may be irrelevant to the Claim:

```text
deployment is secure against credential theft.
```

Evidence should be evaluated against the specific Claim.

---

# Validity versus relevance

These are separate dimensions.

```text
valid + relevant
→ useful Evidence

valid + irrelevant
→ true but not helpful

invalid + relevant
→ appears useful but cannot support conclusion

invalid + irrelevant
→ no useful value
```

This distinction is especially important during reviews.

---

# Freshness

Evidence can become stale.

For example:

```text
security scan passed
```

against:

```text
revision A
```

does not establish the security state of:

```text
revision B
```

Freshness should therefore consider:

```text
Subject revision

configuration revision

environment revision

dependency revision

policy revision

time
```

depending on the Claim.

---

# Revision binding

Where practical, Evidence should bind to the state it evaluated.

For example:

```text
Evidence
  subject_revision: abc123
```

Then when the Subject changes:

```text
current revision: def456
```

Monad can determine that the Evidence may require re-evaluation.

---

# Evidence invalidation

A change does not always invalidate every Evidence item.

For example:

```text
README typo change
```

should not necessarily invalidate:

```text
database performance benchmark
```

Invalidation should follow actual dependency and relevance relationships.

This is one place where Monad's system model may provide significant value.

---

# Dependency-aware freshness

Conceptually:

```text
Evidence
   ↓ depends on
Component A
Config B
Dependency C
```

If only unrelated Component Z changes:

```text
Evidence may remain fresh
```

If Component A changes:

```text
Evidence becomes stale
```

This is more useful than invalidating everything on every commit.

---

# Evidence lifetime

Some Evidence remains useful for years.

Examples:

```text
why an architecture choice was rejected

historical migration results

incident findings
```

Other Evidence expires quickly:

```text
current dependency scan

current test result

runtime health check
```

Retention policy should reflect purpose.

---

# Historical evidence

Evidence should usually remain historically inspectable even after it becomes stale.

A stale test result may still answer:

```text
Why did release 1.4 ship?
```

because it documents what was known at that time.

Current validity and historical relevance are different.

---

# Current versus historical evidence

Monad should distinguish:

```text
valid for current state
```

from:

```text
valid evidence about historical state
```

A test result from six months ago can stop being current without becoming false.

---

# Superseded evidence

A newer Evidence item may supersede an older one for current decisions.

For example:

```text
EV-101:
security scan against revision A

EV-245:
security scan against revision B
```

The earlier Evidence remains historically useful.

The later item becomes the relevant current Evidence.

---

# Corrected evidence

Evidence itself may need correction.

Examples:

```text
incorrect test configuration

broken benchmark harness

misreported environment

parsing error in generated summary
```

Corrections should preserve the original record and explain the change.

---

# Evidence integrity

For consequential Evidence, it may be useful to preserve:

```text
checksum

signature

immutable storage identifier

source revision

native record identifier
```

The appropriate mechanism depends on the risk.

The objective is to make silent alteration detectable or difficult.

---

# Content addressing

Some Evidence artifacts may benefit from content hashes.

For example:

```text
sha256:...
```

This can establish that the Evidence being inspected is the same material that originally supported the Claim.

Content addressing should be used where it earns its operational cost.

---

# Signed evidence

High-assurance environments may require cryptographic signatures.

Potential examples:

```text
release provenance

signed build attestations

supply-chain metadata
```

This is not necessarily required for the initial Monad kernel.

The Evidence model should not prevent it.

---

# Evidence provenance chain

Derived Evidence may form a chain:

```text
Raw Result
    ↓
Parser
    ↓
Normalized Result
    ↓
Aggregate
    ↓
Verification Conclusion
```

Each transformation can affect meaning.

Important transformations should be traceable.

---

# Evidence transformation

If a tool transforms Evidence:

```text
JUnit XML
    ↓ parser
normalized test result
```

record enough provenance to know:

```text
which parser

which version

which input

which transformation
```

when this matters to trust.

---

# Evidence normalization

Different tools report similar facts differently.

For example:

```text
pytest

Vitest

cargo test

Go test
```

all produce test Evidence.

Monad may normalize shared semantics such as:

```text
passed

failed

skipped

duration
```

while preserving the native report.

---

# Preserve native meaning

Normalization should not erase tool-specific information.

Conceptually:

```text
Native Evidence
      +
Normalized Interpretation
```

rather than:

```text
Native Evidence
      ↓
discarded
```

The native source remains important when debugging normalization errors.

---

# Evidence adapters

Integration adapters may translate native outputs into Evidence.

For example:

```text
GitHub Actions
      ↓
CI Adapter
      ↓
Verification Evidence
```

The adapter should not invent a stronger Claim than the native system supports.

---

# Evidence capture

Evidence may be captured:

```text
automatically

manually

by integration

by verification harness

by execution engine

by Experiment

by human reviewer
```

The capture method should itself have provenance where material.

---

# Automatic capture

Automation is appropriate when Evidence is:

```text
frequent

structured

repeatable

machine-generated

important to traceability
```

Examples:

```text
test reports

build checksums

schema validation

policy evaluation
```

---

# Manual capture

Manual Evidence is appropriate when the observation is inherently human.

Examples:

```text
design review

usability evaluation

manual migration inspection

incident analysis
```

Manual does not mean inferior.

It means the method depends on human judgment.

---

# Capture only what matters

Monad should not become an indiscriminate log archive.

The guiding question is:

> **Will retaining this information materially improve future verification, governance, reconstruction, debugging, learning, or accountability?**

If not, it may not deserve durable Evidence status.

---

# Evidence versus logs

Logs record activity.

Evidence supports Claims.

Some log entries may become Evidence.

Most do not need to.

For example:

```text
10 MB application log
```

might produce:

```text
Evidence:
request 1827 failed with timeout
at 14:32:11
```

Preserving only relevant slices can reduce noise.

---

# Evidence references

Large Evidence artifacts should often be referenced rather than embedded.

For example:

```text
Evidence Record
   ├── summary
   ├── checksum
   ├── source
   └── artifact URI
```

This keeps the semantic model lightweight while preserving inspectability.

---

# Durable references

Evidence references should remain resolvable for as long as the associated Claim requires historical support.

Avoid making critical engineering Evidence depend exclusively on ephemeral URLs.

---

# External evidence

Evidence may live outside the repository.

Examples:

```text
CI artifact

GitHub record

deployment log

cloud security report

package registry metadata
```

Monad can record:

```text
native identifier

origin

capture time

checksum where useful

retrieval information
```

without necessarily duplicating the entire artifact.

---

# Evidence portability

Important Evidence should remain understandable if a particular vendor disappears.

This argues for preserving essential metadata in open, inspectable formats even when the native artifact remains external.

---

# Evidence schema

A conceptual Evidence record might contain:

```text
id

type

subject

claim

source

producer

method

observed_at

valid_time

system_revision

environment

result

artifact_reference

integrity

provenance

status
```

This is conceptual.

The exact canonical structure belongs in [Schemas](/docs/artifacts/schemas).

---

# Evidence status

Possible Evidence states might include:

```text
current

stale

superseded

invalid

withdrawn
```

The final lifecycle vocabulary should be minimal and precisely defined.

---

# Current evidence

Evidence is current when it remains applicable to the relevant current Claim and system state.

---

# Stale evidence

Evidence becomes stale when its conditions no longer adequately match the state being evaluated.

Stale does not mean false.

It means:

```text
re-evaluation may be required
```

---

# Invalid evidence

Evidence is invalid if the observation or methodology can no longer support the intended conclusion.

For example:

```text
benchmark harness measured cached output accidentally
```

The Evidence should remain historically visible but should no longer support the Claim.

---

# Withdrawn evidence

Human-generated Evidence may sometimes be withdrawn.

For example:

```text
reviewer discovers their assessment
was based on incorrect information
```

The withdrawal should be recorded rather than deleting the original event.

---

# Evidence confidence

Some Evidence may benefit from a confidence assessment.

For example:

```text
high:
automated deterministic test

moderate:
representative benchmark

low:
single manual observation
```

But confidence should not become an unexplained numeric trust score.

The basis for confidence matters more than the label.

---

# No universal evidence score

Monad should resist compressing Evidence quality into a single number.

A score such as:

```text
Evidence trust = 87
```

can hide important dimensions.

Better to preserve:

```text
source

method

coverage

freshness

provenance

independence

limitations
```

and let the relevant evaluation reason about them.

---

# Independence

Independent Evidence can strengthen confidence.

For example:

```text
unit tests pass

integration tests pass

manual review finds no defect
```

These are stronger together than three copies of the same test result.

---

# Correlated evidence

Multiple Evidence items may share the same underlying weakness.

For example:

```text
three dashboards
```

all derived from:

```text
one incorrect database query
```

They are not truly independent.

Provenance helps expose this.

---

# Evidence coverage

Evidence should make clear what portion of a Claim it actually covers.

For example:

```text
Requirement:
support Linux, macOS, Windows
```

Evidence:

```text
tested Linux only
```

Result:

```text
partial coverage
```

This should not be recorded as full verification.

---

# Coverage matrix

For complex Requirements, a matrix may be useful:

| Requirement aspect | Evidence      | Result     |
| ------------------ | ------------- | ---------- |
| Linux              | test report A | Pass       |
| macOS              | test report B | Pass       |
| Windows            | —             | Unverified |

Such a matrix reveals gaps immediately.

---

# Evidence completeness

Evidence may be:

```text
complete

partial

missing

unavailable
```

for a particular verification objective.

Missing Evidence should remain visible as a gap rather than being silently interpreted as success.

---

# Evidence gaps

Useful questions include:

```text
Which Requirements have no Evidence?

Which Decisions rely only on assumptions?

Which high-risk capabilities have stale Evidence?

Which Releases lack reproducible verification?

Which Claims have contradictory Evidence?
```

These queries make the engineering record actionable.

---

# Evidence obligations

Governance may require Evidence before an operation or lifecycle transition.

For example:

```text
Before release:
- tests passed
- security verification complete
- migration verification complete
```

These are Evidence obligations.

The lifecycle transition should remain blocked until they are satisfied or explicitly waived by appropriate Authority.

---

# Evidence requirements

A Policy may specify:

```text
Operation:
publish release

Required Evidence:
build verification
acceptance tests
security scan
```

This ties Governance directly to inspectable engineering state.

---

# Evidence waiver

Sometimes required Evidence cannot be produced.

A waiver should be explicit.

For example:

```text
required evidence:
browser compatibility verification

waived because:
affected browser environment unavailable

approved by:
authorized release Actor

risk:
accepted
```

The absence of Evidence should not disappear behind the Approval.

---

# Evidence and lifecycle

Evidence often gates lifecycle transitions.

For example:

```text
implemented
    ↓
verification Evidence
    ↓
verified
    ↓
release Evidence
    ↓
released
```

Different artifact types may have different gates.

Monad should not impose one universal lifecycle.

---

# Evidence and planning

Planning should identify required Evidence before work begins.

For example:

```text
Work Package
   ├── implementation tasks
   ├── verification method
   └── required Evidence
```

This prevents teams from implementing first and later inventing how success will be demonstrated.

---

# Evidence and Definition of Done

A strong Definition of Done is evidence-aware.

Instead of:

```text
feature implemented
```

prefer:

```text
feature implemented
+
acceptance tests passed
+
required review complete
+
evidence recorded
```

Completion becomes inspectable.

---

# Evidence and requirements

A Requirement without Evidence may be implemented but unverified.

Conceptually:

```text
Requirement
   ↓ satisfied-by
Implementation
   ↓ verified-by
Verification
   ↓ supported-by
Evidence
```

This chain is central to traceability.

---

# Evidence and decisions

Decisions should link the Evidence that materially informed them.

For example:

```text
ADR-014
Choose SQLite for local read model

Evidence:
EXP-012 benchmark
EXP-015 recovery test
prototype results
```

This makes later reconsideration much easier.

---

# Evidence and specifications

A Specification may define what Evidence is required for conformance.

For example:

```text
SPEC:
identity resolver

Acceptance:
zero false merges in critical fixtures

Evidence:
identity verification report
```

---

# Evidence and policies

Policies may govern:

```text
what Evidence is required

who may produce it

how fresh it must be

which sources are acceptable

when independent review is required
```

For high-risk operations, Evidence can therefore be part of authorization.

---

# Evidence and releases

A Release should be able to answer:

```text
What was verified?

Against which revision?

Using which methods?

What Evidence existed?

Who approved release?

What risks remained?
```

Without this, release confidence becomes anecdotal.

---

# Evidence and incidents

Incidents generate important Evidence.

Examples:

```text
logs

traces

metrics

native state

configuration

deployment revision

operator actions

timeline
```

That Evidence may later challenge Requirements, architecture, Policy, or Verification assumptions.

---

# Evidence and learning

Observed production failures are often stronger Evidence than design expectations.

A healthy system permits:

```text
Evidence
   ↓
correction
   ↓
new Requirement
   ↓
new Decision
   ↓
new Specification
```

The engineering model must be able to learn from reality.

---

# Evidence preservation

Preserve Evidence when it supports:

```text
current critical state

historical release justification

important architecture Decision

security assurance

migration reconstruction

incident analysis

regulatory or contractual obligation
```

Retention should be deliberate.

---

# Evidence deletion

Evidence need not be permanent merely because it once existed.

Deletion may be appropriate for:

```text
temporary debug output

duplicate raw logs

sensitive transient material

low-value intermediate files
```

provided durable conclusions do not depend on information that can no longer be reconstructed.

---

# Retention depth

Not every Evidence chain needs infinite history.

A useful principle is:

> Preserve enough to explain significant engineering state without retaining every incidental execution detail forever.

This is another example of progressive formalization.

---

# Evidence privacy

Evidence may accidentally contain:

```text
credentials

personal information

customer data

internal URLs

tokens

source secrets
```

Evidence capture must therefore be governed by data-handling Policy.

Do not persist secrets merely because a command printed them.

---

# Evidence redaction

When sensitive material must be removed:

```text
preserve the existence of the Evidence

record that redaction occurred

retain enough context to interpret the result
```

where appropriate.

Redaction should not silently alter the meaning of a record.

---

# Evidence access

Some Evidence may require restricted access.

For example:

```text
security findings

credentials exposure analysis

customer incident artifacts
```

Access control and Evidence identity are separate concerns.

An Evidence item can remain part of the semantic graph even if its content is protected.

---

# Evidence and secrets

Secrets should generally never become durable Evidence.

Prefer recording:

```text
credential reference

credential class

authorization result
```

rather than:

```text
credential value
```

---

# Evidence generated by AI

AI-generated output can be Evidence of:

```text
what the model produced
```

It is not automatically Evidence that the output is correct.

For example:

```text
AI says architecture is valid
```

is not sufficient architecture verification.

AI may assist:

```text
analysis

classification

summarization

review
```

but consequential Claims should rely on appropriate verification methods.

---

# AI as evidence producer

When AI produces an evaluation, preserve relevant provenance:

```text
model

provider

prompt or evaluation method

context

tool access

revision
```

The evaluation should remain distinguishable from deterministic verification.

---

# AI-assisted analysis

AI may help interpret large Evidence sets.

For example:

```text
summarize 500 test failures
```

The generated summary is a derived artifact.

The underlying reports remain the primary Evidence.

---

# Evidence tampering

A governed engineering system should assume important Evidence may be accidentally or deliberately altered.

High-risk workflows may therefore require:

```text
immutable identifiers

checksums

signed attestations

native audit records

restricted write Authority
```

The required controls should match the consequence.

---

# Evidence conflicts

Suppose:

```text
CI says tests passed
```

but:

```text
local reproduction fails
```

Monad should not automatically choose one.

Instead record:

```text
conflicting Evidence
```

and investigate differences such as:

```text
environment

revision

configuration

dependency state

test ordering
```

---

# Evidence resolution

Conflict resolution may produce:

```text
one Evidence item invalidated

both remain valid under different conditions

new Experiment required

Requirement clarified

environment constraint discovered
```

The outcome should preserve the reasoning.

---

# Evidence hierarchy

Some organizations may define acceptable Evidence classes.

For example:

```text
release requirement:
must be automated CI verification
```

rather than:

```text
developer says it passed locally
```

Such hierarchy belongs in Policy and should be explicit.

---

# Native authority and evidence

A native source may be authoritative for particular facts.

Examples:

```text
Git
→ commit identity

GitHub
→ pull request state

CI
→ recorded workflow result

package registry
→ published package version
```

Monad should not invent competing truth unnecessarily.

---

# Evidence source authority

The fact that a system produced Evidence does not mean that system is authoritative for every Claim.

For example:

```text
GitHub reports deployment succeeded
```

may reflect orchestration status.

The deployment platform itself may be authoritative for actual runtime state.

Evidence modeling should preserve those distinctions.

---

# Evidence time

Evidence may need several time dimensions.

For example:

```text
observed at

recorded at

valid for

system revision time
```

These can differ.

A report uploaded today may describe a test executed yesterday against a revision from last week.

---

# Evidence validity interval

Some Evidence may have an explicit validity period.

Example:

```text
dependency vulnerability scan
valid until dependency graph changes
```

or:

```text
manual certification
valid for 90 days
```

Time-based expiry should be used only where the underlying assurance actually behaves that way.

---

# Evidence revision

Evidence records themselves may evolve through metadata correction or enrichment.

The Evidence content and its record metadata should have understandable revision semantics.

---

# Evidence lineage

A derived finding may depend on many Evidence items.

For example:

```text
Release Readiness
       ↓
Acceptance Tests
Security Scan
Migration Verification
Compatibility Verification
```

Lineage allows the system to explain why readiness changed when one input becomes stale.

---

# Evidence graphs

Evidence naturally forms relationships.

But Monad should avoid producing an unreadable graph of every process output.

Only relationships useful to reasoning, governance, verification, or reconstruction should be made explicit.

---

# Evidence queries

Useful queries include:

```text
What Evidence supports REQ-017?

Which Claims have contradictory Evidence?

Which Evidence is stale?

What Evidence justified ADR-009?

Which release checks remain missing?

What Evidence depends on this component?

Which verification results were produced against revision abc123?

What current Claims rely on invalidated Evidence?
```

These are examples of the value of explicit Evidence modeling.

---

# Evidence projections

Derived views may present:

```text
verification dashboard

release-readiness report

Requirement coverage matrix

stale Evidence report

security Evidence summary

Experiment result index
```

These are [Projections](/docs/system/concepts).

They are not the canonical Evidence themselves.

---

# Evidence indexes

An Evidence index may support filtering by:

```text
type

Subject

Claim

Requirement

revision

producer

source

status

freshness

time
```

This becomes increasingly important as Evidence volume grows.

---

# Evidence packs

For review or release, Monad may assemble a temporary Evidence pack.

For example:

```text
Release 1.0 Evidence Pack
```

containing references to:

```text
build

tests

security

migration

compatibility

review

approval
```

The pack is a convenience projection over canonical Evidence.

---

# Evidence snapshots

A lifecycle event may preserve the exact Evidence set considered at that moment.

For example:

```text
release approved
```

with:

```text
Evidence snapshot:
EV-101
EV-104
EV-119
EV-123
```

Later Evidence does not rewrite what supported the historical decision.

---

# Evidence completeness at a point in time

This enables questions such as:

```text
What Evidence did the release authority
actually have when version 1.0 was approved?
```

That is different from:

```text
What Evidence exists today about version 1.0?
```

Both are useful.

---

# Evidence and correction

Suppose a Release was approved based on Evidence later found invalid.

The historical sequence should remain:

```text
Evidence accepted
      ↓
Release approved
      ↓
Evidence defect discovered
      ↓
Evidence invalidated
      ↓
release assurance reconsidered
```

History should not be rewritten to imply the invalid Evidence never existed.

---

# Evidence and uncertainty

Evidence may reduce uncertainty without eliminating it.

A good engineering record can say:

```text
Evidence strongly supports X

Evidence weakly supports Y

Z remains unverified
```

rather than forcing every question into:

```text
true / false
```

---

# Evidence and confidence transitions

Conceptually:

```text
Hypothesis
  confidence: low
       ↓
Evidence
       ↓
confidence: moderate
       ↓
more Evidence
       ↓
confidence: high
```

Monad should preserve the Evidence and reasoning rather than merely storing the final confidence label.

---

# Evidence and falsification

Evidence can falsify existing assumptions.

For example:

```text
Architecture assumption:
filesystem events are sufficient
for reconciliation
```

Evidence:

```text
branch switch produced undetected state divergence
```

The correct response is not to hide the failure.

It is to update the architecture.

---

# Evidence is allowed to change the system

The engineering record should support:

```text
Evidence
   ↓
Question
   ↓
Decision reconsidered
   ↓
Specification changed
   ↓
Implementation changed
```

Otherwise the system records Evidence without actually learning from it.

---

# Evidence and simplicity

Evidence should also justify complexity.

For every sophisticated capability, ask:

```text
What Evidence shows this complexity
solves a problem simpler mechanisms cannot?
```

This is particularly important for Monad itself.

---

# Evidence for Monad's foundational assumptions

Important questions include:

```text
Does semantic identity materially improve engineering continuity?

Does explicit provenance improve investigation?

Does graph-based impact analysis outperform simpler indexing?

Does governed AI reduce unsafe automation?

Does structured Context improve AI work quality?

Does the artifact model reduce information fragmentation?

Does the metadata burden remain acceptable?
```

Monad's own architecture should be evidence-seeking rather than self-justifying.

---

# Minimum viable evidence

The first useful Evidence model does not require a generalized assurance platform.

A minimal form may be:

```text
Evidence ID

Evidence Type

Subject

Result

Source

Revision

Produced At

Artifact Reference
```

plus enough provenance to understand where it came from.

Additional structure should be added when real needs justify it.

---

# Evidence maturity

A possible progression is:

```text
Stage 1
links to native reports

Stage 2
structured Evidence identities

Stage 3
Claim and Requirement linkage

Stage 4
freshness and invalidation

Stage 5
Evidence bundles and lifecycle gates

Stage 6
cross-system assurance reasoning
```

Monad should earn each layer through demonstrated need.

---

# Evidence quality criteria

A high-quality Evidence system should be:

```text
inspectable

traceable

revision-aware

provenance-aware

honest about scope

capable of contradiction

capable of invalidation

native-system compatible

reasonably reproducible

proportional in retention cost
```

---

# Evidence anti-patterns

Avoid:

```text
test passed, therefore system correct

review approved, therefore Evidence unnecessary

logs retained forever without purpose

screenshots as the only machine-verifiable record

CI success detached from revision

benchmark without workload definition

historical Evidence silently overwritten

stale Evidence treated as current

AI opinion treated as verification

absence of failure treated as proof of safety

duplicate copies detached from native provenance
```

---

# Evidence should answer a question

Before retaining Evidence, ask:

> **What future engineering question will this help answer?**

Possible answers:

```text
Did the Requirement pass?

Why was this Decision made?

What changed during migration?

Which revision was tested?

Who observed this failure?

Can the result be reproduced?

Was the release authorized?

What Evidence has become stale?
```

If there is no plausible question, retention may be unnecessary.

---

# Evidence Definition of Ready

For Evidence-producing work, the expected Evidence is ready when:

```text
the Claim or Requirement is known

the observation method is defined

the Subject and revision can be identified

the Evidence source is known

success/failure semantics are understood

required retention is known
```

---

# Evidence Definition of Done

An Evidence item is adequately recorded when:

```text
the observation exists

its source is identifiable

its Subject is identifiable

relevant revision or state is known

result is preserved

provenance is sufficient

integrity is adequate for the risk

relationship to the Claim or verification is known

limitations are recorded where material
```

# Example: requirement verification

```text
Requirement:
REQ-042
Parser shall preserve comments
during round-trip transformation.

Verification:
run round-trip fixture suite.

Evidence:
EV-128

Revision:
abc123

Result:
250 fixtures passed
0 failed

Conclusion:
REQ-042 verified for tested fixture set.
```

---

# Example: performance claim

```text
Claim:
impact analysis supports interactive use.

Evidence:
EV-211

Dataset:
100,000 Subjects

Environment:
reference development laptop

Results:
median: 41 ms
p95: 77 ms
p99: 104 ms

Requirement:
p95 ≤ 100 ms

Conclusion:
requirement satisfied under tested workload.
```

---

# Example: stale evidence

```text
EV-211

tested revision:
abc123

current revision:
def456

dependency changed:
impact traversal engine

status:
stale
```

The Evidence remains historically valid.

It no longer supports the current performance Claim without re-evaluation.

---

# Example: contradictory evidence

```text
EV-301
identity preserved after rename
Result: pass

EV-337
identity incorrectly merged after copy/delete
Result: fail
```

The system should not average these into:

```text
identity mostly works
```

without understanding the distinction between scenarios.

Instead, the conflicting Evidence should refine the Claim.

---

# Example: evidence bundle

```text
Release Candidate rc-4
        │
        ├── EV-410 build verification
        ├── EV-411 unit tests
        ├── EV-412 integration tests
        ├── EV-413 security scan
        ├── EV-414 migration verification
        └── EV-415 compatibility report
```

A Release gate can evaluate the bundle without collapsing the underlying Evidence.

---

# Example: human evidence

```text
Evidence:
Architecture review

Reviewer:
authorized architecture reviewer

Subject:
SPEC-017

Observation:
persistence layer leaks infrastructure-specific
types into domain interfaces.

Conclusion:
architecture boundary violated.

Status:
open finding
```

This is legitimate Evidence even though it is not generated by a machine.

---

# Example: AI-derived evidence summary

```text
Raw Evidence:
3,200 test failure records

AI-derived summary:
82% appear related to fixture initialization

Human or deterministic follow-up:
confirmed against grouped stack traces
```

The summary assists analysis.

The raw Evidence remains authoritative for what actually occurred.

---

# Traceability

A mature trace might look like:

```text
REQ-017
   ↓ verified-by
VER-044
   ↓ supported-by
EV-203
   ↓ produced-by
CI-RUN-818
   ↓ evaluated
commit abc123
```

or:

```text
ADR-012
   ↓ informed-by
EXP-006
   ↓ produced
EV-091
EV-092
EV-093
```

The goal is not bureaucracy.

The goal is inspectable reasoning.

---

# Evidence as a first-class system concept

Many engineering systems retain artifacts but not the relationships that explain why those artifacts matter.

Monad treats Evidence as first-class because it enables the system to answer:

```text
Why do we believe this?

What observation supports it?

Is that observation still valid?

Who or what produced it?

What would invalidate it?

Does conflicting Evidence exist?

Which engineering state depends on it?
```

Those are essential questions for reliable human and AI engineering.

---

# Evidence and the semantic model

Evidence should connect naturally to the rest of Monad's semantic system.

Conceptually:

```text
Subject
   │
   ├── has Claim
   │       │
   │       └── supported-by Evidence
   │
   ├── governed-by Policy
   │
   ├── constrained-by Requirement
   │
   └── verified-by Verification
                 │
                 └── produces Evidence
```

This allows engineering reasoning to cross artifact boundaries without flattening their meanings.

---

# Evidence and reality

Monad's canonical model is still a model.

Native systems and observations provide contact with engineering reality.

Evidence is one mechanism that prevents the model from becoming self-referential.

The cycle is:

```text
Model
   ↓
Expectation
   ↓
Action
   ↓
Native Reality
   ↓
Observation
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Verification / Reconciliation
   ↓
Updated Model
```

The system remains useful only if reality is allowed to correct it.

---

# Evidence economy

Evidence has value, but also cost.

Costs include:

```text
storage

capture

normalization

retention

privacy

indexing

review

maintenance
```

Therefore Monad should follow a simple rule:

> **Retain enough Evidence to support important engineering reasoning, but do not turn every observable event into permanent governance data.**

---

# The governing principle

> **Engineering Claims should be as strong as the Evidence supporting them—and no stronger.**

Evidence gives Monad a disciplined way to distinguish:

```text
asserted

observed

verified

inferred

contradicted

stale

unknown
```

That distinction is foundational to an engineering system that humans and AI can inspect, govern, and trust.
