# Authority Requirements

Engineering systems contain many statements.

Not all statements have the same standing.

A source file may claim one thing.

A generated report may claim another.

An AI system may infer something else.

A human reviewer may approve a change.

A native platform may permit an operation.

A policy may require additional evidence before that operation is allowed.

The central question is therefore not merely:

> **What does the system say?**

It is also:

> **Who or what is entitled to establish what the system currently accepts as authoritative within this scope?**

Authority determines which actors, artifacts, systems, processes, or rules are entitled to:

* establish accepted state,
* make decisions,
* approve changes,
* authorize actions,
* resolve conflicts,
* delegate authority,
* or supersede earlier authoritative state.

<Callout type="info" title="Core distinction">
Authority is the legitimate right to establish or authorize state within a defined scope. It is not the same as capability, permission, provenance, ownership, truth, or technical access.
</Callout>

A recurring Monad invariant is:

```text
Capability ≠ Authority
```

Something may be technically capable of changing a repository without being authorized to do so.

Likewise:

```text
Permission ≠ Authority
```

A GitHub token may permit a push.

That does not establish that the push is legitimate under the engineering system's governance rules.

---

# Requirement family scope

The `REQ-AUTH-*` family governs authority associated with:

* Subjects,
* Claims,
* properties,
* relationships,
* Requirements,
* Decisions,
* Specifications,
* Policies,
* lifecycle transitions,
* releases,
* identity changes,
* execution,
* verification,
* evidence acceptance,
* native-system reconciliation,
* AI-generated proposals,
* human approvals,
* delegation,
* exceptions,
* and constitutional change.

The family defines requirements for determining:

* who holds authority,
* over what scope,
* for which actions,
* under what conditions,
* for how long,
* subject to which higher-order constraints,
* and with what evidence.

It does not independently define every policy by which authority is exercised.

Those rules belong in the policy and governance layers.

# Foundational authority requirements

## REQ-AUTH-001 — Authority MUST be explicit for consequential state

Where engineering state materially affects:

* correctness,
* architecture,
* security,
* governance,
* release,
* identity,
* evidence,
* lifecycle,
* or execution,

Monad MUST be capable of determining the authority under which that state became accepted.

Authority MAY be implicit only where an explicit governing rule intentionally defines that default.

---

## REQ-AUTH-002 — Authority MUST be scoped

Authority MUST NOT be treated as an undifferentiated global property.

An authority grant SHOULD identify applicable scope such as:

* project,
* repository,
* domain,
* component,
* artifact,
* Subject,
* property,
* Claim type,
* operation,
* lifecycle transition,
* environment,
* release,
* policy domain,
* or constitutional concern.

---

## REQ-AUTH-003 — Capability MUST NOT imply authority

The ability to perform an operation MUST NOT by itself establish authorization to perform it.

For example:

```text
actor can push to main
```

does not imply:

```text
actor is authorized to approve architecture changes
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-004 — Technical permission MUST NOT imply semantic authority

Native system permissions MAY participate in enforcement.

They MUST NOT be treated as the complete authority model unless that equivalence has been explicitly established for the relevant scope.

---

## REQ-AUTH-005 — Provenance MUST NOT imply authority

Knowing that information originated from:

* Git,
* GitHub,
* CI,
* a human,
* an AI model,
* an external database,
* or Monad itself

MUST NOT automatically make that information authoritative.

---

## REQ-AUTH-006 — Ownership MUST NOT automatically imply authority

An artifact owner MAY have authority over some changes.

Ownership alone MUST NOT establish unlimited authority unless the governing model explicitly grants it.

---

## REQ-AUTH-007 — Authority MUST NOT imply truth

An authoritative Claim may later prove incorrect.

Authority determines which Claim governs accepted system state.

It does not guarantee objective correctness.

---

# Authority hierarchy

## REQ-AUTH-008 — Higher-order authority constraints MUST be representable

Monad MUST support governance structures in which one authority layer constrains another.

The conceptual hierarchy is:

```text
Constitution
    ↓ constrains
Authority
    ↓ governs
Policies and Decisions
    ↓ authorize or constrain
Operations and State Changes
```

The exact representation belongs to the architecture and specifications.

---

## REQ-AUTH-009 — Lower-scope authority MUST NOT override higher-order constraints without explicit permission

A project-level authority MUST NOT silently override a constitutional rule.

A component-level authority MUST NOT silently override a project-level prohibition.

Overrides MUST be explicitly defined by the governing authority structure.

---

## REQ-AUTH-010 — Authority precedence MUST be deterministic

Where multiple authorities apply to the same question, Monad MUST have a deterministic means of establishing:

* precedence,
* composition,
* conflict,
* or unresolved status.

The system MUST NOT silently choose whichever authority happens to be evaluated first.

---

# Authority scope

## REQ-AUTH-011 — Authority scope SHOULD be no broader than necessary

Authority SHOULD follow least-authority principles.

A grant permitting:

```text
approve release evidence for service A
```

SHOULD NOT automatically grant:

```text
modify all project policies
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-012 — Authority MAY be scoped to properties or Claims

Monad SHOULD support cases in which different sources govern different aspects of the same Subject.

Illustratively:

```text
Component A
  name         ← repository manifest authority
  owner        ← governance authority
  deployment   ← deployment platform authority
  health       ← derived observation
```

Whole-object authority MUST NOT be assumed where finer scope is necessary.

---

## REQ-AUTH-013 — Authority MAY be action-specific

Authority to:

```text
propose
```

does not necessarily imply authority to:

```text
approve
```

Likewise:

```text
approve
```

does not necessarily imply authority to:

```text
execute
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-014 — Authority MAY be lifecycle-state-specific

An actor MAY have authority while an artifact is:

```text
draft
```

but not after it becomes:

```text
approved
```

or:

```text
released
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-015 — Authority MAY be environment-specific

Authority in:

```text
development
```

MUST NOT automatically imply authority in:

```text
production
```

---

# Authority subjects

## REQ-AUTH-016 — Monad MUST be able to represent authority held by different actor classes

Possible authority holders include:

* individual humans,
* teams,
* roles,
* organizations,
* services,
* automated systems,
* native platforms,
* policy engines,
* delegated agents,
* and AI systems operating under bounded authority.

---

## REQ-AUTH-017 — Actor identity MUST be durable enough for authority evaluation

Authority MUST NOT depend solely on unstable display names.

Where possible, grants SHOULD reference stable actor or role identities.

---

## REQ-AUTH-018 — Role authority and individual authority MUST remain distinguishable

An individual may act because:

```text
Alice has release-manager role
```

rather than because:

```text
Alice personally holds permanent release authority
```

Monad SHOULD preserve that distinction.

---

# Authority grants

## REQ-AUTH-019 — Authority grants MUST identify what is granted

A consequential authority grant SHOULD define:

* grantee,
* scope,
* permitted authoritative actions,
* constraints,
* source of the grant,
* effective time,
* expiration if any,
* and applicable policy or constitutional bounds.

---

## REQ-AUTH-020 — Authority grants MUST have provenance

Monad MUST be capable of answering:

> Who or what granted this authority?

> Under which rule?

> When?

> For what scope?

---

## REQ-AUTH-021 — A grant MUST originate from sufficient authority

An actor MUST NOT create authority greater than the authority it legitimately possesses unless a higher-order rule explicitly permits that delegation.

---

## REQ-AUTH-022 — Invalid grants MUST NOT become valid merely because they were persisted

A structurally valid authority record is not necessarily an authorized authority record.

> **A valid rule is not necessarily an authorized rule.**

---

# Delegation

## REQ-AUTH-023 — Delegated authority MUST remain bounded

Delegation SHOULD support restrictions such as:

* scope,
* action,
* target,
* duration,
* environment,
* resource,
* risk level,
* approval requirement,
* or maximum delegation depth.

---

## REQ-AUTH-024 — Delegation MUST NOT silently expand authority

A delegate MUST NOT gain powers that the delegating authority did not possess or could not legitimately delegate.

---

## REQ-AUTH-025 — Delegation chains SHOULD be inspectable

Monad SHOULD be capable of explaining:

```text
Constitutional authority
       ↓
Project authority
       ↓
Release manager role
       ↓
Temporary delegate
```

for consequential actions.

---

## REQ-AUTH-026 — Delegated authority SHOULD support expiration

Temporary authority SHOULD be capable of expiring automatically.

---

## REQ-AUTH-027 — Delegated authority SHOULD support revocation

A grant SHOULD be revocable where the governing rules permit revocation.

---

## REQ-AUTH-028 — Revocation MUST affect future authority evaluation

Once a grant is revoked, future authorization MUST NOT continue to rely on that grant.

Historical actions SHOULD still retain the fact that the grant existed when they were performed.

---

# Time

## REQ-AUTH-029 — Authority MUST support temporal validity

Monad SHOULD be capable of determining:

* when an authority grant became valid,
* when it ceased to be valid,
* and whether it was valid at the time of a historical action.

---

## REQ-AUTH-030 — Current authority MUST remain distinguishable from historical authority

An actor who once had authority may no longer have it.

Historical records SHOULD preserve:

```text
authorized at time T
```

without implying:

```text
authorized now
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-031 — Expired authority MUST NOT authorize new actions

An expired grant MUST NOT be accepted for a new governed operation.

---

# Revision-aware authority

## REQ-AUTH-032 — Authorization SHOULD bind to relevant state revisions

Where authorization depends on a proposed change, approval SHOULD identify the specific relevant revision.

For example:

```text
approve release plan revision 17
```

rather than:

```text
approve release plan forever
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-033 — Material revision after approval SHOULD invalidate or re-evaluate authorization

If the approved artifact or plan changes materially, prior approval SHOULD NOT automatically remain valid.

The rules determining materiality belong in policy or specification.

---

## REQ-AUTH-034 — Historical authorization MUST retain the governing revision

Monad SHOULD preserve which:

* policy version,
* authority grant,
* artifact revision,
* and relevant system state

were used to authorize a consequential action.

---

# Authority over Claims

## REQ-AUTH-035 — Monad MUST support authoritative Claims

Where multiple Claims concern the same proposition, Monad SHOULD be able to determine which Claim, if any, is currently authoritative within a scope.

---

## REQ-AUTH-036 — Conflicting Claims MUST NOT be collapsed before authority is evaluated

If two sources assert:

```text
Component A owner = Team X
```

and:

```text
Component A owner = Team Y
```

Monad MUST preserve the conflict long enough to reason about:

* provenance,
* authority,
* time,
* and possible reconciliation.

---

## REQ-AUTH-037 — Multiple Claims MAY each be authoritative in different scopes

For example:

```text
Team X owns runtime implementation
Team Y owns operational deployment
```

Both may be authoritative if their scopes differ.

---

## REQ-AUTH-038 — Authority conflicts MUST be explicit

If two equally applicable authorities produce incompatible accepted Claims, the system MUST expose the conflict rather than silently selecting one.

---

# Property-level authority

## REQ-AUTH-039 — Property-level authority SHOULD be supported where needed

Different properties of one Subject MAY legitimately be governed by different systems.

Example:

```text
Repository
  default branch   ← GitHub
  semantic owner   ← Monad governance
  current commit   ← Git
  release status   ← release system
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-040 — Property authority MUST NOT implicitly extend to unrelated properties

An authoritative source for:

```text
deployment URL
```

MUST NOT automatically become authoritative for:

```text
architecture owner
```

---

# Authority over identity

## REQ-AUTH-041 — Identity-changing operations MUST require appropriate authority

Operations such as:

* merging semantic identities,
* splitting identities,
* rebinding native references,
* declaring equivalence,
* superseding identity,
* or replacing canonical bindings

SHOULD be treated as consequential governed operations.

---

## REQ-AUTH-042 — Inferred identity MUST NOT automatically become authoritative identity

AI or automated systems MAY propose identity bindings.

Such inference MUST remain distinguishable from confirmed authoritative identity unless explicitly permitted by policy.

---

## REQ-AUTH-043 — Identity correction MUST retain historical authority context

If an earlier identity binding is corrected, Monad SHOULD preserve:

* the previous binding,
* the authority under which it was accepted,
* the correcting authority,
* rationale,
* and evidence.

---

# Authority over lifecycle transitions

## REQ-AUTH-044 — Lifecycle transitions MAY require distinct authority

Authority to create an artifact does not necessarily imply authority to:

* approve it,
* activate it,
* release it,
* deprecate it,
* supersede it,
* or delete it.

---

## REQ-AUTH-045 — Invalid lifecycle transitions MUST NOT become valid through technical execution alone

If an actor technically changes:

```text
draft → released
```

without required authority, Monad SHOULD be capable of recognizing the transition as unauthorized even if the underlying file or platform accepted it.

---

# Authority over Requirements

## REQ-AUTH-046 — Requirement acceptance SHOULD identify accepting authority

A proposed requirement SHOULD NOT become accepted merely because it exists in the repository.

Where acceptance matters, Monad SHOULD preserve:

* who accepted it,
* under which authority,
* at what revision,
* and when.

---

## REQ-AUTH-047 — Requirement modification after acceptance SHOULD respect requirement authority

Accepted Requirements MAY require stronger authority to modify than draft Requirements.

---

# Authority over Decisions

## REQ-AUTH-048 — Architecture Decisions SHOULD identify the authority responsible for acceptance

An ADR SHOULD distinguish:

```text
authored by
```

from:

```text
accepted by
```

where those roles differ.

---

## REQ-AUTH-049 — Decision authorship MUST NOT imply decision authority

The person or AI system that drafts an ADR does not automatically possess authority to accept the decision.

---

## REQ-AUTH-050 — Superseding a Decision SHOULD require appropriate authority

A Decision SHOULD NOT be considered superseded merely because another document disagrees with it.

---

# Authority over Specifications

## REQ-AUTH-051 — Specification approval SHOULD be distinguishable from specification authorship

A specification MAY be drafted collaboratively or by AI.

Its normative standing SHOULD arise from an explicit acceptance process where governance requires one.

---

## REQ-AUTH-052 — Implementation MUST NOT silently redefine authoritative specification

If implementation diverges from an approved Specification, Monad SHOULD represent the divergence rather than silently treating implementation behavior as the new normative contract.

---

# Authority over Policies

## REQ-AUTH-053 — Policy creation and activation MUST require legitimate policy authority

A policy MUST NOT become authoritative simply because it is structurally valid or loaded by an engine.

---

## REQ-AUTH-054 — Policy authority SHOULD be distinguishable from policy evaluation capability

A policy engine may technically evaluate any policy text.

That capability does not establish that the policy itself has legitimate authority.

---

## REQ-AUTH-055 — Policy changes MUST NOT silently authorize their own activation

A policy that broadens its own creator's authority SHOULD require independent authorization where self-escalation would otherwise occur.

---

# Authority over execution

## REQ-AUTH-056 — Consequential execution MUST be authorized before execution where governance requires it

The execution pipeline SHOULD distinguish:

```text
can execute
```

from:

```text
is authorized to execute
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-057 — Authorization MUST apply to the concrete resolved operation where necessary

A broad request such as:

```text
deploy service
```

may resolve into multiple concrete actions.

Authorization SHOULD apply to the resolved operation when material differences affect risk or scope.

---

## REQ-AUTH-058 — Authorization SHOULD be revalidated near the execution boundary

Where state can change between approval and execution, Monad SHOULD re-evaluate relevant:

* authority,
* policy,
* target state,
* artifact revision,
* environment,
* and preconditions

immediately before consequential execution.

This reduces time-of-check/time-of-use risk.

---

## REQ-AUTH-059 — Execution success MUST NOT retroactively validate unauthorized action

An operation that succeeds technically without legitimate authority remains unauthorized.

---

# Authority over verification

## REQ-AUTH-060 — Verification execution and verification acceptance MAY require different authority

An automated test system may be authorized to execute tests.

A release authority may separately determine whether the resulting evidence is sufficient for release.

---

## REQ-AUTH-061 — Evidence producer MUST NOT automatically control evidence acceptance

The system producing evidence SHOULD NOT automatically become the sole authority deciding whether that evidence satisfies a requirement.

---

# Authority over releases

## REQ-AUTH-062 — Release authorization MUST be explicit for governed releases

Where releases are governed, Monad SHOULD preserve:

* approving authority,
* approved revision,
* required evidence,
* policy state,
* and authorization time.

---

## REQ-AUTH-063 — Release authority SHOULD be separable from deployment capability

An actor or tool capable of deploying production software MUST NOT automatically be treated as authorized to approve the release.

---

# Native-system authority

## REQ-AUTH-064 — Native systems MUST remain authoritative for capabilities they legitimately own

Where a native system is the intended source of truth for a native property, Monad SHOULD respect that authority.

Examples may include:

* Git for commit identity,
* package registries for published package versions,
* deployment systems for observed deployment identifiers.

---

## REQ-AUTH-065 — Native authority MUST be scoped to native concerns

Git being authoritative for commit identity does not make Git authoritative for:

* business requirements,
* architecture rationale,
* semantic ownership,
* or release approval.

---

## REQ-AUTH-066 — Monad SHOULD model native authority rather than pretending to replace it

Where external systems legitimately govern facts, Monad SHOULD integrate, bind, observe, and reconcile with them.

It SHOULD NOT manufacture duplicate authority merely to centralize control.

---

## REQ-AUTH-067 — Native permission MUST NOT be treated as Monad governance authority

A cloud provider may accept an API call.

GitHub may accept a merge.

The filesystem may permit a write.

Those outcomes MUST NOT be treated as proof that Monad governance authorized the action.

---

# Filesystem authority

## REQ-AUTH-068 — Filesystem authority MUST be explicit by artifact class

Monad MUST NOT assume either:

```text
filesystem is always authoritative
```

or:

```text
filesystem is never authoritative
```

Authority SHOULD be defined by artifact or semantic scope.

---

## REQ-AUTH-069 — Ordinary authored source MAY remain directly editable

Monad's authority model SHOULD permit normal engineering workflows where direct source modification remains legitimate.

Governance SHOULD be proportional to consequence.

---

## REQ-AUTH-070 — Generated Representations MUST NOT become authoritative merely because they exist on disk

If a file is generated from authoritative semantic state, the generated file SHOULD remain a Representation unless explicitly granted separate authority.

---

# AI authority

## REQ-AUTH-071 — AI capability MUST NOT imply AI authority

An AI system MAY be capable of:

* editing code,
* modifying architecture documents,
* creating policies,
* changing schemas,
* triggering deployment,
* or approving work.

That technical ability MUST NOT itself grant authority.

---

## REQ-AUTH-072 — AI authority MUST be explicitly bounded

Where an AI actor receives authority, its scope SHOULD be defined in terms such as:

* permitted actions,
* target scope,
* lifecycle states,
* tools,
* environments,
* resource bounds,
* time bounds,
* review requirements,
* and escalation conditions.

---

## REQ-AUTH-073 — AI-generated proposals SHOULD default to proposal status unless explicitly authorized otherwise

AI MAY generate:

* Requirements,
* Decisions,
* Claims,
* relationships,
* specifications,
* code changes,
* policies,
* or identity bindings.

Those outputs SHOULD remain proposed or inferred unless governing rules explicitly grant the AI authority to establish them.

---

## REQ-AUTH-074 — AI MUST NOT silently expand its own authority

An AI actor MUST NOT create, modify, reinterpret, or exploit governance state in order to gain broader authority unless that operation itself is explicitly authorized.

---

## REQ-AUTH-075 — AI authority SHOULD be revocable independently of AI capability

The system SHOULD be able to remove authority from an AI actor without necessarily disabling the underlying technical capability or model integration.

---

## REQ-AUTH-076 — Model identity SHOULD NOT be treated as sufficient authority identity

Authority SHOULD attach to the governed actor, agent, or execution context rather than simply to a provider model name.

For example:

```text
GPT-X
```

is not, by itself, an authority principal.

---

# Human and AI governance

## REQ-AUTH-077 — Humans and AI SHOULD share the same conceptual authority model

Monad SHOULD NOT require fundamentally separate metaphysical notions of authority for humans and AI.

Both SHOULD be modeled through concepts such as:

* Actor,
* Capability,
* Authority,
* Scope,
* Policy,
* Approval,
* Obligation,
* and Evidence.

Different default restrictions MAY apply.

---

## REQ-AUTH-078 — Human involvement MUST NOT automatically make an action authoritative

A human clicking a button does not establish that the human had sufficient authority.

Authority evaluation still applies.

---

# Approvals

## REQ-AUTH-079 — Approval MUST identify the approving authority

A consequential approval SHOULD record:

* approver,
* authority basis,
* target,
* relevant revision,
* scope,
* time,
* and conditions.

---

## REQ-AUTH-080 — Approval MUST NOT be broader than its target

Approval of:

```text
release candidate 12
```

SHOULD NOT silently authorize:

```text
all future releases
```

---

## REQ-AUTH-081 — Approval MAY expire

Approval SHOULD support expiration when delayed execution could invalidate its assumptions.

---

## REQ-AUTH-082 — Approval SHOULD become stale when material context changes

Relevant context may include:

* target revision,
* policy revision,
* environment,
* dependencies,
* evidence,
* execution plan,
* or security posture.

---

## REQ-AUTH-083 — Self-approval restrictions SHOULD be expressible

Monad SHOULD support policies requiring separation of duties where appropriate.

For example:

```text
author ≠ approver
```

for high-risk operations.

This SHOULD NOT be mandatory for all workflows.

---

# Conflicts

## REQ-AUTH-084 — Conflicting authority claims MUST be detectable

Monad MUST be capable of representing situations in which multiple actors or systems claim authority over the same scope.

---

## REQ-AUTH-085 — Authority conflict resolution MUST be explicit

Resolution MAY depend on:

* constitutional hierarchy,
* specificity,
* scope,
* time,
* policy,
* delegation chain,
* or explicit adjudication.

The system MUST NOT hide unresolved conflict.

---

## REQ-AUTH-086 — Unresolved authority conflict SHOULD fail safely for consequential operations

Where an operation requires authoritative resolution and no valid resolution exists, Monad SHOULD refuse or defer the operation rather than invent authority.

The exact fail behavior SHOULD be policy-defined.

---

# Exceptions

## REQ-AUTH-087 — Exceptions MUST be authorized

An exception to normal governance is still a governed action.

> **An exception is part of governance, not the absence of it.**

---

## REQ-AUTH-088 — Exception authority SHOULD be narrowly scoped

An exception SHOULD identify:

* what rule is being bypassed,
* why,
* by whose authority,
* for what target,
* for how long,
* and what compensating obligations apply.

---

## REQ-AUTH-089 — Exceptions SHOULD NOT permanently mutate ordinary authority

A temporary exception SHOULD NOT silently become a standing authority grant.

---

# Emergency authority

## REQ-AUTH-090 — Break-glass authority MUST be explicitly modeled where supported

Emergency authority SHOULD NOT be implemented as an undocumented bypass.

---

## REQ-AUTH-091 — Emergency authority SHOULD be stronger in evidence and review obligations

Break-glass actions MAY reduce pre-execution constraints.

They SHOULD typically increase requirements for:

* attribution,
* evidence,
* audit,
* notification,
* retrospective review,
* and expiration.

---

## REQ-AUTH-092 — Emergency authority MUST be bounded

Emergency grants SHOULD be constrained by:

* scope,
* duration,
* action,
* and triggering conditions.

---

# Authority and obligations

## REQ-AUTH-093 — Authorization MAY create obligations

An operation MAY be permitted only if additional obligations are attached.

Examples:

* run verification,
* collect evidence,
* notify an owner,
* reconcile state,
* create an audit record,
* or perform post-execution review.

---

## REQ-AUTH-094 — Satisfied authorization MUST NOT imply satisfied obligations

An action may have been legitimately authorized and executed while follow-up governance obligations remain incomplete.

---

# Authority evaluation

## REQ-AUTH-095 — Authority evaluation SHOULD be explainable

For consequential decisions, Monad SHOULD be able to explain:

* which authority grants applied,
* which actor was evaluated,
* which scope matched,
* which higher-order constraints applied,
* and why authority was or was not sufficient.

---

## REQ-AUTH-096 — Authority evaluation SHOULD be deterministic for the same relevant state

Given the same:

* authority records,
* policy revisions,
* actor,
* operation,
* scope,
* and relevant system state,

the result SHOULD be deterministic unless the governing specification explicitly permits otherwise.

---

## REQ-AUTH-097 — Authority evaluation MUST distinguish unknown from unauthorized

These are different states:

```text
authority = no
```

and:

```text
authority = cannot determine
```

Monad MUST NOT collapse uncertainty into a false factual claim.

A policy MAY still require safe failure when authority is unknown.

---

# Authorization results

## REQ-AUTH-098 — Authorization results SHOULD be structured

A consequential authorization evaluation SHOULD be capable of returning information such as:

```text
authorized:
  true | false | unresolved

authority:
  ...

scope:
  ...

basis:
  ...

conditions:
  ...

obligations:
  ...

approvals:
  ...

evaluated_revision:
  ...
```

The exact schema belongs to specification work.

---

## REQ-AUTH-099 — Authorization decision MUST remain distinct from execution result

```text
authorized
```

does not mean:

```text
executed
```

and:

```text
executed successfully
```

does not mean:

```text
authorized
```

---

# Authority freshness

## REQ-AUTH-100 — Cached authority decisions MUST account for staleness

Authority may change through:

* revocation,
* expiration,
* policy change,
* role change,
* scope change,
* or constitutional amendment.

Cached decisions MUST NOT be reused beyond their valid assumptions.

---

## REQ-AUTH-101 — Long-running workflows SHOULD re-evaluate authority at material boundaries

A workflow lasting hours or days SHOULD NOT assume that authority valid at initiation remains valid indefinitely.

---

# Authority and reconciliation

## REQ-AUTH-102 — Reconciliation MUST respect authority boundaries

When native and semantic state differ, Monad MUST determine which source has authority over the disputed scope before automatically overwriting state.

---

## REQ-AUTH-103 — Reconciliation SHOULD NOT use "last write wins" as implicit authority

Temporal recency alone MUST NOT determine semantic authority unless the governing specification explicitly defines that rule.

---

## REQ-AUTH-104 — Conflicting authoritative sources MUST produce explicit reconciliation conflict

Where both sources appear legitimately authoritative, Monad SHOULD preserve the conflict for resolution rather than guessing.

---

# Authority and generated artifacts

## REQ-AUTH-105 — Generated output MUST inherit authority only according to explicit rules

A projection produced from authoritative source state MAY faithfully represent that state.

The generated artifact MUST NOT automatically acquire independent authority.

---

## REQ-AUTH-106 — Generated artifacts SHOULD expose authority lineage where decisions depend on them

If a generated dashboard or report is used for consequential decisions, it SHOULD be possible to trace the report back to the authoritative source state it represents.

---

# Authority and derived state

## REQ-AUTH-107 — Derived state MUST distinguish computation from authoritative acceptance

Monad may derive:

```text
Component A is high risk
```

from several observations.

The derived value SHOULD remain distinguishable from an authoritative risk classification established by an authorized actor.

---

## REQ-AUTH-108 — Automated derivation MAY be authoritative where explicitly governed

Not all authority requires human intervention.

A deterministic engine MAY be authorized to establish state when:

* its scope is explicit,
* the governing rule permits it,
* required inputs are authoritative,
* and verification obligations are satisfied.

---

# Authority and local-first operation

## REQ-AUTH-109 — Core authority state SHOULD be locally inspectable

A local user SHOULD be able to understand the authority governing retained project state without requiring continuous access to a hosted control plane.

---

## REQ-AUTH-110 — Offline operation MUST NOT silently manufacture authority

If required remote authority information is unavailable, Monad MUST NOT simply assume authorization.

The appropriate behavior MAY be:

* deny,
* defer,
* use a locally valid cached grant,
* or allow under an explicit offline policy.

---

## REQ-AUTH-111 — Offline-valid authority SHOULD have explicit validity bounds

Cached or portable authority records SHOULD define enough context to determine when they remain legitimate offline.

---

# Authority integrity

## REQ-AUTH-112 — Authority records SHOULD be protected against unauthorized modification

Because authority controls governance, authority records SHOULD receive protection proportional to their consequence.

Possible mechanisms include:

* repository review,
* signatures,
* access controls,
* attestations,
* immutable history,
* or stronger approval requirements.

The specific mechanism is an architectural decision.

---

## REQ-AUTH-113 — Authority changes SHOULD have stronger governance than ordinary low-risk content edits

Broad authority grants, constitutional changes, or production authority SHOULD require proportionally stronger review than routine documentation edits.

---

# Authority portability

## REQ-AUTH-114 — Authority semantics SHOULD remain portable across native tools

Monad SHOULD avoid defining authority solely in terms of one vendor's permission model.

For example:

```text
release approver
```

is a semantic authority concept.

Its enforcement MAY involve:

* GitHub,
* GitLab,
* CI,
* deployment platforms,
* local tooling,
* or another system.

---

## REQ-AUTH-115 — Native bindings SHOULD preserve semantic authority identity

Changing the native account or platform through which an authority operates SHOULD NOT necessarily require redefining the semantic authority itself.

---

# Authority observability

## REQ-AUTH-116 — Consequential authority changes SHOULD be observable

Changes such as:

* grant,
* delegation,
* revocation,
* expiration,
* suspension,
* scope modification,
* or role reassignment

SHOULD produce inspectable state transitions.

---

## REQ-AUTH-117 — Authority use SHOULD be attributable

For high-consequence operations, Monad SHOULD preserve which authority grant or chain was exercised.

---

# Authority lifecycle

A conceptual authority lifecycle may include:

```text
proposed
   ↓
approved
   ↓
active
   ↓
suspended
   ↓
revoked
```

or:

```text
active
   ↓
expired
```

This is illustrative.

Exact lifecycle semantics belong in the lifecycle and authority specifications.

---

# Authority model

A useful conceptual authority grant may contain:

```text
Authority Grant
├─ identity
├─ grantee
├─ authority source
├─ scope
├─ permitted actions
├─ constraints
├─ effective time
├─ expiration
├─ delegation rules
├─ policy relationships
├─ provenance
└─ status
```

This model is intentionally more precise than:

```text
user = admin
```

because "admin" usually collapses several unrelated concerns.

---

# Authority composition

Authority may need to combine multiple dimensions.

For example:

```text
Actor
  has Role
      ↓
Role has Authority
      ↓
Authority applies to Scope
      ↓
Policy allows Operation
      ↓
Required Approval exists
      ↓
Operation becomes authorized
```

No single edge necessarily proves authorization.

---

# Illustrative authorization case

Suppose an AI agent proposes changing a production deployment policy.

The agent may possess:

```text
capability:
  edit policy file
```

but only:

```text
authority:
  propose policy change
```

The flow might be:

```text
AI proposes revision
       ↓
policy owner reviews
       ↓
authorized approver accepts revision
       ↓
new policy revision becomes active
       ↓
execution engine uses active revision
```

If the AI directly writes the file without approval, the filesystem may contain the change.

But the change has not necessarily become authoritative.

That distinction is fundamental to Monad.

---

# Illustrative scoped authority

Consider:

```text
Subject:
  Service A
```

Different authoritative domains may exist:

| Property or action          | Potential authority    |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Source revision             | Git                    |
| Repository settings         | Git hosting platform   |
| Semantic ownership          | Project governance     |
| Deployment identity         | Deployment platform    |
| Production release approval | Release authority      |
| Architecture direction      | Architecture authority |
| Runtime health observation  | Monitoring system      |
| Risk classification         | Governance process     |

This demonstrates why:

```text
authority = source-of-truth-for-everything
```

is usually too coarse.

# Authority resolution questions

A practical authority resolver should eventually be able to answer questions such as:

* Who may modify this property?
* Who may accept this Requirement?
* Who may approve this release?
* Which system governs this native fact?
* Which authority applies to this environment?
* Is this grant still valid?
* Was this actor authorized at the time of the historical action?
* Which policy constrains this authority?
* Is delegation permitted?
* Is this approval stale?
* Are two authorities in conflict?
* Which authority supersedes the other?
* Is authority unknown or actually absent?
* Can this AI actor perform this action autonomously?
* Does native permission align with semantic authority?
* What authority chain justified this operation?

---

# Authority review checklist

For any consequential authority rule, ask:

1. Who or what holds the authority?
2. Who granted it?
3. Did the grantor have authority to grant it?
4. What exact scope does it cover?
5. What actions does it permit?
6. What actions does it not permit?
7. Does it apply to properties, artifacts, or entire Subjects?
8. Does it apply only in certain lifecycle states?
9. Does it apply only in certain environments?
10. When does it become valid?
11. When does it expire?
12. Can it be revoked?
13. Can it be delegated?
14. Can a delegate delegate again?
15. Which higher-order rule constrains it?
16. Which policies apply?
17. Does approval bind to a specific revision?
18. What happens if the revision changes?
19. What happens if authority changes before execution?
20. Can conflicting authorities exist?
21. How are conflicts exposed?
22. What happens when authority cannot be determined?
23. Is the native platform enforcing the same thing as Monad authority?
24. Is technical capability being mistaken for authority?
25. Could an actor escalate its own authority?
26. Can an AI actor rewrite the rule governing itself?
27. Is historical authority preserved?
28. Is authority use attributable?
29. Can authority be understood offline?
30. Is the complexity proportional to the risk?

# Failure modes

## Global admin authority

```text
role: admin
scope: everything
```

creates excessive authority and weakens explainability.

---

## Capability-as-authority

```text
tool can deploy
therefore tool may deploy
```

is invalid reasoning.

---

## Permission-as-authority

```text
GitHub allowed the merge
therefore the merge was authorized
```

is also invalid.

---

## Provenance-as-authority

```text
GitHub said it
therefore it governs
```

collapses origin and authority.

---

## Ownership-as-authority

```text
team owns component
therefore team may change global security policy
```

overextends scope.

---

## AI self-authorization

An AI system MUST NOT broaden its own authority by modifying the policies that constrain it unless that meta-operation is itself explicitly governed.

---

## Stale approval

Approval of revision 4 must not silently authorize materially different revision 9.

---

## Last-write-wins governance

The most recent value is not necessarily the authoritative value.

---

## Hidden delegation

Authority that can be delegated without explicit lineage becomes difficult to reason about.

---

## Permanent emergency authority

A break-glass mechanism that never expires becomes ordinary unchecked authority.

---

## Authority without provenance

If Monad cannot explain where authority came from, governance becomes opaque.

> **Enforcement without provenance creates opaque power.**

---

## Native-control duplication

Monad should not rebuild GitHub permissions, operating-system permissions, or cloud IAM merely to own another permission engine.

It should model their relationship to semantic authority where useful.

---

## Governance theater

Requiring approvals and authority records for every trivial source edit can increase ceremony without improving control.

Governance must remain proportional.

---

# Progressive formalization

Authority MAY mature incrementally.

## Stage 1 — Explicit owners

Record basic responsibility and authority for major domains.

---

## Stage 2 — Scoped grants

Define authority over specific:

* artifacts,
* domains,
* properties,
* and actions.

---

## Stage 3 — Revision-aware approvals

Tie consequential approvals to exact proposed states.

---

## Stage 4 — Delegation and revocation

Model bounded delegation, expiration, and revocation.

---

## Stage 5 — Policy integration

Evaluate authority together with policy, obligations, and context.

---

## Stage 6 — Native-system reconciliation

Relate Monad authority to:

* Git permissions,
* GitHub settings,
* CI,
* deployment systems,
* and external identity providers.

---

## Stage 7 — Governed autonomous actors

Allow human and AI actors to exercise narrowly scoped authority under explicit policy, evidence, and audit constraints.

---

# Minimal useful authority model

Monad does not need a massive enterprise entitlement platform to begin.

A useful first authority model may only need:

```text
Actor
Authority Grant
Scope
Action
Validity
Provenance
Approval
```

combined with deterministic evaluation.

More complexity should be introduced when concrete engineering problems require it.

---

# Falsifiability

The authority model must justify its complexity.

If native protections such as:

* repository permissions,
* branch protection,
* code review,
* CI gates,
* and deployment controls

are sufficient for a project, Monad SHOULD use them rather than recreating equivalent mechanisms.

Monad's authority model becomes valuable where engineering governance requires semantics that cross native systems, such as:

* property-level authority,
* cross-tool scope,
* semantic identity,
* delegated AI authority,
* revision-aware approval,
* explainable authorization,
* current versus historical authority,
* or reconciliation of competing authoritative sources.

If those needs do not exist, the simpler system may be preferable.

A useful conceptual stack is:

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

The important invariant is that these concepts remain distinct.

# Validation themes

The authority family should eventually be exercised through scenarios such as:

## Capability without authority

Given:

```text
AI agent:
  capability: repository.write
  authority: documentation.propose
```

Attempt:

```text
modify production deployment policy
```

Expected:

```text
technically possible
governance authorization denied
```

---

## Scoped authority

Given:

```text
Team A:
  authority:
    component: A
```

Attempt:

```text
modify Component B ownership
```

Expected:

```text
authority insufficient
```

---

## Expired delegation

Given:

```text
temporary release authority:
  expires: T
```

Attempt execution after `T`.

Expected:

```text
authorization rejected or unresolved according to policy
```

---

## Stale approval

Approve:

```text
release plan revision 8
```

Then materially modify the plan.

Expected:

```text
prior approval no longer silently applies
```

---

## Conflicting authorities

Two legitimate sources claim authority over the same property.

Expected:

```text
explicit authority conflict
no silent last-write-wins resolution
```

---

## Native permission mismatch

GitHub permits a merge.

Monad governance does not.

Expected:

```text
native action may occur
Monad records governance violation or unauthorized state
reconciliation does not reinterpret permission as authority
```

---

## Historical authority

Actor had authority at time `T1` and was revoked at `T2`.

Query the action performed at `T1`.

Expected:

```text
historically authorized
currently not authorized
```

---

## AI proposal

AI drafts an ADR.

Expected:

```text
AI = producer
ADR = proposed
no implicit acceptance authority
```

---

## AI self-escalation

AI proposes a policy granting itself deployment authority.

Expected:

```text
proposal allowed if within capability
activation requires independent legitimate authority
```

---

# Governing principle

Monad should make authority sufficiently explicit that consequential engineering state can answer:

> Who or what was entitled to establish this?

> Over exactly what scope?

> Under which higher-order rule?

> Was that authority valid at the time?

> Was it delegated?

> Was the delegation still active?

> Did the actor merely have capability, or actual authority?

> Was the approval tied to this exact revision?

> Did native permission agree with semantic authority?

> Which authority wins if legitimate sources conflict?

> Can the decision be explained afterward?

The objective is not centralized control for its own sake.

It is governed engineering in which legitimate control is visible, bounded, traceable, and proportional.

> **Authority should be explicit enough to govern consequential action without turning ordinary engineering into ceremony.**
