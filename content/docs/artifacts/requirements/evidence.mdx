# Evidence Requirements

Engineering systems make claims constantly.

A build is healthy.

A requirement is satisfied.

A release is safe.

A migration succeeded.

An architecture assumption remains valid.

A security control is operating.

An AI-generated change is correct.

A policy obligation has been fulfilled.

Those statements should not become trusted engineering state merely because someone—or something—asserted them.

Monad should make it possible to ask:

> **What inspectable evidence supports this claim?**

Evidence creates the bridge between assertion and justified confidence.

It allows engineering state to become:

* inspectable,
* challengeable,
* reproducible,
* reviewable,
* auditable,
* falsifiable,
* and governable.

<Callout type="info" title="Evidence supports claims; it does not become truth automatically">
Evidence can strengthen, weaken, qualify, contradict, or fail to resolve a Claim. The existence of Evidence does not guarantee that the Claim is correct.
</Callout>

---

# Requirement family scope

The `REQ-EVD-*` family governs Evidence used to support or challenge:

* Requirements,
* Specifications,
* Claims,
* Decisions,
* architecture assumptions,
* implementation correctness,
* verification results,
* policy obligations,
* migrations,
* releases,
* security properties,
* operational state,
* experiments,
* reviews,
* AI-generated outputs,
* reconciliation,
* and system invariants.

These requirements govern the semantics and handling of Evidence.

They do not require Monad to replace every native system that produces it.

---

# Foundational evidence requirements

## REQ-EVD-001 — Monad MUST represent Evidence as distinct from Claims

A Claim states something believed or asserted.

Evidence provides inspectable support concerning that Claim.

For example:

```text id="mz76xv"
Claim:
  The build is reproducible.

Evidence:
  Two independent builds produced identical artifacts.
```

Monad MUST NOT collapse these into the same concept.

---

## REQ-EVD-002 — Evidence MUST remain distinct from proof

Evidence can increase confidence without establishing mathematical certainty.

The system SHOULD avoid treating:

```text id="84jrnz"
has evidence
```

as equivalent to:

```text id="xik1pd"
proven universally true
```

---

## REQ-EVD-003 — Evidence MUST remain distinct from authority

Authoritative Evidence sources may deserve special treatment within scope.

Evidence does not become authoritative merely because it exists.

---

## REQ-EVD-004 — Evidence MUST remain distinct from provenance

Provenance explains:

> Where did this Evidence come from?

Evidence explains:

> What inspectable information bears on this Claim?

Both may be needed.

They remain separate concepts.

---

## REQ-EVD-005 — Evidence MUST remain distinct from approval

A reviewer may approve a release after examining Evidence.

The Evidence itself does not perform the approval.

---

## REQ-EVD-006 — Evidence MUST remain distinct from verification

Verification is the process of evaluating an expectation.

Evidence is the inspectable material used to support the resulting evaluation.

---

# Evidence identity

## REQ-EVD-007 — Consequential Evidence SHOULD have stable identity

Evidence that participates in:

* verification,
* governance,
* release decisions,
* review,
* traceability,
* or historical analysis

SHOULD be independently identifiable.

---

## REQ-EVD-008 — Evidence identity MUST NOT depend solely on file location

Moving:

```text id="qifblp"
reports/test-output.json
```

to:

```text id="aw3kml"
artifacts/tests/test-output.json
```

SHOULD NOT automatically create different semantic Evidence if the underlying Evidence remains the same.

---

## REQ-EVD-009 — Multiple Representations MAY refer to the same Evidence

The same Evidence may appear as:

* native test report,
* normalized record,
* summary,
* dashboard entry,
* generated Markdown,
* JSON API result.

Those Representations SHOULD remain distinguishable from the underlying Evidence identity.

---

# Evidence structure

## REQ-EVD-010 — Evidence SHOULD identify its subject

Where meaningful, Monad SHOULD be able to determine what the Evidence concerns.

Examples include:

```text id="s5pj7g"
Requirement
Implementation
Release
Migration
Claim
Component
Interface
Policy obligation
Environment
Artifact
```

---

## REQ-EVD-011 — Evidence SHOULD identify its type

Evidence types MAY include:

* test result,
* build output,
* static-analysis result,
* benchmark,
* experiment result,
* security scan,
* verification report,
* migration report,
* execution result,
* release attestation,
* operational observation,
* human review finding,
* incident record,
* documentary record.

The canonical taxonomy belongs in the domain model or specification.

---

## REQ-EVD-012 — Evidence SHOULD identify its source

Where origin matters, Evidence SHOULD identify:

* native system,
* file,
* API,
* tool,
* actor,
* environment,
* process,
* or other source.

---

## REQ-EVD-013 — Evidence SHOULD identify the relevant revision or state

Evidence often applies only to a specific:

* commit,
* artifact,
* schema version,
* release,
* configuration,
* environment,
* model version,
* or system state.

That applicability SHOULD be explicit where consequential.

---

## REQ-EVD-014 — Evidence SHOULD identify when it was produced or observed

Time is essential when the underlying system can change.

---

## REQ-EVD-015 — Evidence SHOULD preserve sufficient provenance for interpretation

Where necessary, Evidence SHOULD identify:

* producer,
* source,
* method,
* tool,
* tool version,
* environment,
* transformation,
* and revision.

---

# Evidence and Claims

## REQ-EVD-016 — Evidence SHOULD explicitly relate to the Claim it supports or challenges

The existence of Evidence near a Claim in the filesystem MUST NOT be treated as sufficient semantic linkage.

---

## REQ-EVD-017 — Evidence relationships SHOULD be typed

Monad SHOULD be capable of distinguishing relationships such as:

```text id="c61ptz"
supports
contradicts
qualifies
verifies
invalidates
derived-from
corroborates
```

Exact relationship names belong in the domain model.

---

## REQ-EVD-018 — One Evidence record MAY support multiple Claims

A successful integration test may support:

* an interface requirement,
* a behavioral requirement,
* and a release-readiness Claim.

The relationships SHOULD remain explicit.

---

## REQ-EVD-019 — One Claim MAY depend on multiple Evidence records

A consequential Claim SHOULD NOT be forced into a one-to-one Evidence model.

---

## REQ-EVD-020 — Evidence MUST NOT silently strengthen the Claim beyond what it demonstrates

For example:

```text id="vhknne"
Evidence:
  test passed on Linux x86_64
```

does not automatically justify:

```text id="d69c9w"
Claim:
  system works on all supported platforms
```

---

# Evidence scope

## REQ-EVD-021 — Evidence MUST be interpreted within scope

Relevant scope MAY include:

* Subject,
* revision,
* platform,
* environment,
* configuration,
* dataset,
* dependency versions,
* input,
* time,
* workload,
* threat model,
* and operational conditions.

---

## REQ-EVD-022 — Evidence SHOULD expose material scope limitations

For example:

```text id="1974ht"
verified_on:
  - linux-x86_64

not_verified_on:
  - macos-arm64
  - windows-x86_64
```

where those differences matter.

---

## REQ-EVD-023 — Evidence from one scope MUST NOT automatically generalize to another

Evidence concerning:

```text id="r7eg15"
development environment
```

does not automatically establish behavior in:

```text id="10jg0w"
production
```

---

# Evidence validity

## REQ-EVD-024 — Monad SHOULD distinguish Evidence validity from Evidence relevance

Evidence may be perfectly authentic yet irrelevant to a particular Claim.

For example:

```text id="ep1k2y"
valid:
  test result genuinely came from CI

irrelevant:
  test covered an obsolete implementation revision
```

---

## REQ-EVD-025 — Evidence SHOULD expose conditions that affect validity

These may include:

* failed setup,
* corrupted input,
* invalid test fixture,
* missing dependency,
* broken instrumentation,
* incomplete execution,
* unsupported environment,
* expired credentials,
* or known tool defect.

---

## REQ-EVD-026 — Invalid Evidence SHOULD remain historically inspectable where consequential

Invalidation SHOULD NOT require silent deletion.

---

## REQ-EVD-027 — Invalid Evidence MUST NOT continue supporting current Claims as though valid

---

# Evidence freshness

## REQ-EVD-028 — Evidence SHOULD support freshness evaluation

Monad SHOULD be capable of determining whether Evidence may have become stale because the relevant system state changed.

---

## REQ-EVD-029 — Evidence freshness SHOULD be dependency-aware where practical

Evidence about Component A MAY remain valid after an unrelated documentation change.

Evidence about Component A MAY become stale after:

* its source changes,
* a dependency changes,
* configuration changes,
* environment assumptions change,
* or a governing Specification changes.

---

## REQ-EVD-030 — Historical Evidence MUST remain distinguishable from current Evidence

A historical pass remains useful history.

It does not automatically establish present satisfaction.

---

## REQ-EVD-031 — Stale Evidence SHOULD remain inspectable

Staleness SHOULD normally alter applicability, not erase history.

---

# Evidence revision binding

## REQ-EVD-032 — Evidence SHOULD bind to immutable revisions where available

For example:

```text id="ra6js6"
source_commit: 81d91ef
```

is usually stronger historical context than:

```text id="88z2jl"
branch: main
```

---

## REQ-EVD-033 — Moving references MAY be preserved alongside their resolved revision

Example:

```text id="zn8yvf"
requested_ref: main
resolved_commit: 81d91ef
```

---

## REQ-EVD-034 — Evidence SHOULD NOT silently rebind to newer revisions

A report produced against revision A MUST NOT become Evidence for revision B merely because a branch name moved.

---

# Direct and indirect Evidence

## REQ-EVD-035 — Monad SHOULD distinguish direct from indirect Evidence where meaningful

Direct Evidence may observe the property of interest more directly.

Indirect Evidence may support the Claim through inference.

Example:

```text id="jijbbg"
Direct:
  endpoint returned expected response.

Indirect:
  implementation contains code that appears capable of returning it.
```

Both may be useful.

They do not necessarily carry equal weight.

---

## REQ-EVD-036 — Indirect Evidence SHOULD preserve the reasoning or relationship connecting it to the Claim

---

# Primary and derived Evidence

## REQ-EVD-037 — Primary Evidence SHOULD remain distinguishable from derived Evidence

Example:

```text id="gi7j9z"
Primary:
  raw test report

Derived:
  summarized verification dashboard
```

---

## REQ-EVD-038 — Derived Evidence SHOULD identify its source Evidence

---

## REQ-EVD-039 — Derived Evidence MUST NOT hide material contradictions present in its inputs

---

## REQ-EVD-040 — A summary MUST NOT silently replace retained primary Evidence when primary Evidence is required for inspection

---

# Evidence independence

## REQ-EVD-041 — Monad SHOULD support reasoning about independent Evidence sources

Three independently produced observations may provide stronger corroboration than three copies of the same source.

---

## REQ-EVD-042 — Derived copies MUST NOT be counted as independent corroboration

Example:

```text id="edrfb9"
original test result
  ↓
Markdown summary
  ↓
dashboard card
  ↓
AI summary
```

This remains one underlying observation chain.

---

## REQ-EVD-043 — Shared dependencies SHOULD be discoverable where they materially affect independence

Two independent test runners using the same faulty fixture may not provide truly independent Evidence.

---

# Negative Evidence

## REQ-EVD-044 — Evidence MAY weaken or contradict a Claim

Evidence is not restricted to positive support.

---

## REQ-EVD-045 — Contradictory Evidence MUST NOT be discarded merely because other Evidence supports the Claim

---

## REQ-EVD-046 — A failed test MAY be Evidence even when the failure mechanism is not yet understood

The interpretation may remain uncertain.

---

# Absence of Evidence

## REQ-EVD-047 — Absence of Evidence MUST NOT automatically be treated as Evidence of absence

Example:

```text id="bwqyd3"
No security finding was recorded.
```

does not necessarily imply:

```text id="577blk"
No vulnerability exists.
```

---

## REQ-EVD-048 — Absence MAY be informative when the observation method had a meaningful opportunity to detect the condition

For example:

```text id="aejbx5"
A complete scan found no matching dependency.
```

may support absence more strongly than:

```text id="7q47pn"
Nobody reported seeing the dependency.
```

---

## REQ-EVD-049 — Evidence records SHOULD preserve enough method context to evaluate whether absence is meaningful

---

# Evidence uncertainty

## REQ-EVD-050 — Evidence SHOULD support uncertain or inconclusive outcomes

Not all Evidence is:

```text id="ej3c19"
pass
```

or:

```text id="e6tjel"
fail
```

Useful states may include:

```text id="lhkd0j"
incomplete
blocked
inconclusive
conditional
disputed
```

Exact vocabulary belongs in the specification.

---

## REQ-EVD-051 — Inconclusive Evidence MUST NOT be silently treated as positive Evidence

---

## REQ-EVD-052 — Unknown MUST remain distinguishable from false

---

# Evidence strength

## REQ-EVD-053 — Monad SHOULD NOT require a universal numeric Evidence score

A single scalar risks obscuring different dimensions such as:

* directness,
* validity,
* relevance,
* freshness,
* independence,
* reproducibility,
* scope,
* and source quality.

---

## REQ-EVD-054 — Evidence strength MAY be represented through explicit dimensions where useful

The system MAY model attributes such as:

```text id="ajh8gn"
directness
freshness
scope_match
reproducibility
independence
source_reliability
```

if those dimensions prove useful.

---

## REQ-EVD-055 — Evidence strength MUST NOT replace engineering judgment where interpretation remains necessary

---

# Evidence reproducibility

## REQ-EVD-056 — Evidence-producing methods SHOULD be reproducible where practical

Monad SHOULD preserve sufficient information to rerun a method where rerunning matters.

---

## REQ-EVD-057 — Deterministic Evidence SHOULD support exact reproduction where practical

---

## REQ-EVD-058 — Non-deterministic Evidence SHOULD preserve enough context for statistical or approximate reproduction

This is particularly relevant to:

* AI evaluation,
* randomized testing,
* fuzzing,
* distributed systems,
* performance measurements,
* production telemetry.

---

## REQ-EVD-059 — Reproduction results SHOULD create new Evidence rather than overwriting the original

---

# Evidence integrity

## REQ-EVD-060 — Consequential Evidence SHOULD support integrity checking where appropriate

Possible mechanisms include:

* content digests,
* immutable artifact storage,
* signed attestations,
* native CI retention,
* repository history.

---

## REQ-EVD-061 — Integrity MUST remain distinct from correctness

A tamper-proof incorrect test result remains incorrect.

---

## REQ-EVD-062 — Evidence alteration SHOULD preserve history where consequential

If Evidence is:

* corrected,
* redacted,
* normalized,
* regenerated,
* or superseded,

the relationship to the prior form SHOULD remain inspectable.

---

# Evidence provenance

## REQ-EVD-063 — Evidence SHOULD preserve its producer

Where relevant, users SHOULD be able to determine who or what produced it.

---

## REQ-EVD-064 — Evidence SHOULD preserve its method

A result without knowing how it was obtained may be difficult to interpret.

---

## REQ-EVD-065 — Evidence SHOULD preserve relevant tool identity

For example:

```text id="qtbpmy"
tool: rustc
version: 1.x
```

or:

```text id="a8z0hx"
tool: integration-test-runner
version: 4.2
```

where the exact version materially affects the result.

---

## REQ-EVD-066 — AI-generated Evidence summaries SHOULD identify the underlying Evidence

* GitHub Actions reports,

* compiler diagnostics,
* SARIF,
* benchmark outputs,
* deployment logs,
* observability systems,

* vulnerability scanner results.

## REQ-EVD-068 — Monad SHOULD integrate rather than unnecessarily replace native Evidence producers

## REQ-EVD-069 — Normalization SHOULD preserve access to the native record where practical

# Evidence normalization

## REQ-EVD-071 — Monad MAY normalize heterogeneous Evidence into a common read model

Normalization MAY make cross-system reasoning easier.

## REQ-EVD-072 — Normalization MUST preserve material source semantics

A native result such as:

```text id="wp32gk"
skipped
```

SHOULD NOT become:

```text id="59w39z"
passed
```

merely because the normalized model lacks a skipped state.

## REQ-EVD-073 — Lossy normalization SHOULD be explicitly identifiable

A bundle may support:

* release readiness,
* security review,
* migration acceptance,
* compliance,
* feature completion,
* verification of a Requirement.

---

## REQ-EVD-075 — Evidence bundles SHOULD preserve member identity

Bundling SHOULD NOT flatten individual Evidence into an opaque attachment.

## REQ-EVD-076 — Bundle status MUST NOT hide failing or incomplete member Evidence

# Evidence and verification

## REQ-EVD-078 — Verification results SHOULD link to the Evidence used in the evaluation

Example:

```text id="h0b5v3"
Verification VFY-019
    ↓
Evidence

    └── execution-log
```

## REQ-EVD-079 — A verification pass SHOULD remain traceable to the Evidence that justified the pass

---

## REQ-EVD-080 — Verification failure SHOULD preserve the Evidence supporting failure

---

## REQ-EVD-081 — Missing required Evidence SHOULD result in an explicit incomplete or equivalent state rather than an unjustified pass

# Evidence and Requirements

## REQ-EVD-082 — Verifiable Requirements SHOULD identify the Evidence expected to demonstrate satisfaction where practical

This may be expressed through:

* acceptance criteria,
* verification method,
* required Evidence type,
* or verification specification.

---

## REQ-EVD-083 — Evidence SHOULD be traceable to the Requirement it helps satisfy

---

## REQ-EVD-084 — Requirement satisfaction SHOULD NOT be asserted solely because implementation exists

Implementation is not equivalent to Evidence of correctness.

# Evidence and Specifications

## REQ-EVD-085 — Conformance Evidence SHOULD identify the relevant Specification revision

---

## REQ-EVD-086 — Evidence SHOULD support clause-level traceability where finer granularity materially improves verification

Not every Specification requires clause-level Evidence.

---

## REQ-EVD-087 — Evidence generated against a superseded Specification SHOULD NOT automatically establish conformance with the replacement

# Evidence and Decisions

## REQ-EVD-088 — Consequential Decisions SHOULD reference material Evidence that informed them where available

---

## REQ-EVD-089 — Decision rationale SHOULD distinguish Evidence from interpretation

For example:

```text id="l7vi6s"
Evidence:
  index lookup median = 8 ms
  graph lookup median = 42 ms

  graph complexity does not justify the measured benefit for this use case.
```

## REQ-EVD-090 — Decision records SHOULD preserve contradictory Evidence where it materially affected the trade-off

# Evidence and experiments

## REQ-EVD-091 — Experiments SHOULD produce inspectable Evidence

The result of an experiment SHOULD NOT exist only as a narrative conclusion.

---

## REQ-EVD-092 — Experiment Evidence SHOULD preserve experimental conditions

This may include:

* fixtures,
* environment,
* variables,
* controls,
* procedure,
* instrumentation,
* and raw results.

---

## REQ-EVD-093 — Experiment conclusions SHOULD remain distinct from experimental Evidence

---

## REQ-EVD-094 — Negative experimental results SHOULD be retained when they materially reduce uncertainty

# Evidence and Reviews

## REQ-EVD-095 — Review findings MAY function as Evidence

For example:

```text id="hn6ehf"
Finding:
  specification leaves retry semantics undefined.
```

## REQ-EVD-096 — A review report SHOULD NOT automatically be treated as Evidence that every reviewed property is correct

# Evidence and approval

## REQ-EVD-101 — Approvals SHOULD be able to reference the Evidence reviewed

---

## REQ-EVD-102 — Approval MUST NOT retroactively transform weak Evidence into strong Evidence

```text id="b3v18x"
  target schema contains expected structure
```

An AI system may produce Evidence that:


That does not automatically establish:

```text id="i6catq"
the artifact is actually unsafe.
```

---

## REQ-EVD-119 — AI-generated analysis SHOULD preserve relevant model provenance

* model,
* provider,
* configuration,
* prompt,
* retrieval context,
* tool results,

## REQ-EVD-120 — AI-generated summaries SHOULD remain derived Evidence

## REQ-EVD-121 — AI evaluation SHOULD NOT be treated as deterministic verification unless the verification contract explicitly allows it

# Evidence anti-patterns

## Test passed, therefore Requirement satisfied

Only valid if the test actually verifies that Requirement within appropriate scope.

---

## CI is green, therefore release is correct

Green CI is aggregate execution state.

It may not cover every release obligation.

---

## Evidence exists, therefore Claim is true

Evidence can be:

* irrelevant,
* stale,
* invalid,
* incomplete,
* contradictory,
* incorrectly interpreted.

---

## More Evidence is always better

Duplicated or low-quality Evidence can create noise without increasing confidence.

---

## Logs equal Evidence

Logs may become Evidence when used to support a Claim.

Most logs should remain operational records until that role exists.

---

## Latest Evidence wins

Recency matters only within semantic and temporal context.

---

## Human says so

Human testimony may be Evidence.

It remains subject to scope, expertise, memory, bias, and interpretation.

---

## AI says so

AI output may be useful Evidence about what the AI concluded.

It is not automatically Evidence that the conclusion is correct.

---

## Native tool says pass

Tool output must be interpreted according to:

* what the tool actually checked,
* its configuration,
* its scope,
* and its reliability.

---

## Screenshot as universal proof

Screenshots can demonstrate visible state.

They may provide weak Evidence for deeper system properties.

---

## Dashboard as primary source

Dashboards are usually derived projections.

Primary Evidence should remain traceable.

---

## Evidence without revision

Mutable software makes unbound Evidence difficult to interpret historically.

---

## Evidence without method

A number without knowing how it was produced may be nearly meaningless.

---

## Evidence without negative cases

Only storing successful results creates distorted engineering history.

---

## Delete failed Evidence after fixing the problem

Historical failure Evidence can explain why later changes were necessary.

---

## One confidence score

Collapsing complex Evidence quality into one number can create false precision.

---

## Store everything forever

Evidence retention should be deliberate.

Unlimited retention creates:

* cost,
* privacy risk,
* security risk,
* operational burden,
* noise.

---

# Progressive formalization

Evidence support can mature gradually.

## Stage 1 — Native artifacts

Preserve links to:

* CI runs,
* reports,
* tests,
* benchmarks,
* scanner output.

---

## Stage 2 — Stable Evidence identity

Important Evidence receives durable identity.

---

## Stage 3 — Claim linkage

Evidence explicitly supports or contradicts Claims.

---

## Stage 4 — Requirement and verification linkage

Evidence becomes part of an explicit assurance chain.

---

## Stage 5 — Freshness and revision awareness

The system knows when Evidence may no longer apply.

---

## Stage 6 — Evidence obligations

Requirements and Policies can demand specific Evidence before state transitions or execution.

---

## Stage 7 — Cross-system assurance

Evidence from multiple native systems can support integrated reasoning.

---

## Stage 8 — Analytical assurance

Monad can identify:

* missing Evidence,
* stale Evidence,
* contradictory Evidence,
* duplicated origins,
* weak Evidence chains,
* overbroad Claims.

# Evidence review checklist

For consequential Evidence, ask:

1. What Claim does this Evidence bear on?
2. What Requirement or Specification does that Claim derive from?
3. What exactly was observed?
4. What method produced the observation?
5. Who or what produced it?
6. Which tool was used?
7. Which tool version?
8. Which revision was tested?
9. Which environment?
10. Which configuration?
11. Which input data?
12. When was it produced?
13. When was the underlying state observed?
14. Is the Evidence direct or indirect?
15. Is it primary or derived?
16. Is its provenance inspectable?
17. Is the source still available?
18. Is the Evidence valid?
19. Is it relevant?
20. Is it fresh?
21. Does its scope match the Claim?
22. Does it generalize beyond the observed environment?
23. Is the method reproducible?
24. Is the result deterministic?
25. Are negative results preserved?
26. Is contradictory Evidence present?
27. Is this Evidence independent from corroborating Evidence?
28. Does it share common upstream sources?
29. Was any normalization lossy?
30. Is the raw result retained where needed?
31. Does an AI-generated summary stand between us and the primary Evidence?
32. Could the Evidence have become stale?
33. Does a newer revision invalidate applicability?
34. Has the Evidence itself been invalidated?
35. Does a Policy require this Evidence?
36. Is this Evidence required for approval?
37. Is missing Evidence visible?
38. Does an exception merely waive the obligation?
39. Is sensitive information protected?
40. Is retention appropriate?
41. Could the Evidence be independently inspected?
42. Could the method be rerun?
43. Does the Claim overstate what the Evidence demonstrates?
44. What Evidence would falsify the Claim?
45. Are we preserving contradictory results?
46. Are we collecting Evidence because it helps or merely because we can?

# Relationship to Claims

A Claim says:

> **This is believed to be true.**

Evidence says:

> **Here is inspectable information that bears on whether it is true.**

This distinction is foundational.

---

# Relationship to provenance

Provenance answers:

> **Where did this Evidence come from?**

Without provenance, Evidence can be difficult to evaluate.

Without Evidence, provenance merely tells us where an assertion originated.

---

# Relationship to verification

Verification asks:

> **Does the observed system satisfy the explicit expectation?**

Evidence provides the observations used to answer that question.

A useful relationship is:

```text id="xh65x1"
Expectation
    ↓
Verification Method
    ↓
Execution / Inspection
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Verification Result
```

---

# Relationship to Requirements

Requirements define obligations.

Evidence helps establish whether those obligations have been satisfied.

A Requirement without a plausible Evidence path may not be sufficiently verifiable.

---

# Relationship to decisions

Decisions should be informed by Evidence where the question is empirical.

The decision record preserves the interpretation and trade-off.

The Evidence preserves the observations that informed it.

---

# Relationship to releases

A Release can represent a consequential assertion:

> **This version is suitable for distribution or deployment.**

That assertion should be supported by inspectable Evidence appropriate to the release's risk.

---

# Relationship to AI systems

AI systems create a particular Evidence challenge.

AI can:

* summarize Evidence,
* classify Evidence,
* locate Evidence,
* compare Evidence,
* identify contradictions,
* propose interpretations,
* generate candidate findings.

But an AI-generated interpretation SHOULD remain distinguishable from the primary Evidence being interpreted.

Where an AI says:

```text id="uy0v7w"
All requirements appear satisfied.
```

Monad should still be capable of asking:

```text id="eiy03f"
Which Requirements?

Which verification results?

Which Evidence?

Which revisions?

Which assumptions?

Which sources?

Which results were incomplete?

Which contradictory Evidence existed?
```

The quality of AI reasoning improves when these relationships are explicit.

---

# Evidence and the engineering argument

Monad's Artifact system can be understood as an inspectable engineering argument:

```text id="5u0v0y"
Problem
  ↓
Goal
  ↓
Requirement
  ↓
Decision
  ↓
Specification
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Release
```

Evidence does not sit merely at the end.

It can challenge any part of that chain.

Evidence may reveal that:

* the Problem was misunderstood,
* the Requirement is unnecessary,
* the Decision was based on a false assumption,
* the Specification is incomplete,
* the implementation does not conform,
* the verification method is inadequate,
* or the release should not proceed.

A mature engineering system permits Evidence to revise understanding rather than merely certify preexisting conclusions.

---

# Definition of Ready

An Evidence-producing activity is ready when:

* the target Claim or expectation is identifiable,
* relevant scope is known,
* the producing method is sufficiently defined,
* required inputs are available,
* the relevant revision is known where necessary,
* the environment is sufficiently specified,
* required provenance can be captured,
* expected result semantics are known,
* and applicable security or retention constraints are understood.

---

# Definition of Done

A consequential Evidence record is complete enough when:

* it has durable identity where required,
* its Subject is identifiable,
* its relevant Claim or expectation is identifiable where applicable,
* its source is known,
* its producer is known where material,
* its method is known,
* its relevant revision is recorded,
* its relevant scope is recorded,
* its result is explicit,
* its provenance is sufficient for interpretation,
* raw or native Evidence remains accessible where required,
* derived forms identify their source,
* uncertainty is preserved,
* contradictions are not hidden,
* invalidation or staleness can be represented,
* sensitive content is handled appropriately,
* and downstream relationships can be traced.

---

# Core invariants

The Evidence system SHOULD preserve these distinctions:

```text id="29oet0"
Evidence ≠ Claim

Evidence ≠ Proof

Evidence ≠ Authority

Evidence ≠ Approval

Evidence ≠ Verification

Evidence ≠ Provenance

Existence ≠ Validity

Validity ≠ Relevance

Historical validity ≠ Current applicability

Integrity ≠ Correctness

Source identity ≠ Truth

More Evidence ≠ More confidence automatically

AI analysis ≠ Primary Evidence

Successful execution ≠ Successful verification

Missing Evidence ≠ Evidence of absence

Waived Evidence ≠ Satisfied Evidence obligation

Derived Evidence ≠ Independent Evidence

Unknown ≠ false
```

These distinctions prevent assurance mechanisms from collapsing into ceremony.

---

# Governing principle

Monad should make it possible to ask:

> What Claim are we making?

> What Evidence supports it?

> What Evidence contradicts it?

> Where did that Evidence come from?

> What exactly was observed?

> Under what conditions?

> Against which revision?

> Using which method?

> Is the Evidence still valid?

> Is it still relevant?

> Is it still fresh?

> Is it independent?

> Can it be reproduced?

> Is the Claim broader than the Evidence?

> Is any required Evidence missing?

> What would falsify the Claim?

> Would we change our engineering decision if contradictory Evidence appeared?

The objective is not to attach artifacts to engineering conclusions after the fact.

It is to make justified engineering confidence inspectable.

> **Engineering Claims should be as strong as the Evidence supporting them—and no stronger.**
