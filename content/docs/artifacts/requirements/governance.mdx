# Governance Requirements

Engineering governance exists to answer a simple but consequential question:

> **Under what conditions may this action legitimately occur?**

That question becomes difficult when software engineering spans:

* humans,
* AI agents,
* repositories,
* build systems,
* deployment platforms,
* documentation,
* policies,
* releases,
* evidence,
* external services,
* and evolving system state.

Governance is the mechanism by which Monad makes those constraints explicit, inspectable, enforceable where appropriate, and proportional to the consequence of the action.

It connects:

```text
Constitution
    ↓ constrains
Authority
    ↓
Capabilities
    ↓
Policies
    ↓
Obligations
    ↓
Approvals and Evidence
    ↓
Authorized Operation
    ↓
Execution
```

This stack is conceptual rather than a complete execution algorithm.

The central requirement is that these concerns remain distinguishable.

<Callout type="info" title="Governance is not permission management">
Governance determines whether consequential engineering action is legitimate under the system's rules. Native permissions may help enforce that decision, but permission alone does not establish authority, policy compliance, or correctness.
</Callout>

---

# Requirement family scope

The `REQ-GOV-*` family governs:

* constitutional constraints,
* authority relationships,
* policy evaluation,
* approvals,
* obligations,
* lifecycle gates,
* release gates,
* exception handling,
* emergency actions,
* separation of duties,
* evidence requirements,
* AI autonomy,
* delegated action,
* policy conflicts,
* authorization freshness,
* governance provenance,
* governance state transitions,
* and reconciliation after governed operations.

The family does not prescribe one universal workflow for all engineering activity.

Governance SHOULD become stricter as consequence and irreversibility increase.

---

# Foundational governance requirements

## REQ-GOV-001 — Governance MUST be explicit for consequential action

Where an operation can materially affect:

* system correctness,
* production state,
* security,
* architecture,
* identity,
* authority,
* policy,
* release,
* evidence,
* or durable engineering state,

Monad MUST be capable of determining which governance rules apply.

---

## REQ-GOV-002 — Governance MUST be proportional to consequence

Monad MUST NOT require the same governance burden for every operation.

Routine low-risk changes SHOULD remain lightweight.

High-risk, irreversible, security-sensitive, identity-changing, authority-changing, or production-impacting actions MAY require stronger controls.

---

## REQ-GOV-003 — Governance MUST NOT collapse capability, authority, permission, and policy

The following remain distinct:

```text
Capability = what an actor can technically do
Authority  = what an actor may legitimately establish or authorize
Permission = what a native system technically permits
Policy     = rules constraining action
```

Monad MUST preserve those distinctions.

---

## REQ-GOV-004 — Governance MUST be explainable

For consequential governed operations, Monad SHOULD be able to explain:

* which rules applied,
* which authority was evaluated,
* which conditions were satisfied,
* which conditions were unmet,
* which approvals were required,
* which obligations were created,
* and why the operation was allowed, denied, deferred, or unresolved.

---

## REQ-GOV-005 — Governance decisions MUST be attributable

A consequential governance decision SHOULD identify:

* the relevant rules,
* policy versions,
* authority grants,
* approvals,
* actors,
* system state,
* and evaluation time.

---

# Constitutional governance

## REQ-GOV-006 — Monad MUST support higher-order governance constraints

Monad SHOULD support a constitutional layer that constrains:

* allowed authority structures,
* policy creation,
* delegation,
* lifecycle rules,
* capability boundaries,
* amendment mechanisms,
* and other governance primitives.

Conceptually:

```text
Constitution → constrains Governance
```

---

## REQ-GOV-007 — Constitutional rules MUST NOT be silently overridden

Lower-level governance rules MUST NOT override constitutional constraints unless the Constitution explicitly permits that form of override.

---

## REQ-GOV-008 — Constitutional change MUST itself be governed

Changing the rules that constrain governance MUST require a defined amendment process.

---

## REQ-GOV-009 — Constitutional authority MUST remain distinguishable from ordinary policy authority

A project policy SHOULD NOT automatically possess authority to amend constitutional constraints.

---

# Governance scope

## REQ-GOV-010 — Governance rules MUST be scoped

Governance SHOULD be capable of applying to scopes such as:

* project,
* repository,
* component,
* domain,
* artifact,
* environment,
* lifecycle state,
* release,
* operation,
* actor class,
* capability,
* or semantic Subject.

---

## REQ-GOV-011 — More specific governance MAY refine broader governance within allowed bounds

A project-wide rule MAY permit domain-specific refinement.

Such refinement MUST NOT exceed the authority granted by broader rules.

---

## REQ-GOV-012 — Scope inheritance and override behavior MUST be deterministic

Where multiple governance layers apply, Monad MUST define how:

* inheritance,
* override,
* composition,
* precedence,
* and conflict

are resolved.

---

# Policy evaluation

## REQ-GOV-013 — Governance policies SHOULD be explicit and inspectable

Repeated consequential rules SHOULD be representable as explicit policies rather than remaining scattered through undocumented implementation logic.

---

## REQ-GOV-014 — Policy evaluation MUST remain distinct from execution

A policy decision such as:

```text
allow
```

MUST NOT imply:

```text
executed
```

Likewise, successful execution MUST NOT imply that policy evaluation occurred correctly.

---

## REQ-GOV-015 — Policy evaluation SHOULD be deterministic for equivalent relevant state

Given the same:

* policy revisions,
* authority state,
* actor,
* action,
* target,
* context,
* and relevant system state,

the evaluation SHOULD produce the same result.

---

## REQ-GOV-016 — Policy results SHOULD be structured

A governance evaluation SHOULD be capable of returning information such as:

```text
decision:
  allowed | denied | conditional | unresolved

matched_rules:
  ...

required_approvals:
  ...

required_evidence:
  ...

obligations:
  ...

reason:
  ...

policy_revision:
  ...
```

The exact representation belongs in the governance specification.

---

## REQ-GOV-017 — Policy non-applicability MUST remain distinguishable from denial

These are not equivalent:

```text
rule does not apply
```

and:

```text
rule applies and denies
```

Monad SHOULD preserve that distinction.

---

## REQ-GOV-018 — Unknown governance state MUST remain distinguishable from explicit denial

If governance cannot be resolved, the result SHOULD be represented as unresolved rather than falsely asserting that a denial rule exists.

The governing policy MAY still require safe failure.

---

# Governance defaults

## REQ-GOV-019 — Default behavior MUST be explicit per scope

Monad MUST NOT silently assume one universal governance default such as:

```text
default deny everything
```

or:

```text
default permit everything
```

Defaults SHOULD reflect the risk and semantics of the governed scope.

---

## REQ-GOV-020 — Fail-open and fail-closed behavior MUST be intentional

Where a governance subsystem is unavailable, the system SHOULD explicitly define whether an operation:

* fails closed,
* fails open,
* defers,
* falls back to native controls,
* or uses previously validated local state.

---

## REQ-GOV-021 — High-consequence operations SHOULD favor safe failure

Where uncertainty could create material harm, Monad SHOULD favor refusal or deferral over invented authorization.

---

# Approvals

## REQ-GOV-022 — Governance MUST support approval requirements

Policies SHOULD be capable of requiring one or more approvals before consequential action.

---

## REQ-GOV-023 — Approvals MUST bind to a concrete target

An approval SHOULD identify:

* the operation,
* artifact,
* change,
* release,
* policy revision,
* or other governed target.

---

## REQ-GOV-024 — Approvals SHOULD bind to relevant revisions

Approval of:

```text
revision 17
```

SHOULD NOT automatically authorize materially different:

```text
revision 22
```

---

## REQ-GOV-025 — Approval authority MUST be validated

The existence of an approval record MUST NOT establish that the approver had sufficient authority.

---

## REQ-GOV-026 — Approval MAY require multiple authorities

Monad SHOULD support governance rules requiring:

* multiple reviewers,
* independent domains,
* security review,
* release review,
* or other multi-party approval structures.

---

## REQ-GOV-027 — Separation of duties SHOULD be expressible

Policies SHOULD be capable of requiring constraints such as:

```text
author ≠ approver
```

where justified.

This SHOULD NOT be universal.

---

## REQ-GOV-028 — Self-approval MAY be permitted or prohibited by policy

Monad SHOULD not assume self-approval is always invalid.

The rule SHOULD depend on consequence, scope, and governance policy.

---

# Approval freshness

## REQ-GOV-029 — Material changes SHOULD invalidate or trigger re-evaluation of approvals

Changes to:

* target revision,
* execution plan,
* environment,
* dependencies,
* evidence,
* policy,
* or relevant system state

MAY render prior approvals stale.

---

## REQ-GOV-030 — Approval expiration SHOULD be supported

Where time matters, approval SHOULD support explicit expiration.

---

## REQ-GOV-031 — Long-lived approvals SHOULD require deliberate policy

Broad approvals with indefinite validity SHOULD be explicit rather than accidental.

---

# Obligations

## REQ-GOV-032 — Governance MUST support obligations in addition to allow or deny

Governance SHOULD be able to state:

```text
operation permitted,
provided that obligation X is satisfied
```

or:

```text
operation permitted,
and obligation Y follows afterward
```

---

## REQ-GOV-033 — Obligations MAY occur before, during, or after execution

Examples include:

### Before

* obtain approval,
* run required verification,
* collect evidence,
* resolve conflict.

### During

* preserve audit context,
* constrain execution scope,
* enforce resource limits.

### After

* reconcile state,
* publish evidence,
* notify stakeholders,
* run post-deployment verification,
* perform retrospective review.

---

## REQ-GOV-034 — Execution success MUST NOT imply obligation completion

A technically successful operation MAY remain governance-incomplete if required obligations remain outstanding.

---

## REQ-GOV-035 — Outstanding obligations SHOULD remain inspectable

Monad SHOULD be capable of identifying which obligations remain:

* pending,
* satisfied,
* failed,
* waived,
* expired,
* or superseded.

---

# Evidence requirements

## REQ-GOV-036 — Governance MAY require evidence as a precondition

A policy MAY require evidence such as:

* passing verification,
* review record,
* signed artifact,
* test result,
* security scan,
* migration rehearsal,
* or release validation.

---

## REQ-GOV-037 — Evidence existence MUST NOT imply evidence sufficiency

The presence of a test result does not necessarily prove that the required condition was satisfied.

Governance SHOULD evaluate evidence according to explicit requirements.

---

## REQ-GOV-038 — Governance SHOULD record which evidence supported a consequential authorization

For important decisions, Monad SHOULD preserve the evidence set or references relied upon at authorization time.

---

## REQ-GOV-039 — Stale evidence SHOULD be detectable

Evidence MAY become stale when:

* implementation changes,
* dependencies change,
* environment changes,
* requirement scope changes,
* or the evidence itself expires.

---

# Policy and authority

## REQ-GOV-040 — Policy MUST NOT create authority from nothing

A policy that references an actor MUST NOT implicitly grant that actor authority unless the governing authority model explicitly permits such a grant.

---

## REQ-GOV-041 — Policy activation MUST require legitimate authority

A policy MUST NOT become binding merely because it is parseable or loaded by a policy engine.

---

## REQ-GOV-042 — Policies MUST operate within constitutional and authority constraints

A project policy MUST NOT be able to grant permissions beyond the legitimate authority available to the project.

---

# Governance over policy changes

## REQ-GOV-043 — Consequential policy changes MUST themselves be governed

A change that alters:

* production permissions,
* release gates,
* AI autonomy,
* authority requirements,
* security controls,
* or constitutional behavior

SHOULD require proportionate review.

---

## REQ-GOV-044 — Policy changes SHOULD support impact analysis

Before activation, Monad SHOULD be able to determine which:

* actors,
* workflows,
* operations,
* components,
* releases,
* or environments

may be affected.

---

## REQ-GOV-045 — Policy changes SHOULD support simulation where practical

Monad MAY evaluate a proposed policy revision against representative historical or hypothetical operations before activation.

---

## REQ-GOV-046 — Policy rollback MUST preserve history

Returning to an earlier policy SHOULD NOT erase:

* the superseded revision,
* decisions made under it,
* or the period during which it was active.

---

# Governance conflicts

## REQ-GOV-047 — Conflicting governance rules MUST be detectable

Monad SHOULD identify when multiple applicable rules produce incompatible outcomes.

---

## REQ-GOV-048 — Conflict resolution precedence MUST be explicit

Conflict MAY be resolved by:

* constitutional hierarchy,
* specificity,
* policy priority,
* scope,
* explicit override,
* or adjudication.

The rule MUST NOT depend on accidental evaluation order.

---

## REQ-GOV-049 — Unresolved governance conflict SHOULD fail safely for consequential operations

Where the system cannot determine which rule governs a material operation, it SHOULD:

* deny,
* defer,
* or escalate

according to explicit policy.

---

# Lifecycle governance

## REQ-GOV-050 — Lifecycle transitions SHOULD be governable

Monad SHOULD support rules governing transitions such as:

```text
draft → approved
approved → active
active → deprecated
deprecated → historical
```

---

## REQ-GOV-051 — Lifecycle transition authority MAY vary by transition

Authority to create a draft MUST NOT automatically imply authority to activate or release it.

---

## REQ-GOV-052 — Invalid transitions SHOULD be blocked or explicitly recorded as governance violations

A technically possible transition that violates lifecycle rules SHOULD NOT silently become accepted state.

---

# Release governance

## REQ-GOV-053 — Release gates SHOULD be explicit

A release policy MAY require:

* accepted Requirements,
* approved Decisions,
* implemented Specifications,
* passing verification,
* required evidence,
* security checks,
* approval,
* clean reconciliation,
* or satisfied obligations.

---

## REQ-GOV-054 — Release authorization SHOULD be revision-aware

The release approval SHOULD identify the exact release candidate or equivalent revision.

---

## REQ-GOV-055 — Release execution and release authorization MUST remain distinct

A deployment system capable of publishing a release MUST NOT automatically possess authority to approve that release.

---

## REQ-GOV-056 — Release gate bypass MUST be explicitly governed

Emergency or exceptional bypass SHOULD require:

* authorized exception,
* documented rationale,
* bounded scope,
* evidence,
* and post-event review where appropriate.

---

# Execution governance

## REQ-GOV-057 — Consequential operations SHOULD pass through a governance boundary

The execution flow SHOULD support:

```text
Intent
  ↓
Resolve operation
  ↓
Evaluate current state
  ↓
Evaluate governance
  ↓
Authorize
  ↓
Revalidate
  ↓
Execute
```

---

## REQ-GOV-058 — Governance SHOULD evaluate the resolved operation

Where a high-level intent expands into concrete operations, policy SHOULD evaluate enough detail to understand actual consequence.

---

## REQ-GOV-059 — Governance MUST NOT rely solely on pre-execution checks made against stale state

Relevant governance SHOULD be revalidated sufficiently close to execution to reduce time-of-check/time-of-use risk.

---

## REQ-GOV-060 — Partial execution MUST remain governable

If only part of an authorized plan succeeds, Monad SHOULD identify:

* completed operations,
* failed operations,
* remaining obligations,
* rollback needs,
* reconciliation needs,
* and whether further authorization is required.

---

## REQ-GOV-061 — Rollback MUST itself be treated as execution

Rollback MAY be consequential and SHOULD therefore be subject to appropriate authority and governance.

---

# Human governance

## REQ-GOV-062 — Human action MUST be governed according to role and authority, not presumed legitimacy

A human user SHOULD NOT bypass governance merely because they are human.

---

## REQ-GOV-063 — Governance SHOULD preserve meaningful human judgment

Monad SHOULD NOT attempt to formalize every engineering judgment into deterministic policy.

Human review remains appropriate where:

* context is ambiguous,
* risk is qualitative,
* trade-offs are novel,
* or formal policy would create false precision.

---

## REQ-GOV-064 — Governance SHOULD minimize unnecessary human ceremony

Human approval SHOULD be required where it adds meaningful governance value, not as a default ritual.

---

# AI governance

## REQ-GOV-065 — AI actors MUST operate under explicit governance

AI actions SHOULD be evaluated using the same foundational concepts as human actions:

* Actor,
* Capability,
* Authority,
* Policy,
* Scope,
* Approval,
* Obligation,
* Evidence.

---

## REQ-GOV-066 — AI autonomy MUST be bounded by explicit scope

An AI actor's autonomous action SHOULD be constrained by factors such as:

* operation type,
* repository,
* component,
* environment,
* risk,
* lifecycle state,
* budget,
* tool,
* and authority.

---

## REQ-GOV-067 — AI-generated governance changes MUST NOT automatically become authoritative

AI MAY draft:

* policies,
* authority grants,
* constitutional amendments,
* approval rules,
* or lifecycle constraints.

Those changes SHOULD remain proposals unless the AI possesses explicit authority to enact them.

---

## REQ-GOV-068 — AI MUST NOT silently weaken constraints governing itself

An AI actor MUST NOT evade its governance by:

* editing the relevant policy,
* changing its role,
* altering authority metadata,
* switching tools,
* changing representation,
* or invoking a less-governed path.

---

## REQ-GOV-069 — Governance SHOULD constrain actions, not merely models

The same underlying AI model MAY operate under different authority and governance contexts.

Governance SHOULD therefore attach to the actor or execution context rather than relying solely on provider or model identity.

---

# Tool governance

## REQ-GOV-070 — Tool availability MUST remain distinct from tool authorization

A registered tool MAY be technically invokable while still being disallowed in the current context.

---

## REQ-GOV-071 — High-risk tools SHOULD support narrower governance

Examples may include tools capable of:

* production deployment,
* secret access,
* destructive database changes,
* repository administration,
* policy modification,
* authority modification,
* or external publication.

---

## REQ-GOV-072 — Tool calls SHOULD preserve governance context

Consequential tool invocation SHOULD retain enough information to explain:

* actor,
* authority,
* policy result,
* target,
* intent,
* operation,
* and relevant revision.

---

# Integration governance

## REQ-GOV-073 — Native systems MAY continue enforcing native controls

Monad SHOULD integrate with:

* Git hosting permissions,
* branch protection,
* operating-system permissions,
* cloud IAM,
* CI gates,
* deployment controls,

rather than assuming all enforcement must be centralized in Monad.

---

## REQ-GOV-074 — Native enforcement MUST NOT substitute for semantic governance where semantics differ

A successful native operation MAY still violate Monad governance.

Monad SHOULD preserve that distinction.

---

## REQ-GOV-075 — Governance SHOULD reconcile native and semantic control state where useful

If Monad expects a branch to require review but GitHub configuration does not enforce it, the mismatch SHOULD be detectable where that integration is managed.

---

# Exceptions

## REQ-GOV-076 — Exceptions MUST be explicit

A governance exception SHOULD identify:

* the rule being excepted,
* target,
* reason,
* approving authority,
* time bounds,
* and obligations.

---

## REQ-GOV-077 — Exceptions MUST be bounded

An exception SHOULD NOT silently become a permanent broad waiver.

---

## REQ-GOV-078 — Exceptions SHOULD expire

Where practical, exception validity SHOULD end automatically.

---

## REQ-GOV-079 — Exception use SHOULD be reviewable

Consequential exceptions SHOULD leave sufficient evidence for later inspection.

---

# Emergency governance

## REQ-GOV-080 — Break-glass procedures MUST be part of governance

Emergency mechanisms SHOULD NOT exist as undocumented backdoors.

> **An exception is part of governance, not the absence of it.**

---

## REQ-GOV-081 — Emergency actions MAY reduce pre-action friction but SHOULD increase post-action evidence

A legitimate emergency path MAY permit faster execution while requiring stronger:

* audit,
* attribution,
* notification,
* evidence,
* and retrospective review.

---

## REQ-GOV-082 — Break-glass authority SHOULD be temporary and narrowly scoped

Emergency authority SHOULD generally be bounded by:

* duration,
* action,
* target,
* and environment.

---

# Governance provenance

## REQ-GOV-083 — Governance decisions MUST preserve provenance

For consequential decisions, Monad SHOULD be able to answer:

* which rules were evaluated,
* which versions,
* which authority records,
* which inputs,
* which evidence,
* and which evaluator produced the result.

---

## REQ-GOV-084 — Governance provenance MUST remain distinct from governance authority

Knowing who evaluated a policy does not itself establish that the evaluator was authorized to make the policy authoritative.

---

# Governance history

## REQ-GOV-085 — Historical governance state MUST remain inspectable

Monad SHOULD preserve enough historical state to determine why an operation was considered legitimate at the time.

---

## REQ-GOV-086 — Current governance MUST remain distinguishable from historical governance

A rule that once permitted an operation MAY no longer permit it.

Historical records SHOULD not be reinterpreted using current rules unless explicitly performing retrospective analysis.

---

# Governance revisions

## REQ-GOV-087 — Governed decisions SHOULD retain policy and authority revisions

A consequential authorization SHOULD identify the rule versions under which it was made.

---

## REQ-GOV-088 — Governance state changes SHOULD invalidate incompatible cached decisions

Changes to:

* policy,
* authority,
* Constitution,
* target revision,
* lifecycle,
* or environment

MAY require re-evaluation.

---

# Governance observability

## REQ-GOV-089 — Governance events SHOULD be inspectable

Useful governance events may include:

* policy proposed,
* policy activated,
* approval granted,
* approval revoked,
* authority delegated,
* authority revoked,
* exception granted,
* obligation created,
* obligation satisfied,
* authorization denied,
* emergency path invoked.

---

## REQ-GOV-090 — Governance records SHOULD avoid unnecessary sensitive data

Explainability and audit SHOULD NOT require indiscriminate logging of:

* secrets,
* credentials,
* private payloads,
* or unrelated personal data.

---

# Governance integrity

## REQ-GOV-091 — Governance state SHOULD be protected proportionally to consequence

Rules governing production, authority, identity, security, or constitutional state SHOULD receive stronger integrity controls than ordinary low-risk content.

---

## REQ-GOV-092 — Unauthorized governance modification MUST NOT silently become accepted governance

If a user modifies a policy file without sufficient authority, Monad SHOULD distinguish:

```text
file changed
```

from:

```text
policy legitimately activated
```

---

# Governance portability

## REQ-GOV-093 — Governance semantics SHOULD remain portable across native platforms

Core governance concepts SHOULD NOT depend exclusively on:

* GitHub,
* GitLab,
* one cloud provider,
* one CI system,
* or one AI provider.

---

## REQ-GOV-094 — Enforcement bindings MAY vary by platform

A semantic rule such as:

```text
production release requires two authorized approvals
```

MAY be enforced differently across native systems while preserving the same underlying governance meaning.

---

# Local-first governance

## REQ-GOV-095 — Core governance rules SHOULD be locally inspectable

A developer SHOULD be able to understand relevant project governance without continuous access to a hosted governance service.

---

## REQ-GOV-096 — Core governance evaluation SHOULD be locally possible where practical

Monad SHOULD preserve local-first operation for governance that can be evaluated from locally available authoritative state.

---

## REQ-GOV-097 — Remote unavailability MUST NOT silently disable governance

If a remote dependency needed for authorization is unavailable, the system SHOULD follow an explicit offline behavior rather than assuming permission.

---

# Governance validation

## REQ-GOV-098 — Governance rules SHOULD be testable

Policies and governance rules SHOULD support:

* positive cases,
* negative cases,
* boundary cases,
* conflict cases,
* stale approval cases,
* exception cases,
* and unauthorized escalation cases.

---

## REQ-GOV-099 — Governance tests SHOULD include counterexamples

A rule SHOULD demonstrate not only when action is permitted, but when superficially similar action must be rejected.

---

## REQ-GOV-100 — Policy changes SHOULD run affected governance tests

Where policy impact can be determined, changes SHOULD trigger relevant validation before activation.

---

# Governance simulation

## REQ-GOV-101 — Proposed governance changes SHOULD be simulatable where useful

Monad MAY support evaluating:

```text
old policy
```

versus:

```text
proposed policy
```

against representative operations to identify changes in outcome.

---

## REQ-GOV-102 — Simulation MUST NOT itself activate governance

A simulated policy result MUST remain distinct from active authoritative governance.

---

# Governance reconciliation

## REQ-GOV-103 — Governance drift SHOULD be detectable

Monad SHOULD be able to identify divergence between intended governance and observed native enforcement where integrations support that comparison.

---

## REQ-GOV-104 — Governance drift MUST NOT be silently normalized

If native state contradicts expected governance, reconciliation SHOULD identify:

* authoritative source,
* mismatch,
* remediation options,
* and any required approval.

---

A conceptual governance evaluation may produce:

```text

Deny
Conditional
Unresolved

```

This is illustrative.

The exact result model belongs in the governance specification.

A conditional result may require:

```text
approval
evidence
verification
obligation
scope reduction
state correction
```

before execution may proceed.

```text

authority

```

# Illustrative release case

Suppose an AI agent requests:

```text
publish release v1.4.0 to production
```

Governance might require:

```text
actor capability:
  release.publish

actor authority:
  release.execute

release candidate:
  immutable revision

verification:
  required suite passed

approval:
  release authority

security:
  required checks passed

policy:
  active production release policy
```

The result might be:

```text
authorized
obligations:
  - record release evidence
  - reconcile deployment state
```

Only after that evaluation does execution begin.

---

# Illustrative denial case

Suppose the same AI actor has:

```text
capability:
  repository.write

authority:
  documentation.propose
```

and attempts:

```text
change production deployment policy
```

Expected governance result:

```text
decision: denied
reason:
  authority outside scope
```

The fact that the repository token permits the write is irrelevant to the semantic governance decision.

---

# Illustrative conditional case

An operation may be valid only after review:

```text
decision: conditional

requirements:
  - security approval
  - migration verification
  - current production backup evidence
```

Once those conditions are satisfied, governance can be re-evaluated.

# Governance anti-patterns

## Governance as universal bureaucracy

Every edit does not need an approval ceremony.

---

## Governance as native permissions

Filesystem and platform permissions are enforcement mechanisms, not the complete governance model.

---

## Hidden hard-coded policy

Governance rules scattered through engine conditionals become difficult to inspect, test, and evolve.

---

## Capability equals authority

A tool being available does not mean its use is legitimate.

---

## Human equals trusted

Humans may also act outside authority.

---

## AI equals untrusted

AI should not be excluded from authority categorically.

It should receive bounded authority appropriate to risk and context.

---

## Approval detached from revision

A generic:

```text
looks good
```

should not authorize arbitrary later changes.

---

## Silent policy override

More specific rules MUST NOT silently weaken higher-order constraints.

---

## Permanent exception

A temporary bypass should not become ordinary governance.

---

## Stale authorization

A governance result must not outlive the state assumptions that justified it.

---

## Opaque denial

A system that says only:

```text
not allowed
```

without explaining why becomes difficult to trust or repair.

## Governance as truth engine

Governance decides legitimacy under rules.

It does not establish objective truth.

# Progressive formalization

Governance MAY mature incrementally.

## Stage 1 — Human conventions

Document major responsibilities and decision rights.

---

## Stage 2 — Explicit authority

Represent who may approve or establish consequential state.

---

## Stage 3 — Policy capture

Express repeated governance rules explicitly.

---

## Stage 4 — Structured approvals and evidence

Bind approvals and evidence to concrete revisions.

---

## Stage 5 — Automated evaluation

Evaluate policy deterministically before consequential actions.

---

## Stage 6 — Integrated enforcement

Coordinate Monad governance with native tools.

---

## Stage 7 — Continuous governance

Support:

* drift detection,
* stale approval invalidation,
* obligation tracking,
* policy simulation,
* and governed human/AI autonomy.

---

# Minimal useful governance system

A useful first governance layer does not need to become an enterprise policy platform.

It may begin with:

```text
Actor
Authority
Action
Target
Policy
Approval
Evidence
Decision
```

and a narrow deterministic evaluator.

The system should grow only where governance complexity is justified by actual engineering risk.

---

# Falsifiability

Monad's governance layer must justify itself.

If a project can achieve sufficient safety and traceability through:

* Git,
* code review,
* branch protection,
* CI,
* native deployment controls,
* and simple documented ownership,

Monad SHOULD use those mechanisms rather than recreating them.

A richer governance model earns its cost when the engineering system needs capabilities such as:

* cross-tool authority,
* property-level authority,
* semantic policy scope,
* governed AI autonomy,
* revision-aware approvals,
* obligations,
* explainable authorization,
* exception management,
* historical governance,
* or reconciliation between native and semantic controls.

If those needs never materialize, the simpler system may be correct.

# Governance review checklist

For any proposed governance rule, ask:

1. What consequential behavior is being governed?
2. Why does it need governance?
3. Which scope does the rule cover?
4. Who has authority to create the rule?
5. Which actors are affected?
6. Which capabilities are relevant?
7. What operation is governed?
8. Which target or resource is involved?
9. Is the default permit, deny, defer, or something else?
10. Is the default explicit?
11. Does the rule require approval?
12. Who may approve?
13. Is approval revision-aware?
14. Can approval expire?
15. Does the rule require evidence?
16. What evidence is sufficient?
17. Can that evidence become stale?
18. Does the operation create obligations?
19. How are obligations tracked?
20. Can an exception be granted?
21. Who may grant it?
22. Does it expire?
23. Is there a break-glass path?
24. How is that path audited?
25. Can rules conflict?
26. How are conflicts resolved?
27. What happens if the evaluator is unavailable?
28. What happens if authority is unknown?
29. Does native permission differ from semantic authority?
30. Can the rule be tested?
31. Can a proposed revision be simulated?
32. Can an AI actor modify the rule governing itself?
33. Can a lower layer weaken a higher-order rule?
34. Are historical decisions reproducible?
35. Is the governance burden proportional to the consequence?

# Relationship to authority

Authority answers:

> **Who or what may legitimately decide or authorize?**

Governance answers:

> **Under which rules and conditions may the consequential action proceed?**

A useful relationship is:

```text
Authority establishes legitimate decision rights.
Policy constrains those rights in context.
Governance evaluates whether action may proceed.
```

---

# Relationship to execution

Governance occurs before and around execution.

Execution performs the actual change.

A useful conceptual chain is:

```text
Intent
  ↓
Resolved Operation
  ↓
Current State
  ↓
Governance Evaluation
  ↓
Authorization
  ↓
Execution
  ↓
Observation
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Reconciliation
```

The existence of governance MUST NOT blur the distinction between:

```text
allowed
executed
succeeded
verified
```

Each remains a separate fact.

# Governing principle

Monad governance should make it possible to determine:

> What action is being proposed?

> Who or what is proposing it?

> What can that actor technically do?

> What is that actor legitimately authorized to do?

> Which policies apply?

> Which approvals are required?

> Which evidence must exist?

> Which obligations follow?

> Are those approvals and evidence still current?

> Has the relevant state changed?

> Is an exception being used?

> Who authorized that exception?

> Can the decision be explained later?

> Does native enforcement agree with semantic governance?

> Is the governance burden justified by the consequence?

The objective is neither unrestricted automation nor process for process's sake.

It is controlled freedom:

> **Governance should constrain consequential action without turning ordinary engineering into ceremony.**
