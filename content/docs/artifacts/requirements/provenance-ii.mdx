# Provenance Requirements

Engineering systems constantly produce information.

A file is generated.

A requirement is inferred.

A test result is recorded.

An AI agent proposes a change.

A specification is derived from a decision.

A deployment modifies production.

A projection summarizes several authoritative sources.

Later, someone asks:

> **Where did this come from?**

Without provenance, that question often has to be reconstructed from:

* Git history,
* filenames,
* logs,
* CI systems,
* issue comments,
* memory,
* timestamps,
* generated metadata,
* and assumptions.

Monad should make provenance explicit wherever knowing origin materially affects:

* trust,
* interpretation,
* authority,
* reproducibility,
* traceability,
* reconciliation,
* evidence,
* debugging,
* or governance.

<Callout type="info" title="Provenance explains origin, not truth">
A perfectly preserved provenance chain can lead to an incorrect claim. Provenance tells us where something came from and how it was produced; it does not automatically establish correctness, authority, or evidentiary strength.
</Callout>

---

# Requirement family scope

The `REQ-PROV-*` family governs provenance for:

* Subjects,
* Representations,
* Claims,
* Evidence,
* observations,
* inferences,
* generated artifacts,
* projections,
* transformations,
* execution,
* tool calls,
* AI outputs,
* native-system observations,
* imported records,
* releases,
* verification results,
* decisions,
* and reconciliation.

The objective is not universal surveillance.

Monad SHOULD preserve provenance where origin materially affects the meaning or reliability of engineering state.

---

# Foundational provenance requirements

## REQ-PROV-001 — Monad MUST preserve meaningful origin

Where origin affects interpretation, trust, authority, reproducibility, or traceability, Monad MUST be capable of recording where information or state came from.

---

## REQ-PROV-002 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from content

Two records MAY contain identical content while having different provenance.

For example:

```text
Claim A:
  generated independently by Actor X

Claim B:
  copied from Claim A by Actor Y
```

Content equality does not imply provenance equality.

---

## REQ-PROV-003 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from authority

A record originating from an authoritative actor MAY be authoritative within scope.

A record originating from a non-authoritative actor MAY still be useful.

Monad MUST NOT infer authority merely from provenance.

---

## REQ-PROV-004 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from evidence

A provenance record may describe how Evidence was obtained.

It is not automatically Evidence for the underlying Claim.

---

## REQ-PROV-005 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from identity

Knowing where a Subject or Representation came from MUST NOT define its semantic identity.

---

# Provenance model

A useful conceptual distinction is:

```text
Subject
  │
  ├── represented-by ──→ Representation
  │
  ├── described-by ────→ Claim
  │
  └── supported-by ────→ Evidence

Provenance
  │
  ├── records producer
  ├── records source
  ├── records derivation
  ├── records observation
  ├── records transformation
  └── records execution context
```

Provenance may apply to many kinds of engineering records.

It SHOULD NOT require every provenance-bearing object to use exactly the same physical schema.

---

# Producer provenance

## REQ-PROV-006 — Monad SHOULD identify the producer of consequential records

Where meaningful, provenance SHOULD identify the Actor or system that produced a record.

A producer may be:

* a human,
* an AI agent,
* a compiler,
* a test runner,
* a CI system,
* an integration,
* a script,
* an external service,
* or another engineering system.

---

## REQ-PROV-007 — Producer identity SHOULD be stable enough for later interpretation

Provenance SHOULD avoid relying solely on transient labels such as:

```text
worker-17
```

when a more durable Actor or system identity is available.

---

## REQ-PROV-008 — Producer MUST NOT be conflated with author, owner, subject, or authority

These may be different.

For example:

```text
producer: build-system
author: developer
subject: release-artifact
authority: release-manager
```

---

# Source provenance

## REQ-PROV-009 — Derived information SHOULD identify its source material

If an artifact, Claim, summary, projection, or inference was produced from prior information, Monad SHOULD be capable of identifying the relevant sources.

---

## REQ-PROV-010 — Source provenance SHOULD preserve semantic source identity where possible

A source SHOULD be referenced using durable identity rather than only its current file location.

---

## REQ-PROV-011 — Native source references SHOULD remain available

Where a source originates in a native system, provenance SHOULD preserve useful native references such as:

* repository,
* commit,
* file path,
* issue,
* pull request,
* workflow run,
* deployment,
* external record,
* or API object.

These native references SHOULD remain distinguishable from Monad semantic identity.

---

# Requested and resolved origin

## REQ-PROV-012 — Requested origin SHOULD be distinguishable from resolved origin where resolution occurs

An actor may request:

```text
latest approved specification
```

and the system may resolve that request to:

```text
SPEC-014 revision 7
```

Monad SHOULD be capable of recording both when the distinction matters.

---

## REQ-PROV-013 — Provenance SHOULD preserve resolution decisions that materially affect output

If a result depends on:

* alias resolution,
* version selection,
* fallback behavior,
* repository discovery,
* policy resolution,
* dependency selection,
* or environment selection,

the resolved origin SHOULD be inspectable.

---

## REQ-PROV-014 — Ambiguous resolution SHOULD NOT be hidden

If multiple candidate origins existed, provenance SHOULD preserve enough context to explain why one was selected where that choice materially affects interpretation.

---

# Derivation provenance

## REQ-PROV-015 — Derived state MUST remain distinguishable from directly observed or authored state

Monad SHOULD preserve distinctions such as:

```text
authored
observed
imported
generated
derived
inferred
normalized
projected
```

where these distinctions affect interpretation.

---

## REQ-PROV-016 — Derived records SHOULD identify their derivation inputs

A generated artifact SHOULD be traceable to the inputs used to generate it where practical.

---

## REQ-PROV-017 — Multi-step derivations SHOULD preserve a derivation chain

For example:

```text
Requirement
  ↓
Normalized Requirement
  ↓
Index Entry
  ↓
Search Projection
  ↓
AI Context Bundle
```

The final context bundle SHOULD remain traceable to the relevant upstream material.

---

## REQ-PROV-018 — Derivation provenance SHOULD distinguish direct and transitive sources

The immediate source of a result may differ from its deeper lineage.

Both may matter.

---

# Transformation provenance

## REQ-PROV-019 — Meaningful transformations SHOULD be identifiable

Where information is changed through:

* normalization,
* conversion,
* summarization,
* migration,
* translation,
* redaction,
* aggregation,
* code generation,
* or projection,

Monad SHOULD record the transformation where it affects interpretation or reproducibility.

---

## REQ-PROV-020 — Transformations SHOULD identify the mechanism used

Where practical, provenance SHOULD identify:

* tool,
* implementation,
* version,
* configuration,
* model,
* prompt,
* migration,
* or algorithm

used to perform the transformation.

---

## REQ-PROV-021 — Lossy transformations MUST be distinguishable from lossless transformations where meaningful

A summary is not equivalent to the original source.

A redacted record is not equivalent to the unredacted record.

A projection may intentionally omit information.

Monad SHOULD preserve that fact.

---

# Observation provenance

## REQ-PROV-022 — Observed state SHOULD identify the observation source

An observation SHOULD be capable of recording where the observed state came from.

Examples:

* filesystem,
* Git repository,
* GitHub API,
* database,
* deployment platform,
* test runner,
* process state,
* user input,
* telemetry system.

---

## REQ-PROV-023 — Observation time SHOULD be recorded where state can change

For mutable state, provenance SHOULD distinguish when the state was observed.

---

## REQ-PROV-024 — Observed state SHOULD remain distinguishable from recorded claims about that state

For example:

```text
observed:
  branch protection enabled

recorded claim:
  production branches are protected
```

The second may be derived from the first, but they are not identical records.

---

## REQ-PROV-025 — An observation MUST NOT silently become current truth indefinitely

Mutable observations MAY become stale.

The provenance model SHOULD support freshness reasoning.

---

# Temporal provenance

## REQ-PROV-026 — Monad SHOULD distinguish relevant forms of time

Where required, provenance SHOULD distinguish:

```text
observed_at
recorded_at
produced_at
executed_at
valid_from
valid_until
```

The exact temporal model belongs in the relevant specifications.

---

## REQ-PROV-027 — Recorded time MUST NOT be assumed to equal valid time

A fact may be recorded after it became true.

A historical fact may be entered long after the event.

---

## REQ-PROV-028 — Historical provenance MUST remain interpretable using historical context

When inspecting past state, Monad SHOULD preserve enough context to understand:

* the source,
* producer,
* relevant revision,
* and environment

that existed at the time.

---

# Revision provenance

## REQ-PROV-029 — Provenance SHOULD bind consequential records to relevant revisions

A test result MAY apply to:

```text
commit abc123
```

but not:

```text
commit def456
```

Monad SHOULD preserve that relationship.

---

## REQ-PROV-030 — Revision references SHOULD be immutable where the native system supports immutable identifiers

Durable provenance SHOULD prefer:

* commit hashes,
* content digests,
* immutable release identifiers,
* immutable artifact versions,

over moving references such as:

```text
main
latest
current
```

when long-term interpretation matters.

---

## REQ-PROV-031 — Moving references MAY be recorded in addition to resolved immutable revisions

For example:

```text
requested_ref: main
resolved_commit: abc123
```

This preserves both user intent and actual source.

---

# Execution provenance

## REQ-PROV-032 — Consequential execution SHOULD preserve provenance

Monad SHOULD be able to answer:

* who requested the action,
* who or what executed it,
* what operation was performed,
* against which target,
* with which resolved inputs,
* under which authorization,
* using which tool,
* and what result was observed.

---

## REQ-PROV-033 — Requester and executor MUST remain distinguishable

An AI agent may request an operation that is executed by:

* a local daemon,
* CI runner,
* GitHub,
* Kubernetes,
* a deployment platform,
* or another service.

Those are different provenance roles.

---

## REQ-PROV-034 — Execution location SHOULD be captured where operationally relevant

Relevant location may include:

* developer workstation,
* CI runner,
* isolated sandbox,
* remote build environment,
* production environment,
* edge environment.

---

## REQ-PROV-035 — Execution environment SHOULD be identifiable where it affects reproducibility or interpretation

This may include:

* operating system,
* architecture,
* runtime,
* dependency versions,
* container image,
* environment profile,
* relevant configuration.

---

## REQ-PROV-036 — Execution provenance SHOULD preserve the resolved operation

High-level intent alone MAY be insufficient.

For consequential actions, Monad SHOULD be capable of recording the concrete operation ultimately executed.

---

# Tool provenance

## REQ-PROV-037 — Tool identity SHOULD be preserved for consequential tool-produced results

Where a result depends materially on a tool, provenance SHOULD identify that tool.

---

## REQ-PROV-038 — Tool version SHOULD be recorded where behavior may vary by version

This is especially important for:

* compilers,
* schema validators,
* migration tools,
* test frameworks,
* generators,
* AI models,
* package managers,
* build systems,
* and deployment clients.

---

## REQ-PROV-039 — Tool output MUST NOT be assumed correct merely because the tool is trusted

Tool provenance establishes origin, not correctness.

---

# AI provenance

## REQ-PROV-040 — Consequential AI-generated content SHOULD preserve AI provenance

Where appropriate, Monad SHOULD be able to identify:

* AI Actor,
* provider,
* model,
* model version or identifier,
* relevant prompt or instruction context,
* source context,
* tool usage,
* and generation time.

---

## REQ-PROV-041 — AI provenance SHOULD preserve source context where the output depends on retrieved material

If an AI-generated artifact was based on:

* Requirements,
* ADRs,
* source code,
* test results,
* documentation,
* web material,
* or native-system state,

those sources SHOULD remain traceable where practical.

---

## REQ-PROV-042 — AI output MUST remain distinguishable from source evidence

An AI summary of Evidence is a derived representation.

It is not the original Evidence.

---

## REQ-PROV-043 — AI inference MUST remain distinguishable from observation

An AI may infer:

```text
this component appears unused
```

from repository analysis.

That inference SHOULD NOT be recorded as though the system directly observed:

```text
this component is unused
```

---

## REQ-PROV-044 — AI-generated citations SHOULD resolve to actual sources

Where an AI output cites supporting material, Monad SHOULD preserve resolvable source references.

---

## REQ-PROV-045 — Missing AI provenance SHOULD limit trust where provenance is necessary for evaluation

A consequential AI result whose source context cannot be reconstructed MAY need:

* revalidation,
* human review,
* or regeneration.

---

# Human provenance

## REQ-PROV-046 — Human-authored consequential records SHOULD preserve authorship where relevant

This may include:

* Requirements,
* Decisions,
* approvals,
* reviews,
* exceptions,
* and policy changes.

---

## REQ-PROV-047 — Human authorship MUST remain distinct from authority

A person may write a document without possessing authority to approve or activate it.

---

## REQ-PROV-048 — Collaborative authorship SHOULD support multiple contributors where meaningful

Monad SHOULD avoid forcing complex collaborative work into a false single-author provenance model.

---

# Generated artifacts

## REQ-PROV-049 — Generated artifacts MUST remain distinguishable from authored artifacts

A generated representation SHOULD identify that it was generated where that distinction matters.

---

## REQ-PROV-050 — Generated artifacts SHOULD identify their generator

The provenance SHOULD be capable of identifying:

* generator,
* version,
* relevant inputs,
* configuration,
* and generation time.

---

## REQ-PROV-051 — Regenerated output SHOULD preserve lineage to the logical artifact where appropriate

Regeneration SHOULD NOT accidentally create a new semantic Subject merely because bytes or file location changed.

---

## REQ-PROV-052 — Generated status MUST NOT determine authority

Generated artifacts MAY be authoritative.

Authored artifacts MAY be non-authoritative.

Generation provenance and authority remain separate concerns.

---

# Projections and read models

## REQ-PROV-053 — Derived projections SHOULD be traceable to canonical inputs

A projection SHOULD be capable of identifying the state from which it was produced.

---

## REQ-PROV-054 — Projection provenance SHOULD support freshness evaluation

Monad SHOULD be capable of determining whether a projection was built from current or stale source state.

---

## REQ-PROV-055 — Projection regeneration SHOULD be reproducible where practical

Given equivalent:

* source revisions,
* transformation logic,
* configuration,
* and environment,

a deterministic projection SHOULD produce equivalent output.

---

## REQ-PROV-056 — A projection MUST NOT erase contradictory source provenance

If upstream authoritative claims disagree, a derived view SHOULD NOT silently flatten them into one apparently certain fact.

---

# Imported data

## REQ-PROV-057 — Imported records SHOULD identify their external origin

When information enters Monad from another system, provenance SHOULD preserve:

* source system,
* native identifier,
* observation or import time,
* and transformation performed.

---

## REQ-PROV-058 — Import SHOULD NOT imply adoption of external authority

Imported information may remain:

* external,
* advisory,
* observed,
* inferred,
* or pending confirmation.

---

## REQ-PROV-059 — Import transformations SHOULD be traceable

Normalization during import SHOULD preserve enough information to understand the relationship between original and normalized forms.

---

# Native systems

## REQ-PROV-060 — Native provenance SHOULD be preserved rather than replaced

If Git already provides:

* commit author,
* commit hash,
* parent history,
* timestamp,
* or repository identity,

Monad SHOULD reference or integrate that provenance rather than duplicating it unnecessarily.

---

## REQ-PROV-061 — Monad provenance MAY add semantic context around native provenance

For example:

```text
Git commit abc123
  ↓ realizes
Work Package WP-004
  ↓ satisfies
Requirement REQ-ID-001
```

Monad adds relationships and semantic context without pretending to own Git's native history.

---

## REQ-PROV-062 — Native history MUST NOT automatically define semantic history

A file rename recorded by Git MAY represent:

* no semantic change,
* one Subject moving,
* a split,
* a merge,
* or a replacement.

Semantic interpretation requires explicit modeling beyond native history.

---

# Provenance and authority

## REQ-PROV-063 — Provenance SHOULD support authority evaluation

Authority rules MAY use provenance to answer questions such as:

* Who produced this Claim?
* Which system generated this artifact?
* Was this Evidence produced by an approved verifier?
* Was this approval recorded by an authorized Actor?

---

## REQ-PROV-064 — Provenance MUST NOT itself grant authority

A record from:

```text
security-team@example
```

does not automatically become authoritative merely because the source label appears trustworthy.

Authority must be established separately.

---

# Provenance and evidence

## REQ-PROV-065 — Evidence SHOULD preserve sufficient provenance to evaluate relevance and validity

Useful evidence provenance may include:

* producer,
* observation method,
* tool,
* revision,
* environment,
* source,
* and time.

---

## REQ-PROV-066 — Evidence derived from other Evidence SHOULD preserve derivation

A summarized report SHOULD remain traceable to its underlying observations and results where those are retained.

---

## REQ-PROV-067 — Provenance SHOULD support identification of non-independent Evidence

Ten reports copied from one original source do not provide ten independent observations.

Provenance SHOULD make such relationships discoverable.

---

# Provenance and claims

## REQ-PROV-068 — Claims SHOULD identify their origin where material

A Claim may be:

* authored,
* observed,
* imported,
* inferred,
* generated,
* derived,
* or reconstructed.

That origin can materially affect interpretation.

---

## REQ-PROV-069 — Claim correction SHOULD preserve prior provenance

Correcting a Claim SHOULD NOT erase:

* who made the original Claim,
* its source,
* or the reason it was superseded.

---

## REQ-PROV-070 — Conflicting Claims SHOULD preserve independent provenance

When two sources disagree, Monad SHOULD NOT merge the Claims merely because they concern the same property.

---

# Provenance and decisions

## REQ-PROV-071 — Decisions SHOULD preserve relevant input provenance

Where a decision materially depends on:

* experiments,
* benchmarks,
* stakeholder input,
* Requirements,
* incidents,
* or prior architecture,

those inputs SHOULD remain traceable.

---

## REQ-PROV-072 — Decision provenance MUST NOT replace rationale

Knowing which meeting or person produced a decision does not explain why the decision was made.

---

# Provenance and requirements

## REQ-PROV-073 — Requirements SHOULD preserve source rationale where meaningful

A Requirement may originate from:

* user need,
* regulation,
* architecture constraint,
* operational experience,
* security concern,
* experiment,
* business objective,
* or another Requirement.

That origin SHOULD remain inspectable when consequential.

---

## REQ-PROV-074 — Derived Requirements SHOULD identify upstream obligations

If one Requirement is introduced to satisfy another, that relationship SHOULD be traceable.

---

# Provenance and specifications

## REQ-PROV-075 — Specifications SHOULD preserve derivation from Requirements and Decisions

A normative specification clause SHOULD be traceable to the obligations or architectural decisions that justify it where practical.

---

## REQ-PROV-076 — Generated specifications SHOULD remain distinguishable from approved specifications

Generation provenance does not imply specification authority.

---

# Provenance and verification

## REQ-PROV-077 — Verification results SHOULD preserve execution provenance

A verification result SHOULD identify enough context to determine:

* what was verified,
* against which revision,
* using which verification procedure,
* in which environment,
* and with which result.

---

## REQ-PROV-078 — Re-running verification SHOULD create new provenance rather than silently replacing history

A new test run is a new observation, even when its result is identical.

---

# Provenance and releases

## REQ-PROV-079 — Release artifacts SHOULD be traceable to their build inputs

Where practical, a release SHOULD identify:

* source revision,
* build process,
* dependencies,
* environment,
* artifact digest,
* verification results,
* and authorization.

---

## REQ-PROV-080 — Release provenance SHOULD support reproducibility investigations

Monad SHOULD preserve enough provenance to answer:

> **What produced this exact release artifact?**

---

# Provenance and reconciliation

## REQ-PROV-081 — Reconciliation SHOULD preserve the origin of conflicting state

If Monad and a native system disagree, the system SHOULD be able to identify:

* what each side reported,
* where each value came from,
* when each was observed,
* and which authority rules apply.

---

## REQ-PROV-082 — Reconciliation MUST NOT erase the losing observation

Resolving a conflict SHOULD preserve historical provenance of the displaced or rejected state where consequential.

---

# Corrections

## REQ-PROV-083 — Provenance records SHOULD support correction without silent rewriting

If provenance was recorded incorrectly, correction SHOULD preserve:

* the original record where necessary,
* corrected value,
* reason,
* correcting actor,
* and correction time.

---

## REQ-PROV-084 — Correction SHOULD be distinguishable from source mutation

Changing the metadata about a historical event is different from changing the event itself.

---

# Provenance integrity

## REQ-PROV-085 — Consequential provenance SHOULD be protected against silent tampering

The required protection MAY vary by consequence.

Possible mechanisms include:

* repository history,
* immutable logs,
* content digests,
* signed records,
* append-only storage,
* attestations.

No single mechanism is universally required.

---

## REQ-PROV-086 — Content digest MAY establish content integrity but MUST NOT be treated as proof of truth

A hash can show that bytes have not changed relative to a known digest.

It does not show that those bytes are correct.

---

## REQ-PROV-087 — Signatures MUST NOT be treated as proof of correctness

A signature may establish:

```text
Actor X signed record Y
```

It does not establish:

```text
record Y is true
```

---

# Provenance completeness

## REQ-PROV-088 — Provenance requirements SHOULD be proportional to consequence

Monad MUST NOT require exhaustive provenance for every transient value.

---

## REQ-PROV-089 — Missing provenance SHOULD be represented explicitly where consequential

The system SHOULD be able to distinguish:

```text
source known
```

from:

```text
source unknown
```

rather than inventing provenance.

---

## REQ-PROV-090 — Unknown provenance MUST NOT be treated as false provenance

If origin is unavailable, the system SHOULD preserve uncertainty.

---

# Provenance granularity

## REQ-PROV-091 — Provenance SHOULD be captured at the smallest useful semantic scope

Document-level provenance may be sufficient for one artifact.

Claim-level provenance may be required for another.

Monad SHOULD avoid both:

* universal coarse provenance,
* and unnecessary property-level provenance everywhere.

---

## REQ-PROV-092 — Mixed-provenance artifacts SHOULD support finer-grained attribution where needed

A document may contain:

* human-authored sections,
* generated sections,
* imported facts,
* and AI summaries.

A single document-level producer MAY be insufficient.

---

# Provenance querying

## REQ-PROV-093 — Monad SHOULD support querying provenance relationships

Useful questions include:

* Where did this come from?
* Who produced it?
* What source material contributed to it?
* Which tool transformed it?
* Which revision did it apply to?
* Was it observed or inferred?
* Which artifacts were derived from this one?
* Which current Claims depend on this source?
* Which release includes this artifact?
* Which AI outputs used this source?
* Which Evidence shares the same origin?
* Is this information independently corroborated?

---

## REQ-PROV-094 — Provenance queries SHOULD support both upstream and downstream traversal

Upstream:

```text
Where did this artifact come from?
```

Downstream:

```text
What depends on this source?
```

Both are important.

---

# Provenance graph

## REQ-PROV-095 — Provenance relationships SHOULD be typed

Monad SHOULD distinguish relationships such as:

```text
generated-from
derived-from
observed-from
imported-from
normalized-from
summarized-from
verified-by
executed-by
requested-by
produced-by
```

rather than using one ambiguous generic provenance edge.

---

## REQ-PROV-096 — Provenance graphs SHOULD avoid meaningless edge proliferation

A relationship SHOULD exist because it materially helps explain origin or derivation.

Monad SHOULD NOT connect everything merely because a technical dependency can be inferred.

---

# Provenance portability

## REQ-PROV-097 — Core provenance SHOULD be representable without dependence on a single SaaS platform

A project's meaningful engineering history SHOULD remain inspectable even if one remote provider becomes unavailable.

---

## REQ-PROV-098 — Native references MAY remain provider-specific

Portability does not require pretending:

```text
GitHub Pull Request #42
```

is a provider-neutral object.

Monad SHOULD preserve the native identity while attaching portable semantic relationships around it.

---

# Local-first provenance

## REQ-PROV-099 — Critical provenance SHOULD be locally inspectable where practical

Core engineering reasoning SHOULD NOT become impossible to inspect solely because a remote service is unavailable.

---

## REQ-PROV-100 — Remote-only provenance SHOULD be identifiable

If essential detail exists only in an external system, Monad SHOULD be able to indicate that dependency.

---

# Provenance retention

## REQ-PROV-101 — Provenance retention SHOULD be proportional to long-term value

Monad SHOULD retain provenance that materially supports:

* audit,
* reproducibility,
* verification,
* authority,
* traceability,
* debugging,
* release analysis,
* or historical understanding.

---

## REQ-PROV-102 — Monad MUST NOT require indefinite retention of every transient execution detail

Low-value ephemeral provenance MAY expire according to policy.

---

## REQ-PROV-103 — Provenance deletion MAY itself require governance

Deleting high-value provenance MAY affect:

* auditability,
* evidence,
* compliance,
* release history,
* or incident reconstruction.

Such deletion SHOULD be governed proportionally.

---

# Sensitive provenance

## REQ-PROV-104 — Provenance SHOULD minimize unnecessary secret capture

Provenance MUST NOT indiscriminately record:

* access tokens,
* credentials,
* private keys,
* passwords,
* sensitive environment variables,
* or confidential payloads.

---

## REQ-PROV-105 — Sensitive provenance SHOULD support appropriate access controls

The ability to inspect provenance does not imply universal access to every provenance payload.

---

## REQ-PROV-106 — Redacted provenance SHOULD disclose that redaction occurred where meaningful

A sanitized representation SHOULD not masquerade as the complete source record.

---

# Reproducibility

## REQ-PROV-107 — Reproducible operations SHOULD preserve enough provenance to reproduce them

Depending on the operation, that MAY include:

* source revision,
* dependency versions,
* tool versions,
* configuration,
* environment,
* seed,
* command,
* inputs,
* AI model,
* prompt,
* and relevant external state.

---

## REQ-PROV-108 — Reproducibility requirements SHOULD reflect determinism

Some operations are deterministic.

Others, particularly AI operations, may not be.

Monad SHOULD preserve enough provenance to distinguish:

```text
exact reproducibility
```

from:

```text
approximate or statistical reproducibility
```

---

# Provenance validation

## REQ-PROV-109 — Provenance references SHOULD be validated where possible

The system SHOULD detect:

* missing referenced sources,
* malformed identifiers,
* impossible revision references,
* broken derivation chains,
* invalid Actor identities,
* and incompatible provenance relationships.

---

## REQ-PROV-110 — Valid provenance structure MUST NOT imply provenance truth

A syntactically valid record can still contain incorrect claims about origin.

---

# Provenance contradictions

## REQ-PROV-111 — Conflicting provenance SHOULD remain explicit

If two systems report different origins, Monad SHOULD preserve the disagreement until resolved.

---

## REQ-PROV-112 — Reconciliation SHOULD NOT invent a single provenance history solely for convenience

Ambiguity MAY be a legitimate current state.

---

# Historical reconstruction

## REQ-PROV-113 — Monad SHOULD support reconstruction of consequential engineering history

Where sufficient provenance exists, users SHOULD be able to answer questions such as:

> Why does this artifact exist?

> What created it?

> Which source revision produced it?

> Which decision led to it?

> Which actor approved it?

> Which verification established confidence in it?

> Which release included it?

---

## REQ-PROV-114 — Historical reconstruction MUST distinguish recorded history from inferred reconstruction

If Monad infers missing history after the fact, that reconstruction MUST remain distinguishable from originally recorded provenance.

---

# Retrospective provenance

## REQ-PROV-115 — Retrospective provenance MAY be added when historical evidence supports it

Legacy systems may lack explicit provenance.

Monad MAY reconstruct relationships from:

* Git history,
* issues,
* artifacts,
* logs,
* release records,
* or human review.

---

## REQ-PROV-116 — Retrospectively inferred provenance MUST remain marked as inferred until sufficiently confirmed

Inferred history SHOULD NOT silently become equivalent to directly recorded historical fact.

---

# Provenance confidence

## REQ-PROV-117 — Monad SHOULD NOT require a universal numeric provenance-confidence score

Confidence may depend on:

* source quality,
* directness,
* method,
* corroboration,
* integrity,
* and reconstruction method.

One universal scalar risks false precision.

---

## REQ-PROV-118 — Provenance certainty MAY be expressed qualitatively where useful

Examples may include:

```text
recorded
observed
verified
inferred
reconstructed
disputed
unknown
```

The exact vocabulary belongs in the domain model or specification.

A conceptual minimal provenance record may contain:

```yaml

  revision: abc123

  tool: verifier
  version: 0.8.0
```

This is illustrative only.

Different provenance domains will require different structures.

# Rich derivation example

A more complex chain might resemble:

```text
REQ-ID-001
  ↓ source-for
SPEC-IDENTITY-003
  ↓ input-to

  ↓ generated

  ↓ validated-by
```

The value lies not in having many edges.

The value lies in being able to reconstruct the meaningful path.

# AI derivation example

Suppose an AI agent produces an architectural recommendation.

Useful provenance might establish:

```text
producer:
  engineering-agent

  model-version-X

  - REQ-ID-001
  - ADR-014
  - repository revision abc123
  - EXP-021 results

  - dependency-analyzer

```

The proposal remains a proposal.

Its provenance does not grant it authority.

# Provenance anti-patterns

## Path equals provenance

A filepath tells us where something currently resides.

It does not explain where the semantic content originated.

---

## Git history equals complete provenance

Git is valuable native provenance.

It does not automatically capture:

* semantic derivation,
* authority,
* AI context,
* external observations,
* lifecycle reasoning,
* or execution semantics.

---

## Author equals source

An author may have synthesized several sources.

Those distinctions should remain available where consequential.

---

## Provenance equals trust

Knowing the origin of information does not prove the source was correct.

---

## Provenance equals authority

Knowing who created something does not prove they had authority to establish it.

---

## Provenance equals evidence

A record of origin does not automatically support the proposition being asserted.

---

## Generated equals unreliable

Generated artifacts can be trustworthy.

Their generation should simply remain explicit.

---

## Human-authored equals authoritative

Human authorship is also merely provenance.

---

## AI-generated equals false

AI generation is a provenance fact, not a truth judgment.

---

## Hash equals truth

A hash can faithfully identify false content.

---

## Signature equals correctness

A signer can sign an incorrect statement.

---

## Latest equals source revision

Moving references should be resolved where durable provenance matters.

---

## One giant provenance blob

Unstructured logs make origin harder rather than easier to reason about.

---

## Capture everything forever

Exhaustive provenance can become:

* expensive,
* invasive,
* noisy,
* and unusable.

Provenance must earn its capture and retention cost.

---

## Flattened derivation

A generated summary should not pretend to have been directly observed from every upstream source.

---

## Silent retrospective certainty

Reconstructed provenance should remain distinguishable from contemporaneously recorded provenance.

---

# Progressive formalization

Provenance can mature gradually.

## Stage 1 — Native references

Preserve:

* Git commits,
* file paths,
* issue references,
* workflow runs,
* release identifiers.

---

## Stage 2 — Producer attribution

Record who or what produced consequential artifacts and results.

---

## Stage 3 — Stable semantic relationships

Bind provenance to semantic Subjects rather than only locations.

---

## Stage 4 — Derivation chains

Represent:

```text
source → transformation → output
```

explicitly.

---

## Stage 5 — Execution provenance

Capture resolved operations, tools, environments, authorization, and results.

---

## Stage 6 — Provenance-aware evidence and verification

Use provenance to evaluate:

* relevance,
* freshness,
* reproducibility,
* independence,
* and applicability.

---

## Stage 7 — Historical and causal analysis

Enable richer reconstruction of:

* why artifacts exist,
* what decisions produced them,
* how system state evolved,
* and which downstream state depends on a source.

---

# Minimal useful provenance system

A first implementation does not need a universal provenance ledger.

A useful minimum may support:

```text
producer
source
subject
revision
time
transformation
native reference
```

for the records where those fields provide material engineering value.

The system can expand only when additional provenance demonstrably improves:

* reproducibility,
* traceability,
* governance,
* debugging,
* evidence quality,
* or AI context.

# Provenance review checklist

For any consequential record, ask:

1. What is this record about?
2. Who or what produced it?
3. Is the producer the same as the author?
4. Is the producer the same as the authority?
5. Which source material contributed to it?
6. Was the source authored, observed, imported, or inferred?
7. Which native system owns the source?
8. Which native identifier applies?
9. Which semantic Subject applies?
10. Which revision was used?
11. Was a moving reference resolved?
12. What was requested?
13. What was actually resolved?
14. Which transformation occurred?
15. Which tool performed it?
16. Which tool version?
17. Which configuration mattered?
18. Was the transformation lossy?
19. When was the source observed?
20. When was the result produced?
21. When was it recorded?
22. Which environment was used?
23. Which Actor requested the operation?
24. Which Actor or system executed it?
25. Where did execution occur?
26. Was the output generated by AI?
27. Which model and context mattered?
28. Is the result directly observed or inferred?
29. Can the derivation chain be followed?
30. Can upstream sources be inspected?
31. Can downstream dependencies be found?
32. Is the provenance complete enough for its consequence?
33. Is any provenance unknown?
34. Is any provenance disputed?
35. Was any history reconstructed after the fact?
36. Is the record sensitive?
37. Are secrets excluded?
38. Could the operation be reproduced?
39. Does the provenance support freshness evaluation?
40. Does provenance remain distinct from authority and truth?

# Relationship to semantic identity

Semantic identity answers:

> **What enduring thing is this?**

Provenance answers:

> **Where did this particular representation, Claim, observation, or state come from?**

A Subject can survive many provenance events:

```text
Subject S
  ├── authored representation A
  ├── generated representation B
  ├── imported representation C
  └── projected representation D
```

Each Representation may have different provenance while still referring to the same Subject.

---

# Relationship to Evidence

Evidence asks:

> **What inspectable support bears on this Claim?**

Provenance asks:

> **Where did that Evidence come from, how was it obtained, and under what conditions?**

A test result without sufficient provenance may be difficult to interpret.

A test result with excellent provenance may still fail to prove the intended Claim.

The distinction matters.

---

# Relationship to authority

Authority asks:

> **Who may legitimately establish this state?**

Provenance asks:

> **Who actually produced or changed this record?**

Those answers may differ.

If they do, that difference can become governance-relevant.

---

# Relationship to traceability

Traceability connects engineering intent and realization:

```text
Requirement
  ↓
Decision
  ↓
Specification
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Evidence
```

Provenance explains the origin and production history of the records participating in those relationships.

Traceability asks:

> How are these engineering concepts related?

Provenance asks:

> Where did each of them come from?

---

# Relationship to history

History is not merely a sequence of timestamps.

A useful engineering history may require:

```text
what changed
who changed it
why it changed
what source informed it
which revision existed
which transformation occurred
what authority applied
what evidence resulted
```

Provenance provides part of that foundation.

---

# Traceability expectations

Accepted provenance requirements should eventually trace into models, schemas, execution infrastructure, verification, and evidence.

Illustratively:

```text
REQ-PROV-036
  ↓ refined-by
Execution Provenance Specification
  ↓ implemented-by
Execution Recorder
  ↓ exercised-by
Governed Tool Invocation
  ↓ verified-by
Execution Provenance Conformance Test
  ↓ evidenced-by
Verification Result
```

Actual relationships should only be created when the corresponding artifacts exist.

---

# Governing principle

Monad should make it possible to ask:

> Where did this information come from?

> Who or what produced it?

> Which source revision was used?

> Was it authored, observed, imported, generated, or inferred?

> Which transformation occurred?

> Which tool performed that transformation?

> What was requested?

> What was actually resolved?

> When was the source observed?

> When was the result recorded?

> Which environment mattered?

> What downstream artifacts depend on it?

> Can the derivation chain be reconstructed?

> Can the result be reproduced?

> Is the provenance direct or reconstructed?

> Is any origin unknown or disputed?

> Does this provenance establish only origin, or is someone incorrectly treating it as truth or authority?

The objective is not to preserve every event forever.

It is to preserve enough meaningful origin that engineering knowledge does not become detached from how it came to exist.

> **A system that knows what it believes should also be able to explain where those beliefs came from.**
