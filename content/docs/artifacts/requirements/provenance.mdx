# Provenance Requirements

Engineering information rarely appears from nowhere.

A requirement may originate in a conversation, become a structured artifact, be transformed into a specification, influence implementation, produce verification results, and later appear in a generated report.

An observed fact may come from Git, GitHub, CI, a local filesystem, a deployment platform, a database, a human reviewer, or an AI system.

A generated claim may depend on several earlier observations.

A projection may itself be derived from semantic state that was derived from native systems.

Monad needs to preserve enough of that lineage to answer a deceptively simple question:

> **Where did this information come from?**

That question is different from:

> Is it true?

> Is it authoritative?

> Is it current?

> Is it relevant?

> Is it permitted?

Those concerns interact with provenance, but they are not provenance.

<Callout type="info" title="Core distinction">
Provenance explains the origin and derivation of information. It does not, by itself, establish truth, authority, permission, correctness, or trustworthiness.
</Callout>

---

# Requirement family scope

The `REQ-PROV-*` family governs provenance associated with:

* Subjects,
* Representations,
* Claims,
* Evidence,
* observations,
* inferences,
* generated artifacts,
* projections,
* imports,
* native-system references,
* transformations,
* execution,
* verification,
* reconciliation,
* migrations,
* human actions,
* AI actions,
* tool operations,
* and historical records.

It includes requirements for distinguishing:

* origin,
* producer,
* observer,
* recorder,
* transformation,
* derivation,
* source material,
* execution location,
* requested origin,
* resolved origin,
* and current storage location.

It does not independently define:

* whether information is authoritative,
* whether a Claim is true,
* whether an actor was permitted to act,
* whether evidence is sufficient,
* whether a source should be trusted,
* or whether a lifecycle transition was valid.

Those are governed by related requirement families.

# Foundational provenance requirements

## REQ-PROV-001 — Monad MUST preserve material provenance

Monad MUST preserve provenance for information where origin or derivation materially affects:

* interpretation,
* verification,
* authority resolution,
* debugging,
* reconciliation,
* reproducibility,
* auditability,
* historical reconstruction,
* AI reasoning,
* or governance.

Provenance capture SHOULD be proportional to engineering value.

Not every transient value requires a full lineage graph.

---

## REQ-PROV-002 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from content

The information:

```text
Component A depends on Component B.
```

is distinct from:

```text
This assertion was produced by dependency scanner X
from source files Y and Z
during execution E
at time T.
```

Monad MUST be able to preserve that distinction.

---

## REQ-PROV-003 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from authority

Monad MUST preserve:

```text
Provenance ≠ Authority
```

A source may be identifiable without being authoritative.

An authoritative source may also produce an incorrect or stale assertion.

The system MUST NOT infer authority solely from origin.

---

## REQ-PROV-004 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from trust

Knowing where information originated MUST NOT automatically determine whether it should be trusted.

For example:

```text
source: CI system
```

does not necessarily mean:

```text
result: correct
```

The CI configuration itself may have been defective.

---

## REQ-PROV-005 — Provenance MUST remain distinct from evidence

Provenance describes how information came to exist.

Evidence supports or challenges a proposition.

The same artifact MAY participate in both roles, but those semantic roles MUST remain distinguishable.

---

# Origin

## REQ-PROV-006 — Monad SHOULD record origin where origin affects interpretation

Where meaningful, Monad SHOULD preserve the origin from which information first entered the relevant engineering process.

Possible origins include:

* a human-authored artifact,
* native engineering system,
* imported repository,
* external service,
* experiment,
* execution,
* verification activity,
* AI generation,
* observation,
* or derived semantic process.

---

## REQ-PROV-007 — Requested origin MUST be distinguishable from resolved origin where they differ

An operation may request:

```text
repository default branch
```

while actually resolving to:

```text
commit 8f36...
```

Monad SHOULD support distinguishing:

```text
requested origin
```

from:

```text
resolved origin
```

when reproducibility or interpretation depends on the distinction.

---

## REQ-PROV-008 — Original origin SHOULD remain distinguishable from current storage location

Moving information into Monad MUST NOT rewrite its provenance as though Monad created it.

For example:

```text
origin:
  GitHub issue
```

may coexist with:

```text
currently persisted in:
  Monad semantic store
```

---

# Producer

## REQ-PROV-009 — Monad MUST be capable of identifying the producer of derived information

Where information is created through an operation, Monad SHOULD be able to identify the producer.

A producer may be:

* a human,
* an AI agent,
* a compiler,
* a parser,
* a scanner,
* a generator,
* a CI job,
* a verification harness,
* an import process,
* or another system component.

---

## REQ-PROV-010 — Producer identity MUST be distinguishable from source identity

A tool may produce a Claim using information from another source.

For example:

```text
source:
  package-lock.json

producer:
  dependency analyzer
```

Those are different provenance roles.

---

## REQ-PROV-011 — Intermediate producers SHOULD be preserved where material

When information passes through several transformations, Monad SHOULD preserve intermediate producers if omitting them would make the result difficult to:

* reproduce,
* explain,
* debug,
* verify,
* or correct.

---

# Observer

## REQ-PROV-012 — Observation provenance MUST identify the observer where meaningful

An observation SHOULD identify what system or actor performed the observation when that fact matters.

Example:

```text
Observer:
  Git integration

Observed:
  commit abc123 exists
```

---

## REQ-PROV-013 — Observer MUST be distinguishable from observed source

If Monad observes GitHub through an integration, then:

```text
observer:
  Monad GitHub integration
```

is distinct from:

```text
observed system:
  GitHub
```

---

## REQ-PROV-014 — Observation MUST NOT be represented as direct creation by the observer

If Monad detects an external fact, it MUST NOT imply that Monad produced that fact.

---

# Recorder

## REQ-PROV-015 — Monad SHOULD distinguish recorder from producer

The system that records information may not be the system that created it.

For example:

```text
producer:
  test runner

recorder:
  verification service
```

Monad SHOULD preserve the distinction where it affects lineage.

---

## REQ-PROV-016 — Recording time MUST be distinguishable from production time where necessary

An artifact may be produced at one time and recorded by Monad later.

Where timing matters, Monad SHOULD preserve both.

---

# Derivation

## REQ-PROV-017 — Derived information MUST be traceable to material inputs

When Monad derives information algorithmically, it SHOULD preserve references to the material inputs that contributed to the result.

Example:

```text
files
  ↓
parser
  ↓
symbols
  ↓
dependency analysis
  ↓
relationship
```

---

## REQ-PROV-018 — Derivation SHOULD identify the transformation

Where practical, Monad SHOULD record the mechanism responsible for transforming inputs into outputs.

This may include:

* parser identity,
* algorithm,
* rule,
* model,
* prompt,
* generator,
* command,
* version,
* or execution record.

---

## REQ-PROV-019 — Derivation MUST NOT imply correctness

A complete derivation chain proves lineage.

It does not necessarily prove that the transformation was correct.

---

## REQ-PROV-020 — Multi-source derivation MUST be representable

A derived artifact or Claim MAY depend on multiple sources.

Monad MUST support provenance in which one result depends on:

```text
Source A
Source B
Source C
   ↓
Transformation
   ↓
Derived Result
```

---

# Transformation

## REQ-PROV-021 — Transformations SHOULD be identifiable where they can change meaning

Transformations such as:

* normalization,
* summarization,
* parsing,
* conversion,
* migration,
* inference,
* compilation,
* aggregation,
* or projection

SHOULD be represented when they materially affect the relationship between source and result.

---

## REQ-PROV-022 — Lossy transformations MUST be distinguishable from lossless transformations where relevant

Monad SHOULD be capable of indicating whether a transformation preserves all information required to reconstruct its source.

For example:

```text
Markdown → summary
```

is typically lossy.

```text
canonical record → alternate serialization
```

may be intended to be lossless.

---

## REQ-PROV-023 — Semantic normalization MUST retain lineage to native form

If Monad normalizes a native concept into a semantic representation, the normalized result SHOULD remain traceable to the native data from which it was derived.

---

# AI provenance

## REQ-PROV-024 — AI-generated information MUST retain AI provenance

Claims, summaries, relationships, classifications, specifications, code, or other artifacts generated by AI MUST be attributable to the AI operation that produced them when persisted as meaningful engineering state.

---

## REQ-PROV-025 — AI provenance SHOULD identify the generation context needed for interpretation

Where materially useful, Monad SHOULD preserve information such as:

* producing actor or agent,
* model/provider identity,
* prompt or instruction reference,
* source context,
* tool calls,
* relevant configuration,
* generation time,
* and resulting artifact.

Exact retention requirements SHOULD account for privacy, cost, reproducibility, and provider constraints.

---

## REQ-PROV-026 — AI-generated content MUST NOT lose source attribution during promotion

If AI-generated material is later:

* reviewed,
* accepted,
* edited,
* approved,
* or made authoritative,

its earlier provenance SHOULD remain recoverable where useful.

Approval changes governance status.

It does not rewrite history.

---

## REQ-PROV-027 — Human editing of AI-generated material SHOULD be distinguishable from original AI generation

Where meaningful, provenance SHOULD be able to show:

```text
AI produced draft
      ↓
human edited
      ↓
human approved
```

rather than collapsing the sequence into a single unexplained artifact.

---

# Human provenance

## REQ-PROV-028 — Human-authored consequential changes SHOULD retain actor provenance

Changes affecting important engineering semantics SHOULD be attributable to the responsible human actor where practical.

Examples include:

* requirement acceptance,
* architecture decisions,
* policy changes,
* authority grants,
* release approvals,
* manual identity merges,
* and exception approvals.

---

## REQ-PROV-029 — Human provenance MUST NOT imply exclusive authorship when work is collaborative

Monad SHOULD support multiple contributing actors or transformation stages where a single-author model would be misleading.

---

# Native-system provenance

## REQ-PROV-030 — Native-system facts SHOULD retain native provenance

When Monad imports or observes information from native systems, it SHOULD preserve enough provenance to determine which native system supplied the information.

Examples include:

* Git,
* GitHub,
* CI,
* package registries,
* deployment platforms,
* issue trackers,
* databases,
* and cloud systems.

---

## REQ-PROV-031 — Native object identifiers SHOULD be preserved

Where available and stable enough, provenance SHOULD retain native identifiers necessary to revisit the original source.

Examples include:

* commit SHA,
* issue ID,
* run ID,
* deployment ID,
* package version,
* artifact digest,
* or database key.

---

## REQ-PROV-032 — Native-system provenance SHOULD preserve version or revision where interpretation depends on it

A GitHub issue observed today may differ from the same issue observed yesterday.

A file path may refer to different contents across commits.

Where interpretation depends on versioned state, provenance SHOULD include a stable revision or snapshot reference.

---

# Execution provenance

## REQ-PROV-033 — Consequential executions MUST retain execution provenance

Operations that modify state or produce evidence SHOULD retain enough provenance to determine:

* what was requested,
* what actually executed,
* which actor initiated it,
* which tool performed it,
* which environment executed it,
* which inputs were used,
* and what outputs resulted.

---

## REQ-PROV-034 — Requested operation MUST be distinguishable from executed operation

An execution system may resolve, expand, modify, or partially perform the requested operation.

Monad MUST be capable of distinguishing:

```text
requested action
```

from:

```text
actual executed action
```

where correctness or auditability depends on it.

---

## REQ-PROV-035 — Execution environment SHOULD be recordable

Where results depend on environment, provenance SHOULD be able to identify relevant execution context such as:

* operating system,
* architecture,
* runtime,
* dependency versions,
* repository revision,
* environment profile,
* container image,
* toolchain,
* or build configuration.

---

## REQ-PROV-036 — Execution location SHOULD be distinguishable from artifact origin

A source artifact may originate locally while execution occurs remotely.

For example:

```text
source origin:
  local repository

execution location:
  hosted CI runner
```

Monad SHOULD preserve this distinction where useful.

---

## REQ-PROV-037 — Partial execution MUST preserve provenance for completed effects

If an operation partially succeeds, Monad MUST retain provenance for what actually occurred rather than recording only the intended operation.

---

# Verification provenance

## REQ-PROV-038 — Verification results MUST identify the verification activity that produced them

Evidence created by:

* tests,
* reviews,
* inspections,
* static analysis,
* experiments,
* benchmarks,
* or formal checks

SHOULD be traceable to the activity that produced the evidence.

---

## REQ-PROV-039 — Verification provenance SHOULD include evaluated subject and revision

Where possible, Monad SHOULD preserve exactly what was evaluated.

Examples:

```text
requirement revision
specification revision
source commit
binary digest
configuration
environment
```

This prevents later evidence from appearing to verify a different state than the one actually tested.

---

## REQ-PROV-040 — Verification reruns MUST remain distinguishable

Two executions of the same test suite MUST remain distinguishable when their outputs may differ.

---

# Evidence provenance

## REQ-PROV-041 — Evidence MUST retain source provenance

Evidence MUST provide enough provenance to determine how it was obtained.

---

## REQ-PROV-042 — Evidence copied or imported from another system SHOULD preserve original provenance

Importing evidence into Monad MUST NOT make Monad appear to be its producer.

---

## REQ-PROV-043 — Derived evidence MUST preserve the relationship to its source evidence

If one evidence artifact is aggregated, summarized, normalized, or transformed into another, that derivation SHOULD be retained.

---

# Claim provenance

## REQ-PROV-044 — Persisted Claims SHOULD identify their asserting source

Where Claims are first-class artifacts, Monad SHOULD preserve who or what asserted them.

---

## REQ-PROV-045 — Repeated assertions of the same proposition MAY retain distinct provenance

Two sources may independently assert:

```text
Requirement R is satisfied.
```

Monad SHOULD be able to retain both assertion histories even if the proposition is semantically equivalent.

---

## REQ-PROV-046 — Claim correction MUST preserve prior provenance

Correcting a Claim SHOULD NOT erase:

* who made the earlier assertion,
* when it was made,
* what evidence supported it,
* or why it was later corrected.

---

# Representation provenance

## REQ-PROV-047 — Generated Representations SHOULD retain derivation provenance

Generated documentation, indexes, dashboards, reports, or visualizations SHOULD be traceable to the semantic or native information from which they were produced when the relationship matters.

---

## REQ-PROV-048 — Authored and generated Representations MUST remain distinguishable

Monad SHOULD be capable of determining whether a Representation was:

* authored directly,
* generated,
* imported,
* mirrored,
* transformed,
* or reconstructed.

---

## REQ-PROV-049 — Regeneration SHOULD NOT destroy historical provenance where retained history matters

If a generated artifact changes after regeneration, Monad SHOULD preserve enough information to understand the prior generated state when required for:

* release reconstruction,
* audit,
* debugging,
* or evidence.

---

# Import provenance

## REQ-PROV-050 — Imported information MUST retain source-system provenance

Importing a repository, dataset, artifact collection, or external knowledge base MUST preserve enough metadata to identify the source.

---

## REQ-PROV-051 — Import time MUST NOT be mistaken for creation time

If an artifact was created years before Monad imported it, the import operation MUST NOT silently overwrite the artifact's historical origin.

---

## REQ-PROV-052 — Import transformations SHOULD be recorded

If an import process:

* renames,
* restructures,
* normalizes,
* filters,
* deduplicates,
* merges,
* or enriches

the imported material, those transformations SHOULD be traceable.

---

# Migration provenance

## REQ-PROV-053 — Schema or storage migrations SHOULD preserve semantic provenance

Changing storage representation MUST NOT unnecessarily destroy provenance relationships.

---

## REQ-PROV-054 — Migration-produced state SHOULD be distinguishable from originally authored state

When a migration rewrites records, Monad SHOULD be able to identify the migration as the immediate producer without losing the original semantic origin.

Conceptually:

```text
Original Author
     ↓
Original Record
     ↓
Migration
     ↓
Migrated Record
```

---

## REQ-PROV-055 — Lossy migrations MUST be detectable

If a migration cannot preserve all prior information, the loss SHOULD be explicit and evidenced.

---

# Reconciliation provenance

## REQ-PROV-056 — Reconciliation changes MUST retain reconciliation provenance

When Monad updates semantic state because native state has changed, the resulting update MUST be distinguishable from:

* direct human editing,
* AI inference,
* execution,
* import,
* or ordinary authored changes.

---

## REQ-PROV-057 — Reconciliation SHOULD preserve both prior observation and new observation

Where historical reasoning matters, Monad SHOULD retain enough provenance to show:

```text
previously observed state
      ↓
new native observation
      ↓
reconciliation result
```

---

## REQ-PROV-058 — Reconciliation conflicts MUST retain the competing sources

If reconciliation reveals incompatible state from multiple sources, Monad MUST preserve the provenance of those sources rather than collapsing them prematurely.

---

# Time

## REQ-PROV-059 — Provenance timestamps MUST have explicit semantics

A timestamp associated with provenance MUST have a defined meaning.

Possible meanings include:

* source creation time,
* observation time,
* production time,
* execution time,
* recording time,
* import time,
* transformation time,
* or reconciliation time.

---

## REQ-PROV-060 — Distinct provenance times MUST NOT be collapsed when their difference matters

For example:

```text
source event occurred: 10:00
observed by Monad:      10:03
recorded locally:       10:04
```

may be materially different.

---

## REQ-PROV-061 — Unknown source time MUST remain distinguishable from recording time

If Monad does not know when an external artifact was originally created, it MUST NOT substitute import or observation time and present it as source creation time.

---

# Identity

## REQ-PROV-062 — Provenance MUST refer to stable semantic or native identity where available

Provenance relationships SHOULD avoid relying solely on mutable labels or paths when more durable identifiers are available.

---

## REQ-PROV-063 — Provenance MUST survive Representation relocation where identity survives

If a Representation moves from:

```text
docs/old-name.md
```

to:

```text
docs/new-name.md
```

its earlier provenance SHOULD remain connected to the same semantic Subject where identity continuity has been established.

---

## REQ-PROV-064 — Identity merge and split operations MUST retain provenance

When two identities are merged or one identity is split, the operation SHOULD preserve:

* prior identities,
* initiating actor,
* rationale,
* evidence,
* time,
* and resulting identity relationships.

---

# Versioning

## REQ-PROV-065 — Provenance SHOULD identify source revision where mutable sources are involved

For mutable sources, provenance SHOULD reference a revision, version, snapshot, digest, timestamped observation, or equivalent boundary where necessary to reproduce interpretation.

---

## REQ-PROV-066 — Artifact version and provenance event MUST remain distinct

A document revision is not the same thing as a provenance event.

One artifact revision may participate in many provenance relationships.

One provenance event may produce several artifacts.

---

# Reproducibility

## REQ-PROV-067 — Provenance SHOULD support reproducibility where reproducibility is required

For reproducible operations, provenance SHOULD retain enough information to reconstruct:

* inputs,
* tool versions,
* configuration,
* source revision,
* environment,
* execution procedure,
* and resulting outputs.

---

## REQ-PROV-068 — Reproducibility claims MUST NOT exceed retained provenance

Monad MUST NOT claim an operation is reproducible if essential inputs or execution details required for reproduction were not retained.

---

## REQ-PROV-069 — Non-reproducible operations MAY still have useful provenance

Perfect reproducibility is not required for provenance to be valuable.

For example, a human architectural review may not be mechanically reproducible but should still retain:

* participants,
* reviewed material,
* outcome,
* and rationale.

---

# Local-first provenance

## REQ-PROV-070 — Core provenance SHOULD remain inspectable locally

Provenance necessary to understand the project's retained semantic state SHOULD be locally inspectable without requiring continuous access to external services.

---

## REQ-PROV-071 — Remote provenance references MUST degrade intelligibly when remote systems are unavailable

If an external reference cannot be resolved, Monad SHOULD still preserve:

* the reference itself,
* known identity,
* last observed metadata,
* provenance relationship,
* and resolution status.

---

## REQ-PROV-072 — Provenance capture SHOULD avoid unnecessary dependency on centralized services

Local engineering activity SHOULD NOT require a remote provenance service merely to record core lineage.

---

# Privacy and sensitive information

## REQ-PROV-073 — Provenance capture MUST respect privacy and secret-handling boundaries

Monad MUST NOT indiscriminately preserve every input merely because it may improve provenance.

Sensitive data may include:

* credentials,
* private prompts,
* personal information,
* confidential source material,
* environment secrets,
* proprietary external content,
* and regulated data.

---

## REQ-PROV-074 — Provenance SHOULD support redacted references where full source retention is inappropriate

Where the source itself cannot safely be retained, Monad MAY retain:

* digest,
* identifier,
* metadata,
* classification,
* redacted reference,
* or other non-sensitive lineage information.

---

## REQ-PROV-075 — Diagnostic output MUST NOT unnecessarily disclose sensitive provenance

Errors and reports SHOULD provide enough provenance for debugging without leaking secrets or private content.

---

# Integrity

## REQ-PROV-076 — Provenance records SHOULD be tamper-evident where integrity requirements justify it

High-assurance workflows MAY require provenance records to support mechanisms such as:

* hashes,
* signatures,
* immutable logs,
* signed attestations,
* or externally verifiable references.

The exact mechanism is a specification and architecture concern.

---

## REQ-PROV-077 — Provenance integrity MUST NOT be confused with source correctness

A cryptographically intact provenance record can faithfully prove that a bad source produced a bad result.

Integrity validates lineage, not necessarily truth.

---

# Granularity

## REQ-PROV-078 — Provenance granularity SHOULD match the reasoning need

Monad SHOULD capture provenance at the smallest level that provides material value without imposing excessive capture cost.

Possible levels include:

* artifact,
* Subject,
* Representation,
* Claim,
* relationship,
* field,
* execution,
* evidence item,
* or transformation.

---

## REQ-PROV-079 — Field-level provenance SHOULD be available where whole-object provenance is insufficient

For some merged or synthesized records, individual properties may originate from different sources.

Monad SHOULD support finer-grained provenance where the distinction matters.

Example:

```text
Component:
  name        ← package manifest
  owner       ← governance file
  health      ← derived analysis
  deployment  ← deployment platform
```

---

## REQ-PROV-080 — Provenance detail SHOULD NOT exceed sustainable maintenance cost without justification

Monad SHOULD avoid provenance models so exhaustive that capture friction causes engineers or systems to bypass them.

---

# Progressive formalization

## REQ-PROV-081 — Provenance MAY begin coarse and become more precise

Early capture MAY record:

```text
source: GitHub
```

while later formalization may record:

```text
repository
issue
revision
observer
timestamp
adapter
transformation
```

where the additional precision earns its cost.

---

## REQ-PROV-082 — Coarse provenance MUST NOT masquerade as precise provenance

If source lineage is only known approximately, Monad MUST preserve that uncertainty.

---

# Provenance chains

## REQ-PROV-083 — Monad MUST support multi-stage provenance chains

Engineering information may move through:

```text
native source
   ↓
observation
   ↓
normalization
   ↓
semantic claim
   ↓
inference
   ↓
generated projection
```

The model SHOULD preserve enough of that chain to explain consequential derived results.

---

## REQ-PROV-084 — Provenance chains SHOULD remain navigable in both directions where practical

It SHOULD be possible to ask:

> What produced this artifact?

and:

> What artifacts were derived from this source?

where the retained data supports both questions.

---

## REQ-PROV-085 — Provenance chains MUST NOT imply causality beyond recorded derivation

A provenance link may show that information was used as an input.

It does not necessarily establish broader causal meaning.

---

# Provenance and deletion

## REQ-PROV-086 — Deleting a Representation SHOULD NOT unnecessarily erase lineage

Where history or auditability matters, deleting an active Representation SHOULD preserve enough provenance to explain:

* that it existed,
* what Subject it represented,
* and what later happened to it.

---

## REQ-PROV-087 — Retention policies MAY limit provenance history

Monad MAY support policies that:

* compact,
* expire,
* redact,
* summarize,
* archive,
* or delete

provenance records.

Such behavior MUST be explicit where it affects auditability or reproducibility.

---

# Provenance and derived projections

## REQ-PROV-088 — Projections SHOULD expose derivation provenance when decisions may rely on them

A generated dashboard or AI context package SHOULD be able to indicate its source state or generation revision where stale or misleading projections could cause incorrect action.

---

## REQ-PROV-089 — Projection regeneration SHOULD create a distinguishable provenance event

If the same projection is generated twice, the two generation events SHOULD remain distinguishable where reproducibility or freshness matters.

---

# Provenance and releases

## REQ-PROV-090 — Release artifacts SHOULD be traceable to their production inputs

A release SHOULD be able to reference relevant:

* source revision,
* build inputs,
* build execution,
* toolchain,
* generated artifacts,
* verification,
* approvals,
* and resulting distributable.

---

## REQ-PROV-091 — Release provenance SHOULD support historical reconstruction

Where practical, Monad SHOULD preserve enough lineage to answer:

> What exactly produced release R?

---

# Provenance and decisions

## REQ-PROV-092 — Consequential decisions SHOULD retain decision provenance

A Decision SHOULD be traceable to relevant:

* context,
* participants,
* source requirements,
* alternatives,
* evidence,
* experiments,
* review,
* and approval activity.

---

## REQ-PROV-093 — Decision provenance MUST NOT imply decision correctness

A perfectly documented decision may still prove wrong.

Provenance preserves reasoning history.

It does not sanctify the outcome.

---

# Provenance and requirements

## REQ-PROV-094 — Requirements SHOULD preserve source provenance

Where known, a Requirement SHOULD be traceable to the source from which the obligation arose.

Possible sources include:

* stakeholder need,
* regulation,
* architecture constraint,
* prior decision,
* experiment,
* user observation,
* operational incident,
* or project principle.

---

## REQ-PROV-095 — Derived Requirements SHOULD retain their derivation

If a Requirement is decomposed or derived from another obligation, Monad SHOULD preserve that relationship.

---

# Provenance and evidence-backed reasoning

## REQ-PROV-096 — Reasoning chains SHOULD preserve both evidentiary and provenance relationships

Consider:

```text
Test Run
   ↓ produced
Evidence
   ↓ supports
Claim
   ↓ concerns
Requirement
```

The system SHOULD preserve both:

* **provenance**: the test run produced the evidence,
* **epistemic relationship**: the evidence supports the Claim.

Those relationships are not interchangeable.

---

# Provenance queries

## REQ-PROV-097 — Monad SHOULD support provenance-oriented queries

The system SHOULD eventually support questions such as:

* Where did this Claim come from?
* Which actor produced this artifact?
* Which source revision was used?
* What transformation created this value?
* Which evidence came from this verification run?
* Which AI operation proposed this relationship?
* What was the original native object?
* What generated this page?
* Which artifacts depend on this source?
* Which migration rewrote this record?
* Was this value authored, observed, inferred, or derived?
* When did Monad first observe this state?
* What changed between the source and the current Representation?

---

## REQ-PROV-098 — Provenance queries MUST preserve uncertainty

If Monad cannot determine a source or transformation with confidence, it SHOULD return an explicit unknown or partial answer rather than inventing lineage.

---

# Provenance representation

## REQ-PROV-099 — Provenance SHOULD be machine-readable where automation depends on it

Consequential provenance SHOULD be represented in structured form when automated:

* verification,
* reconciliation,
* traceability,
* impact analysis,
* security,
* or AI reasoning

depends on it.

---

## REQ-PROV-100 — Provenance MAY have human-readable Representations

Structured provenance MAY be rendered as:

* timelines,
* lineage graphs,
* audit trails,
* generated reports,
* execution traces,
* or artifact histories.

Those Representations MUST remain distinguishable from the provenance model itself.

# Illustrative provenance chain

The following is explanatory rather than canonical serialization.

Suppose an AI system proposes a relationship between two components.

```text
Git repository at commit abc123
          ↓ observed by
Repository Scanner v2
          ↓ produced
Parsed dependency facts
          ↓ supplied to
AI analysis operation
          ↓ produced
Candidate Relationship
          ↓ reviewed by
Human engineer
          ↓ approved as
Authoritative Relationship
```

A useful provenance record might answer:

```text
Original native source:
  Git repository

Resolved source revision:
  abc123

Observer:
  Repository Scanner v2

Intermediate result:
  parsed dependency facts

Inference producer:
  AI analysis operation

Candidate assertion:
  Component A depends on Component B

Reviewer:
  human engineer

Governance outcome:
  approved
```

The chain explains how the relationship entered the system.

It still does not imply that every step had equal authority.

# Provenance role matrix

| Role             | Question                                           |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Origin           | Where did this information begin?                  |
| Source           | What input supplied the information?               |
| Producer         | Who or what created this result?                   |
| Observer         | Who or what detected the source state?             |
| Recorder         | Who or what persisted the record?                  |
| Transformer      | What changed the representation or meaning?        |
| Derivation input | What prior information contributed to this result? |
| Execution        | What operation produced the outcome?               |
| Environment      | Where and under what conditions did it happen?     |
| Revision         | Which version of the source was involved?          |
| Time             | When did the relevant provenance event occur?      |
| Actor            | Who or what participated?                          |

These roles MAY overlap in simple cases.

Monad SHOULD preserve their distinction where collapsing them would lose important information.

# Provenance event model

A useful conceptual model is:

```text
Inputs
  ↓
Provenance Event
  ↓
Outputs
```

A provenance event may include:

```text
actor
operation
tool
environment
time
inputs
outputs
source revisions
transformations
```

This does not require every provenance relationship to become a heavyweight event record.

It provides a consistent way to reason about consequential transformations.

# Provenance versus neighboring concepts

## Provenance versus authority

```text
Provenance:
  Who said this?

Authority:
  Whose statement governs?
```

---

## Provenance versus evidence

```text
Provenance:
  Where did this artifact come from?

Evidence:
  What proposition does it support or challenge?
```

---

## Provenance versus identity

```text
Identity:
  What thing is this?

Provenance:
  How did this information about that thing arise?
```

---

## Provenance versus history

History records what happened over time.

Provenance records how one information state derives from another.

They overlap but are not identical.

---

## Provenance versus causality

```text
Artifact B derived from Artifact A
```

is a provenance relationship.

It does not necessarily prove:

```text
A is the root cause of some broader system behavior.
```

# Capture strategy

Monad SHOULD favor provenance capture that can be produced automatically from normal engineering activity.

Examples include:

* execution wrappers,
* integration adapters,
* Git revisions,
* tool invocation records,
* structured AI calls,
* generated artifact metadata,
* verification harnesses,
* and reconciliation events.

Manual provenance entry SHOULD be reserved for information that cannot reasonably be captured automatically.

> **The best provenance system is one engineers do not have to remember to maintain by hand.**

---

# Minimal capture

At minimum, a consequential provenance record will often need enough information to identify:

```text
what
came from
where
through what operation
performed by whom or what
at approximately what time
```

More detail should be added only when the engineering benefit justifies it.

# Failure modes

## Treating storage as origin

```text
stored in Monad
```

does not mean:

```text
created by Monad
```

---

## Treating provenance as authority

```text
came from GitHub
```

does not mean:

```text
GitHub governs this semantic claim
```

---

## Losing revision identity

```text
source: README.md
```

may be insufficient if the file changed between observation and use.

---

## Overwriting historical provenance

Updating current state must not make old information appear to have originated differently.

---

## AI provenance collapse

AI-generated content must not become indistinguishable from human-authored or verified content merely because it was accepted.

---

## Provenance theater

Recording enormous amounts of metadata that nobody can query, interpret, or maintain is not useful provenance.

---

## Lossy migration without disclosure

Transforming data while silently discarding lineage undermines historical trust.

---

## Capture burden

A provenance system that requires constant manual annotation is likely to decay.

---

## Network-dependent lineage

Core provenance should not become unintelligible merely because a hosted service is temporarily unavailable.

# Falsifiability

Provenance is valuable only when it improves engineering outcomes.

Monad SHOULD reject provenance detail that:

* is never queried,
* cannot affect a decision,
* does not improve reproducibility,
* does not improve debugging,
* does not improve traceability,
* does not improve governance,
* and imposes meaningful maintenance cost.

The system SHOULD seek the smallest provenance model sufficient for the reasoning it needs.

> **More lineage is not automatically more knowledge.**

---

# Governing principle

Monad should preserve enough provenance that consequential engineering information can answer:

> Where did this come from?

> Who or what produced it?

> What source revision was involved?

> What transformation occurred?

> What was observed versus inferred?

> When did it happen?

> Where did it execute?

> What did it depend on?

> What was changed along the way?

> Can the result be reproduced or independently checked?

But provenance must remain what it is:

> **a record of lineage, not a substitute for truth, authority, evidence, or judgment.**
