# What is a review?

A **Review** is a structured examination of an engineering Subject by one or more Actors.

The Subject may be:

```text
Requirement

Specification

Architecture

ADR

implementation

pull request

schema

Policy

migration

experiment

release candidate

Verification result

Evidence bundle

incident analysis

operational change
```

A Review asks questions such as:

```text
Is this correct?

Is this complete?

Is this coherent?

Does it satisfy its stated purpose?

What assumptions does it make?

What could fail?

What evidence is missing?

What conflicts exist?

What should change before proceeding?
```

A Review produces findings.

It may influence governance, Verification, planning, implementation, or release decisions.

But Review is not itself synonymous with any of them.

---

# Review is not approval

One of the most important distinctions is:

```text
Review ≠ Approval
```

A reviewer may conclude:

```text
I examined this work and found no blocking problems.
```

That does not necessarily mean:

```text
I possess the Authority to authorize the next action.
```

Likewise, an approver may rely on Reviews performed by others.

Conceptually:

```text
Review
   ↓
Findings
   ↓
Evidence / Judgment
   ↓
Governance
   ↓
Approval or Denial
```

The same Actor may sometimes review and approve.

The semantic roles should still remain distinct.

---

# Review is not verification

[Verification](/docs/artifacts/verification) evaluates an explicit expectation against Evidence.

Review is broader.

A Review may examine:

```text
quality

clarity

architecture

risk

reasoning

maintainability

security

consistency

trade-offs

unknowns
```

without having a binary acceptance criterion.

For example:

```text
Review:
Does this architecture create unnecessary coupling?
```

may produce:

```text
Finding:
The persistence abstraction leaks database-specific identifiers
into the semantic domain model.
```

That finding is useful even if there is no formal pass/fail criterion.

---

# Review can be a verification method

Some Reviews are explicitly conformance-oriented.

Example:

```text
Expectation:
Domain components must not depend on infrastructure-specific types.

Method:
Architecture Review

Finding:
Database-specific identifier leaked through interface.

Verification Result:
FAIL
```

In this case:

```text
Review
```

is the method, while:

```text
Verification Result
```

is the conclusion against the explicit expectation.

The distinction remains useful.

---

# Review is not discussion

Engineering discussion is valuable.

But unstructured conversation should not automatically become a formal Review.

A Review normally has enough structure to identify:

```text
what was reviewed

why it was reviewed

who participated

which revision was examined

what findings emerged

which findings matter

what happens next
```

This allows the result to remain useful after the conversation ends.

---

# Review is not commentary

Comments may participate in a Review.

For example:

```text
GitHub pull request comments
```

may contain review findings.

But:

```text
comment ≠ Review
```

A Review is the larger semantic activity.

Individual comments are Representations or contributions within it.

---

# Why Reviews exist

Engineering work frequently contains problems that automated systems cannot identify completely.

Examples include:

```text
incorrect assumptions

unclear Requirements

unnecessary complexity

weak abstractions

architectural drift

poor failure semantics

missing edge cases

security concerns

ambiguous ownership

insufficient Evidence

misaligned trade-offs

unintended coupling

operational risk
```

Reviews provide a deliberate mechanism for discovering these problems before they become embedded in the system.

---

# Reviews produce findings

The primary output of a Review is usually one or more **Findings**.

A Finding is a structured observation or judgment resulting from the Review.

Examples:

```text
Requirement is ambiguous.

Specification does not define failure behavior.

Migration lacks rollback strategy.

Interface exposes infrastructure-specific details.

Evidence does not support the stated Claim.

Implementation violates architecture boundary.

Security assumption is unjustified.

No problem found in reviewed scope.
```

Findings should be traceable to the Review that produced them.

---

# Findings are not all defects

A Finding may represent:

```text
defect

risk

question

ambiguity

gap

suggestion

observation

recommendation

conflict

missing Evidence

unresolved assumption

positive confirmation
```

Not every useful Review outcome should be forced into:

```text
bug
```

The taxonomy should remain rich enough to preserve meaning without becoming bureaucratic.

---

# Illustrative finding classes

A practical conceptual vocabulary might include:

```text
BLOCKER

MAJOR

MINOR

QUESTION

RISK

GAP

SUGGESTION

OBSERVATION
```

Exact canonical classifications should be defined only where they provide operational value.

---

# Severity and disposition are different

A finding may have:

```text
Severity:
Major
```

and later:

```text
Disposition:
Accepted
```

or:

```text
Disposition:
Rejected with rationale
```

These are different dimensions.

Severity describes consequence.

Disposition describes what happened to the finding.

---

# Finding lifecycle

A useful conceptual lifecycle is:

```text
Raised
   ↓
Acknowledged
   ↓
Investigating
   ↓
Resolved
   ↓
Verified
   ↓
Closed
```

Alternative paths may include:

```text
Rejected

Deferred

Superseded

Accepted Risk

Duplicate

Not Applicable
```

The final lifecycle should remain as simple as actual engineering work permits.

---

# Findings need identity when consequence justifies it

A typo suggestion in a small documentation patch may not need a globally durable identity.

A critical architecture finding probably does.

A finding earns durable identity when it may need to survive:

```text
revision changes

Review closure

release boundaries

migration

tool changes

cross-artifact traceability

long-running remediation
```

The cost of formalization should remain proportional to consequence.

---

# Review scope

Every Review has scope.

Scope might identify:

```text
Subject

revision

artifact section

component

interface

change set

release candidate

Requirement set

risk domain

time period
```

Without scope, statements such as:

```text
review passed
```

are dangerously ambiguous.

---

# Review revision

A Review should normally identify the revision examined.

For example:

```text
Subject:
SPEC-014

Revision:
7

Review:
REV-032
```

If the Specification later changes:

```text
Revision:
8
```

the previous Review remains historically meaningful but may no longer establish anything about the new state.

---

# Review freshness

Reviews can become stale.

Potential invalidators include:

```text
Subject changed materially

relevant Requirement changed

architecture changed

risk model changed

Policy changed

dependency behavior changed

new Evidence emerged

review assumption was invalidated
```

A stale Review should remain visible as history.

It should not silently masquerade as current assurance.

---

# Review provenance

A consequential Review should preserve enough provenance to answer:

```text
Who reviewed it?

When?

Which revision?

Using what criteria?

What context was available?

What tools assisted?

What findings were produced?

What Evidence was examined?
```

This matters because engineering judgment depends on context.

---

# Review criteria

A Review may define explicit criteria.

Examples:

```text
Requirement quality

architectural consistency

security posture

API compatibility

migration safety

operational readiness

maintainability

testability

observability

reproducibility
```

Explicit criteria improve consistency and help future reviewers understand the basis of judgment.

---

# Review checklists

Checklists can be useful when recurring mistakes matter.

For example:

```text
Architecture Review

[ ] responsibilities are explicit
[ ] ownership boundaries are coherent
[ ] authority is not accidentally duplicated
[ ] native systems remain first-class
[ ] failure semantics are defined
[ ] identity semantics survive relocation
[ ] derived state is distinguishable from canonical state
```

But checklists must not become ceremonial substitutes for thought.

A checked box is not Evidence that the underlying question was considered well.

---

# Review depth should match risk

Not every artifact deserves the same Review process.

A conceptual model:

```text
Low consequence
→ lightweight self-review

Moderate consequence
→ peer Review

High consequence
→ specialized Review

Critical consequence
→ independent multi-discipline Review
```

This mirrors Monad's broader principle of proportional governance.

---

# Self-review

The author may review their own work.

Self-review is especially useful before requesting external Review.

Typical questions:

```text
Did I solve the stated problem?

Did I accidentally broaden scope?

Are assumptions explicit?

Are failure cases covered?

Is the result internally consistent?

Can another engineer understand why this exists?

Did I provide enough Evidence?
```

Self-review is useful.

It is not independent assurance.

---

# Peer review

Peer Review introduces another perspective.

It can expose:

```text
author blind spots

unclear reasoning

hidden assumptions

local optimization

unexpected integration effects
```

Peer Review is often appropriate for ordinary engineering changes.

---

# Independent review

Independent Review is valuable when:

```text
consequence is high

author bias matters

regulatory or governance requirements exist

safety or security is involved

architectural commitments are difficult to reverse
```

The reviewer should have enough independence that:

```text
review outcome
```

is not merely a restatement of the author's prior conclusion.

---

# Specialist review

Some domains require specialized expertise.

Examples:

```text
Security Review

Database Review

API Compatibility Review

Architecture Review

Privacy Review

Operational Readiness Review

Accessibility Review

Performance Review
```

A generic reviewer should not be assumed competent in every technical domain.

---

# Multi-reviewer examination

Complex changes may need several Review perspectives.

For example:

```text
Release Candidate
    │
    ├── Architecture Review
    ├── Security Review
    ├── Migration Review
    ├── Operations Review
    └── Product Requirement Review
```

These Reviews may produce independent findings.

They should not be collapsed into one vague:

```text
reviewed
```

flag.

---

# Architecture Review

Architecture Review asks whether the proposed or implemented structure remains coherent with system responsibilities and invariants.

Potential questions include:

```text
Are responsibility boundaries explicit?

Does this component own the right semantics?

Has infrastructure leaked into the domain model?

Has a derived Representation become accidentally authoritative?

Are native systems still treated as legitimate owners?

Does this architecture create duplicate sources of truth?

Are failure boundaries understood?

Does this decision unnecessarily constrain future evolution?
```

Architecture Review should challenge structure, not merely formatting.

---

# Requirement Review

Requirement Review asks whether a Requirement is:

```text
necessary

clear

bounded

testable

consistent

traceable

feasible

non-duplicative
```

A Requirement should often be reviewed before implementation begins.

Otherwise engineering may precisely implement the wrong expectation.

---

# Specification Review

Specification Review may examine:

```text
behavior

inputs

outputs

invariants

failure semantics

state transitions

compatibility

acceptance criteria

Verification method

authority boundaries

edge cases
```

A good Specification Review should make implementation more deterministic.

---

# ADR Review

An [Architecture Decision](/docs/artifacts/decisions) Review should examine:

```text
decision context

alternatives

trade-offs

assumptions

evidence

reversibility

consequences

supersession conditions
```

Reviewing only the selected option is insufficient.

The reasoning matters.

---

# Schema Review

Schema Review may examine:

```text
semantic correctness

field ownership

identity representation

versioning

migration strategy

required versus optional fields

unknown-state handling

forward compatibility

backward compatibility
```

A structurally valid schema can still encode the wrong model.

---

# Interface Review

Interface Review should inspect:

```text
ownership

operation semantics

input/output contracts

side effects

idempotency

authorization

errors

versioning

revision behavior

partial failure

transport independence
```

Interfaces often become long-lived commitments.

Review depth should reflect that.

---

# Code Review

Code Review examines implementation.

Useful concerns include:

```text
correctness

clarity

maintainability

security

performance

failure handling

tests

architecture alignment

unnecessary complexity
```

Monad should not reduce Review semantics to pull requests alone.

A pull request is one native Representation of Review activity.

---

# Migration Review

Migration Review deserves special attention because migrations can alter durable state.

Questions may include:

```text
What changes?

What remains invariant?

How is identity preserved?

What data can be lost?

Can the migration resume?

Can it be retried?

How is partial failure handled?

How will results be reconciled?

How will success be verified?
```

---

# Security Review

Security Review examines adversarial risk.

It may consider:

```text
trust boundaries

authorization

credential handling

input validation

attack surface

dependency risk

privilege escalation

data exposure

unsafe defaults

auditability
```

Security Review should not be treated as a generic code-quality check.

---

# Operational Readiness Review

Before deployment or release, operational Review may ask:

```text
Can this be observed?

Can failure be detected?

Can operators diagnose it?

Can it be rolled back or compensated?

Are runbooks sufficient?

Are dependencies understood?

Are alerts meaningful?

Are capacity assumptions justified?
```

Operational readiness is different from implementation correctness.

---

# Release Review

A Release Review examines whether a candidate is ready to move into a supported lifecycle state.

Inputs may include:

```text
Verification results

Evidence

known risks

open findings

migration readiness

compatibility status

security status

release notes

rollback plan
```

The Review can inform release Approval.

It should not itself be silently treated as Approval unless the same action explicitly performs both roles.

---

# Evidence Review

Evidence itself may require Review.

Questions include:

```text
Does this Evidence support the stated Claim?

Is provenance sufficient?

Is the Evidence stale?

Was the correct revision evaluated?

Are limitations disclosed?

Could the result be misleading?
```

A large amount of Evidence is not useful if it does not support the relevant Claim.

---

# Review of Verification

Verification artifacts may also be reviewed.

For example:

```text
Are acceptance criteria correct?

Does the test actually exercise the Requirement?

Is the fixture representative?

Could the method produce false positives?

Could it miss important failure classes?

Is the result being interpreted too broadly?
```

This is especially important for critical Verification infrastructure.

---

# Review of AI-generated work

AI-generated engineering work should be reviewed according to consequence.

The review should evaluate:

```text
the artifact

the Claim

the implementation

the resulting system behavior
```

not merely whether the prose or code appears plausible.

---

# AI-assisted Review

AI systems can assist reviewers by:

```text
comparing implementation to Specifications

finding missing edge cases

tracing Requirements

summarizing diffs

identifying suspicious patterns

checking consistency

searching related artifacts

suggesting Review questions
```

These capabilities can reduce review burden.

They do not remove the need for appropriate authority or independent judgment.

---

# AI findings are still findings

An AI reviewer may produce:

```text
candidate Finding
```

with provenance such as:

```text
producer:
AI model

model version:
...

review method:
Specification comparison
```

The Finding should not become authoritative merely because it was generated automatically.

---

# Human confirmation of AI findings

For consequential issues, a useful flow is:

```text
AI Review
   ↓
Candidate Finding
   ↓
Human Examination
   ↓
Confirmed / Rejected / Modified
```

This preserves both automation value and epistemic clarity.

---

# Human reviewers can also be wrong

Monad should not encode:

```text
human judgment = truth
```

Human Reviews can suffer from:

```text
bias

fatigue

missing context

incorrect assumptions

domain gaps

social pressure

premature consensus
```

Important conclusions remain strongest when connected to reasoning and Evidence.

---

# Review comments versus findings

Native tools often represent Review activity as comments.

For example:

```text
GitHub PR comment
```

may contain:

```text
"This function can silently overwrite canonical state."
```

Monad may interpret this as a Finding.

The native comment remains the Native Reference.

The semantic Finding may have a separate durable identity.

---

# Native review systems remain first-class

GitHub, GitLab, Gerrit, IDEs, document systems, and issue trackers already support Review workflows.

Monad should integrate with them rather than replace them unnecessarily.

Conceptually:

```text
Semantic Review
      │
      ├── represented-by GitHub review
      ├── represented-by issue discussion
      └── represented-by review artifact
```

The semantic model provides continuity across representations.

---

# GitHub Review example

A GitHub pull request may contain:

```text
PR #52

Reviewer:
Alice

Review state:
Changes requested

Comments:
4
```

Monad may derive or bind:

```text
Review:
REV-052

Subject:
Implementation of SPEC-008

Native Reference:
GitHub PR #52

Findings:
F-103
F-104
F-105
F-106
```

The GitHub state remains native GitHub state.

The semantic Review captures engineering meaning.

---

# Review state versus native state

GitHub might report:

```text
CHANGES_REQUESTED
```

Monad may separately track:

```text
Review findings:
3 resolved
1 unresolved blocker
```

These are related but not identical.

Native state should not be overwritten by semantic interpretation.

---

# Review synchronization

If Monad writes Review state back to a native system, the direction of authority must be explicit.

Examples:

```text
Monad Finding
→ create GitHub comment
```

or:

```text
GitHub review comment
→ observed by Monad
→ candidate Finding
```

Bidirectional synchronization should not be assumed.

---

# Review and reconciliation

Native Review activity may diverge from Monad's semantic model.

For example:

```text
GitHub comment edited

Finding marked resolved in Monad

PR reopened

new commit invalidated previous Review
```

Reconciliation should surface those changes rather than silently guessing which state wins.

---

# Review status

An illustrative Review lifecycle might be:

```text
Planned

In Progress

Completed

Superseded

Cancelled
```

The Review's lifecycle should remain distinct from individual finding lifecycles.

A Review may be complete while some findings remain unresolved.

---

# Review outcome

A Review outcome may summarize the state of its findings.

For example:

```text
No Findings

Non-blocking Findings

Changes Requested

Blocked

Accepted with Known Risk
```

These labels are contextual.

The underlying findings remain more important than the summary label.

---

# No universal "review passed"

A single global:

```text
review:
PASS
```

can erase important nuance.

Consider:

```text
Security Review:
PASS

Architecture Review:
PASS WITH 2 MINOR FINDINGS

Migration Review:
BLOCKED
```

Flattening this to:

```text
reviewed:
true
```

destroys useful engineering state.

---

# Review disposition

Each significant Finding should eventually have a disposition.

Examples:

```text
Resolved

Accepted

Rejected

Deferred

Accepted Risk

Duplicate

Superseded

Not Applicable
```

The disposition should include rationale when that rationale matters.

---

# Rejecting a finding

A Finding may be rejected because:

```text
the reviewer misunderstood the system

the concern is outside scope

Evidence contradicts the Finding

the proposed change would create greater harm

the underlying assumption was incorrect
```

Rejecting a Finding is legitimate.

Silently deleting it is not.

---

# Accepted risk

Sometimes a Finding is valid but intentionally unresolved.

Example:

```text
Finding:
Current implementation does not support Windows.

Disposition:
Accepted Risk

Rationale:
M0 is Linux/macOS only.

Expiry:
Before M2 general availability.
```

This is more honest than pretending the issue does not exist.

---

# Deferred findings

A Finding may be real but not currently actionable.

A deferral should ideally record:

```text
why

until when or until what condition

risk

owner

affected scope
```

Otherwise:

```text
deferred
```

becomes another word for forgotten.

---

# Review debt

Unresolved Review findings create engineering debt.

Useful queries include:

```text
Which blockers remain open?

Which accepted risks are approaching expiry?

Which findings were deferred repeatedly?

Which releases contain unresolved major findings?

Which components accumulate recurring Review concerns?
```

This converts Review history into operational knowledge.

---

# Re-review

Material changes may require re-review.

Example:

```text
REV-021 reviewed SPEC-008@v3

SPEC-008@v4 introduces
new authority semantics
```

The earlier Review may remain useful historically.

But the new revision may require another Review.

---

# Review invalidation

A Review may be invalidated or superseded when:

```text
the wrong revision was examined

key Evidence was missing

review criteria changed materially

a reviewer conflict was discovered

the Subject changed significantly

a critical assumption proved false
```

Invalidation should be recorded rather than rewriting history.

---

# Review history

Review history can explain engineering evolution.

For example:

```text
REV-011
Architecture proposal rejected.

REV-019
Revised proposal accepted with findings.

REV-027
Implementation review discovered hidden coupling.

REV-034
Refactor resolved coupling.
```

This history can be more informative than the final architecture alone.

---

# Review and historical truth

The [Building Monad](/docs/building-monad) narrative may explain how Review conclusions changed.

The [System](/docs/system) documentation explains the current authoritative understanding.

The Review artifacts preserve the inspectable engineering record between them.

History should not be erased merely because the final system changed.

---

# Review rationale

Important Review conclusions should preserve rationale.

Instead of:

```text
Rejected.
```

prefer:

```text
Rejected because the proposed cache would become
a second authoritative state store and violate the
current ownership model.
```

The rationale may later matter more than the outcome.

---

# Review Evidence

Reviewers may rely on:

```text
Requirements

Specifications

source code

tests

benchmark results

threat models

experiments

runtime observations

historical incidents

external standards

native system state
```

Those sources should be referenced when they materially support the Finding.

---

# Finding without Evidence

Some findings are expert judgments rather than experimentally demonstrated facts.

That is acceptable.

The system should distinguish:

```text
Observation

Inference

Judgment

Evidence-backed conclusion
```

rather than pretending every review comment has equal epistemic status.

---

# Review confidence

A reviewer may sometimes express uncertainty.

For example:

```text
Finding:
Possible race condition in reconciliation.

Confidence:
Moderate

Reason:
Concurrency behavior is not fully specified.
```

Preserving uncertainty is better than overstating certainty.

---

# Review conflicts

Reviewers may disagree.

Example:

```text
Reviewer A:
Interface is too broad.

Reviewer B:
Breadth is justified by provider portability.
```

Monad should not collapse this immediately into one authoritative conclusion.

The disagreement may require:

```text
additional Evidence

architecture decision

experiment

higher-authority review

explicit trade-off decision
```

---

# Competing findings

Two findings may conflict directly.

For example:

```text
Finding A:
Split these components.

Finding B:
Splitting creates excessive coordination overhead.
```

Both may be legitimate observations.

The eventual decision should preserve the trade-off.

---

# Review escalation

A Review may escalate when:

```text
Authority is insufficient

risk exceeds reviewer scope

finding affects constitutional rules

security issue is critical

reviewers cannot resolve conflict

additional expertise is required
```

Escalation should be a first-class outcome rather than an informal side channel.

---

# Review gates

Governance may require certain Reviews before lifecycle transitions.

Example:

```text
Specification
    ↓
Architecture Review
    ↓
Security Review
    ↓
Implementation Authorization
```

or:

```text
Release Candidate
    ↓
Release Review
    ↓
Approval
    ↓
Release
```

The exact gates should remain Policy-driven.

---

# Review obligations

Governance may attach obligations such as:

```text
resolve all blockers

obtain specialist Review

document accepted risk

repeat Review after major changes
```

These obligations should remain visible until satisfied or explicitly waived.

---

# Review waivers

A required Review may occasionally be waived.

A waiver should record:

```text
which Review was waived

why

Authority

scope

risk

expiration

mitigations
```

Waived does not mean reviewed.

---

# Emergency Review

Urgent incidents may require abbreviated Review.

For example:

```text
critical production vulnerability
```

may justify:

```text
fast security Review

emergency Approval

immediate Execution
```

followed by:

```text
full retrospective Review
```

The emergency path should remain visible as an exception.

---

# Review and planning

Review findings often create work.

A Finding may produce:

```text
Task

Work Package

Requirement change

Specification revision

Experiment

ADR

risk record
```

The relationship should be explicit where useful.

---

# Review-driven change

Conceptually:

```text
Review
   ↓
Finding
   ↓
Remediation Work
   ↓
Implementation
   ↓
Verification
   ↓
Finding Resolution
```

This creates a closed assurance loop.

---

# Review and Work Packages

A Work Package may define required Review as part of its Definition of Done.

For example:

```text
WP-018

Required Reviews:
- architecture
- security
- implementation

Required Finding State:
no unresolved blockers
```

This makes Review an explicit engineering input rather than an ad hoc final ceremony.

---

# Review and Definition of Ready

A Review is ready when:

```text
the Subject exists

scope is clear

the relevant revision is identified

required context exists

Review criteria are known

required reviewers are available

necessary Evidence is accessible
```

Starting Review too early can waste effort if the Subject is not coherent enough to examine.

---

# Review and Definition of Done

A Review is complete when:

```text
the defined scope was examined

findings were recorded

critical questions were resolved or explicitly left open

required Evidence was referenced

outcome is recorded

follow-up obligations are visible

provenance is sufficient
```

Completion does not require every Finding to be fixed.

It requires the Review itself to have produced an intelligible result.

# Machine-readable review state

Review artifacts should eventually expose machine-readable fields where useful.

This can support queries such as:

```text
all unresolved blockers

all Reviews for SPEC-012

all security findings affecting release 1.0

all findings accepted as risk

all stale Reviews

all artifacts missing required Review
```

Human-readable prose can coexist with structured state.

---

# Review projections

Useful derived views may include:

```text
Review dashboard

finding backlog

artifact Review history

release Review summary

risk-by-component view

review coverage

open blocker report
```

These are projections over the underlying Review and Finding state.

---

# Review coverage

Coverage can answer:

```text
Which critical Specifications received Architecture Review?

Which releases received Security Review?

Which migrations were reviewed?

Which components repeatedly avoid peer Review?
```

Coverage should not be mistaken for quality.

A bad Review still counts as a Review.

---

# Review effectiveness

More important metrics ask whether Reviews find meaningful issues.

Possible signals include:

```text
defects found before release

repeated finding categories

post-release defects that should have been found

time to resolve major findings

frequency of waived Reviews

finding recurrence

review rework rate
```

Metrics should improve the process, not incentivize performative Review activity.

---

# Avoid reviewer productivity metrics

Metrics such as:

```text
comments per reviewer

reviews per day

findings per pull request
```

can create harmful incentives.

A good Review may produce:

```text
No findings.
```

because the work is excellent.

A poor Review may produce dozens of low-value comments.

Quantity is not quality.

---

# Review latency

Review delay matters because long queues slow engineering.

Possible measurements include:

```text
time to first Review

time to final Review

time waiting on blocker resolution
```

But reducing latency should not undermine meaningful examination.

---

# Review burden

Review itself has cost.

High-friction processes can create:

```text
rubber-stamping

review avoidance

large batches

late feedback

process fatigue
```

Monad should therefore prefer:

> **the lightest Review process that provides adequate assurance for the consequence involved.**

---

# Small changes

A tiny low-risk change may need only:

```text
self-review

automated checks
```

For example:

```text
fix documentation typo
```

Forcing a formal architecture Review would add cost without corresponding value.

---

# Large architectural changes

A change to:

```text
semantic identity

authority model

canonical persistence

constitutional rules

migration semantics
```

deserves much deeper Review because errors may shape the entire system.

---

# Review before implementation

Some of the highest-value Reviews happen before code exists.

Examples:

```text
Requirement Review

architecture proposal Review

Specification Review

threat-model Review
```

Early Review reduces the cost of correcting foundational mistakes.

---

# Review during implementation

Iterative Review can expose problems while design remains flexible.

Examples:

```text
draft pull request

prototype Review

incremental architecture Review

schema Review before migration completion
```

This is often better than a single large Review at the end.

---

# Review after implementation

Post-implementation Review remains valuable for:

```text
code

operational readiness

security

release

Evidence

Verification results
```

Different lifecycle stages require different questions.

---

# Review after release

Post-release Review may examine:

```text
incident behavior

actual performance

operator experience

unexpected complexity

false assumptions

user impact
```

This feeds learning back into Requirements, architecture, and future work.

---

# Incident Review

Incident Review should emphasize learning and system improvement.

Useful questions include:

```text
What happened?

What conditions made it possible?

Why was it not detected earlier?

Which safeguards failed?

Which assumptions were wrong?

What Evidence exists?

What should change?
```

The goal is causal understanding, not simplistic blame assignment.

---

# Review of failures

Failures are especially valuable Review subjects.

A failed experiment, migration, release, or Verification may expose:

```text
incorrect assumptions

weak abstractions

missing observability

bad governance

inadequate testing

poor recovery semantics
```

Review should preserve that knowledge.

---

# Review and learning

A mature engineering loop is:

```text
Build
  ↓
Review
  ↓
Findings
  ↓
Change
  ↓
Verify
  ↓
Observe
  ↓
Learn
```

Review is therefore not just a gate.

It is one of the mechanisms through which the engineering system improves its own understanding.

---

# Review and traceability

A finding may trace across multiple artifacts.

Example:

```text
REV-032
   ↓ raised
FIND-104
   ↓ affects
SPEC-008
   ↓ causes
WP-021
   ↓ implemented-by
commit abc123
   ↓ verified-by
VER-044
   ↓ supported-by
EV-203
```

This preserves the reasoning chain from discovery to resolution.

---

# Review and requirements

Review findings may reveal that a Requirement itself is wrong.

For example:

```text
Finding:
Requirement assumes all Subjects have files.
```

That may result in:

```text
Requirement revision
```

rather than an implementation fix.

Review should be allowed to challenge upstream assumptions.

---

# Review and decisions

A difficult Finding may require an [Architecture Decision](/docs/artifacts/decisions).

Example:

```text
Finding:
Semantic identity can either wrap native IDs
or use project-local stable IDs.

Resolution:
ADR-019
```

The Review identifies the issue.

The ADR records the decision.

---

# Review and experiments

When Reviewers disagree because Evidence is insufficient:

```text
Review disagreement
   ↓
Experiment
   ↓
Evidence
   ↓
Decision
```

This is preferable to resolving technical uncertainty through authority alone.

---

# Review and evidence

Review findings should cite Evidence where appropriate.

For example:

```text
Finding:
current index design becomes impractical
above approximately 1M edges.

Evidence:
EXP-014 benchmark results.
```

This is stronger than unsupported preference.

---

# Review and current system truth

A Review Finding is not automatically current System truth.

It may be:

```text
proposed

contested

accepted

resolved

obsolete

superseded
```

The authoritative [System](/docs/system) documentation should reflect conclusions that have actually become current commitments.

---

# Review and Building Monad

The [Building Monad](/docs/building-monad) narrative may explain:

```text
why a Review happened

what disagreements emerged

how thinking changed

what lessons were learned
```

The Review artifact preserves the inspectable engineering record.

The narrative interprets that history.

---

# Review and native authority

Review systems such as GitHub may own native states like:

```text
APPROVED

CHANGES_REQUESTED

COMMENTED
```

Monad should preserve those states as native facts.

It may additionally model:

```text
semantic findings

governance meaning

artifact relationships

Review scope

resolution state
```

without pretending those concepts are identical.

---

# Review and authorization

A GitHub account may have permission to approve a pull request.

That means:

```text
native capability exists
```

It does not automatically imply:

```text
Monad Policy authorizes this Actor
to approve this engineering change.
```

Native permission and Monad Authority remain distinct.

---

# Review security

Reviews may contain sensitive information.

Examples:

```text
vulnerability details

credentials accidentally exposed in diffs

private architecture

incident data

customer information
```

Review systems should preserve appropriate access controls and avoid copying sensitive content unnecessarily.

---

# Review context

Reviewers need enough context to judge the Subject.

Relevant context may include:

```text
Requirement

Specification

architecture

related decisions

previous findings

current implementation

known constraints

recent changes

Evidence
```

But context should remain purpose-specific.

Dumping the entire repository into every Review is neither efficient nor necessarily safer.

---

# Context quality matters

A reviewer operating with incomplete context may produce incorrect findings.

Monad's Context system may eventually help assemble:

```text
the smallest sufficient review context
```

while preserving provenance and authority.

---

# Review independence and context

Independent Review does not mean context-free Review.

A reviewer should understand:

```text
the problem

constraints

existing decisions

system invariants
```

Independence concerns judgment and authority, not ignorance.

---

# Review reproducibility

Human Review is not perfectly reproducible.

But a future engineer should still be able to reconstruct:

```text
what was examined

what information was available

what criteria were used

why findings were raised

how they were resolved
```

That is sufficient for durable engineering history.

---

# Review determinism

Automated Review tools may be deterministic.

AI Review may be probabilistic.

Human Review is judgment-based.

The Review record should not falsely present these methods as epistemically identical.

---

# Review method provenance

A Review should identify its method where that distinction matters.

Examples:

```text
Manual architecture review

Static security scanner

LLM-assisted Specification review

Peer code review

Independent operational review
```

This helps interpret the strength and limitations of the result.

---

# Automated review

Automation can examine:

```text
dependency rules

style

schema consistency

security patterns

API changes

documentation links

policy constraints
```

Automated Review is valuable when rules are sufficiently explicit.

It should not create the illusion that all engineering judgment has been automated.

---

# Static analysis as Review

A static analyzer may produce Finding-like outputs.

Example:

```text
Finding:
untrusted input reaches shell execution.

Producer:
security scanner
```

Monad can model this within the same general Finding system while preserving the tool's provenance.

---

# Review aggregation

A high-level Review may aggregate findings from several methods.

For example:

```text
Security Review
    │
    ├── static analyzer
    ├── dependency scanner
    ├── manual threat review
    └── penetration test
```

The aggregate Review should preserve the underlying Evidence and findings.

---

# Review disagreement with automation

An automated tool may raise a Finding that a human rejects.

Example:

```text
Tool:
possible SQL injection

Human:
false positive because parameterized query
is enforced by lower-level API
```

The rejection and rationale should remain inspectable.

This helps improve future tooling.

---

# Repeated false positives

If a Review method repeatedly produces false positives, that is useful system Evidence.

It may justify:

```text
method refinement

configuration change

tool replacement

scope reduction
```

Review infrastructure itself should evolve based on observed value.

---

# Review anti-pattern: approval theater

A process where reviewers simply click:

```text
Approve
```

without meaningful examination creates the appearance of governance without assurance.

Monad should prefer explicit evidence of useful examination over ceremony.

---

# Review anti-pattern: style-only Review

A Review focused entirely on:

```text
naming

formatting

minor style preferences
```

while ignoring:

```text
correctness

architecture

risk

failure semantics
```

may waste reviewer attention.

Automate mechanical concerns where practical.

Preserve human attention for judgment.

---

# Review anti-pattern: drive-by comments

Comments without:

```text
context

rationale

consequence

actionability
```

can create noise rather than improve the system.

A good Finding explains why the issue matters.

---

# Review anti-pattern: late architecture Review

Reviewing architecture only after implementation is effectively complete makes structural changes expensive.

Architecture Review should occur while architecture is still changeable.

---

# Review anti-pattern: review everything equally

Treating:

```text
README typo
```

and:

```text
constitutional authority model change
```

as equivalent creates process dysfunction.

Review depth should reflect consequence.

---

# Review anti-pattern: hidden findings

Important Review conclusions should not remain only in:

```text
private messages

ephemeral calls

unrecorded meetings
```

If a Finding affects engineering decisions, preserve it somewhere durable enough to survive the conversation.

---

# Review anti-pattern: finding deletion

A finding that is resolved should not simply disappear.

Historical findings explain:

```text
why code changed

why architecture changed

why a decision exists

why a release was delayed
```

Resolution is better than erasure.

---

# Review anti-pattern: authority by persistence

A Finding does not become authoritative merely because nobody responded to it.

Unresolved is not accepted.

Old is not true.

Repeated is not canonical.

---

# Review anti-pattern: consensus as correctness

Several reviewers agreeing does not guarantee correctness.

Consensus is useful social Evidence.

It is not a substitute for technical Evidence where technical Evidence is available.

---

# Review anti-pattern: reviewer omniscience

No reviewer should be assumed to understand every aspect of a complex system.

Good Review processes make it easy to escalate:

```text
I do not know.

This needs security expertise.

We need an experiment.

The Requirement is unclear.

The available Evidence is insufficient.
```

Explicit uncertainty is a strength.

---

# Review anti-pattern: unresolved questions hidden in prose

If a Review uncovers a real open question, preserve it explicitly.

For example:

```text
OPEN QUESTION:
Should semantic identity survive repository split?
```

Do not bury it inside a paragraph that future engineers will never discover.

---

# Review anti-pattern: no closure semantics

If findings can be raised but there is no way to know:

```text
resolved?

rejected?

deferred?

accepted risk?
```

the Review record becomes increasingly difficult to interpret.

Findings need enough lifecycle to support closure.

---

# Review anti-pattern: no revision binding

A Review that says:

```text
looks good
```

without identifying what revision was examined is fragile.

Materially changing the Subject should make re-review requirements visible.

---

# Review anti-pattern: using Review to invent Requirements

Reviewers may discover missing Requirements.

They should not silently encode them into implementation comments.

The proper flow may be:

```text
Review Finding
   ↓
Requirement update
   ↓
Specification update
   ↓
Implementation
```

This preserves authority and traceability.

---

# Review quality criteria

A strong Review should be:

```text
scoped

revision-aware

purposeful

risk-proportional

traceable

clear about findings

clear about uncertainty

supported by Evidence where appropriate

independent where consequence requires it

connected to follow-up work

historically preservable
```

---

# A Review should answer a question

Before creating a Review, ask:

> **What uncertainty, risk, or engineering judgment requires examination?**

Examples:

```text
Does this Specification define enough behavior to implement safely?

Does this architecture preserve authority boundaries?

Could this migration lose semantic identity?

Does this release contain unresolved critical risk?

Does the Evidence actually support the performance Claim?

Is this interface likely to become an irreversible compatibility burden?
```

If the question is unclear, the Review process probably will be too.

---

# Minimum viable Review model

Monad does not need an elaborate enterprise Review platform to begin.

A useful minimum is:

```text
Review ID

Subject

Revision

Review Type

Reviewer

Findings

Outcome

Provenance
```

For significant findings:

```text
Finding ID

Description

Severity

Disposition
```

This is enough to preserve much more engineering meaning than unstructured comments alone.

---

# Review maturity

A reasonable progression is:

```text
Stage 1
Native review links and durable notes

Stage 2
Structured Review identity and findings

Stage 3
Revision-aware Review

Stage 4
Finding lifecycle and traceability

Stage 5
Policy-driven Review obligations

Stage 6
Impact-aware re-review

Stage 7
Cross-system human + machine Review graph
```

Each stage should earn its complexity.

---

# Review invariants

The Review system should preserve several distinctions:

```text
Review ≠ Approval

Review ≠ Verification

Comment ≠ Review

Finding ≠ Decision

Finding ≠ Requirement

Native review state ≠ semantic Review state

Reviewer opinion ≠ truth

Resolved ≠ erased

Rejected ≠ deleted

Accepted Risk ≠ fixed

Old Review ≠ current Review

Review completion ≠ finding resolution

Native permission ≠ Monad Authority

Automated finding ≠ authoritative conclusion

Consensus ≠ proof
```

# Example: Specification Review

```text
Review:
REV-031

Type:
Specification Review

Subject:
SPEC-008@v4

Finding:
Failure behavior is undefined when
native identity resolution returns
multiple plausible Subjects.

Severity:
Major

Disposition:
Resolved

Resolution:
SPEC-008@v5 introduces
AmbiguousIdentity error semantics.
```

The Review explains why the Specification changed.

---

# Example: Architecture Review

```text
Review:
REV-044

Subject:
Projection persistence architecture

Finding:
Proposal allows derived projection state
to become authoritative after native
source deletion.

Severity:
Blocker

Rationale:
Violates canonical/derived separation.

Disposition:
Accepted

Follow-up:
ADR-021
```

The Review surfaces the issue.

The ADR records the architectural decision.

---

# Example: rejected Finding

```text
Finding:
FIND-118

Claim:
The filesystem adapter should own
semantic identity because it observes paths.

Disposition:
Rejected

Rationale:
Path is a Native Reference property.
Owning semantic identity in the adapter
would couple Subject continuity to location.
```

The Finding remains historically useful even though it was rejected.

---

# Example: accepted risk

```text
Finding:
Windows filesystem event semantics
have not been verified.

Severity:
Major

Disposition:
Accepted Risk

Scope:
M0 preview only

Constraint:
Linux and macOS supported

Expiration:
Before Windows support milestone
```

The unresolved condition remains explicit.

---

# Example: AI-assisted Review

```text
Review:
REV-052

Method:
AI-assisted Specification comparison

Subject:
Implementation of SPEC-012

Candidate Finding:
Implementation accepts ambiguous Subject
resolution without escalation.

Human Review:
Confirmed

Disposition:
Resolved

Verification:
VER-088
```

AI helped discover the issue.

Human confirmation and Verification established the final assurance chain.

---

# Example: conflicting Reviews

```text
REV-061
Architecture Reviewer:
Split Identity and Reconciliation components.

REV-062
Operations Reviewer:
Do not split yet because the operational
boundary would add complexity without
independent lifecycle needs.
```

A useful response is:

```text
Architecture question opened.

Evidence requested.

Prototype or ADR required.
```

Not:

```text
majority vote wins automatically.
```

# Example: Review to remediation

```text
REV-071
   ↓
FIND-209
   ↓
WP-044
   ↓
commit 7ac91e
   ↓
VER-117
   ↓
EV-302
   ↓
FIND-209 resolved
```

This is an inspectable path from discovery to correction.

# Example: Release Review

```text
Release:
RC-4

Architecture Review:
Complete

Security Review:
Complete

Migration Review:
Complete

Verification:
1 incomplete item

Known Risks:
2 accepted
```


is not the same as:

```text
Approved
```

# Review traceability

A mature trace may look like:

```text
Requirement
    ↓
Specification
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Finding
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Work Package
    ↓
Implementation
    ↓
Verification
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Finding Resolution
    ↓
Release
```

This turns Review from ephemeral commentary into part of the engineering record.

---

# Review as part of Monad's control loop

Reviews participate in the larger engineering loop:

```text
Understand
    ↓
Define
    ↓
Design
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Decide
    ↓
Implement
    ↓
Verify
    ↓
Release
    ↓
Observe
    ↓
Review again when reality demands it
```

Review is not a single checkpoint.

It is a recurring mechanism for challenging engineering assumptions.

---

# The governing principle

> **A Review should preserve enough structure that future engineers can understand what was examined, which concerns were raised, why those concerns mattered, what happened to them, and how the Review influenced the system.**

The goal is not to maximize Review artifacts.

The goal is to keep important engineering judgment from disappearing into transient conversations, tool-specific comments, or unexplained decisions.

Reviews are valuable when they make the engineering process more inspectable, more falsifiable, and more capable of learning.
