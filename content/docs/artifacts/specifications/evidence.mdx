# Evidence Specification

**Specification ID:** `SPEC-EVD-001`
**Status:** Draft
**Version:** `0.1.0`
**Normative:** Yes
**Primary requirements:** `REQ-EVD-*`
**Related requirements:** `REQ-CLM-*`, `REQ-PROV-*`, `REQ-SEM-*`, `REQ-ID-*`, `REQ-AUTH-*`, `REQ-GOV-*`, `REQ-VER-*`, `REQ-REC-*`, `REQ-PORT-*`
**Parent specification:** `SPEC-SEM-001 — Semantic Kernel Specification`

## 1. Purpose

This specification defines the semantic contract for **Evidence** in Monad.

Engineering systems routinely make assertions such as:

```text
The requirement is satisfied.
```

```text
The implementation behaves correctly.
```

```text
This component caused the failure.
```

```text
The release is safe to promote.
```

```text
This architecture decision remains valid.
```

Those assertions become useful only when they can be connected to inspectable material that supports, contradicts, qualifies, or otherwise informs them.

Monad calls that material **Evidence**.

The governing distinction is:

> **Evidence is material that bears on a Claim. It is not the Claim itself, and its existence does not make the Claim true.**

Evidence may include:

* test results,
* source code,
* runtime traces,
* logs,
* measurements,
* documents,
* commits,
* review findings,
* experiment results,
* build artifacts,
* deployment observations,
* screenshots,
* configuration snapshots,
* native-system records,
* human observations,
* AI-generated analyses,
* external references.

The Evidence model MUST preserve enough context to answer questions such as:

* What exactly is this Evidence?
* Where did it come from?
* What Claim does it bear on?
* Does it support or contradict that Claim?
* For what revision, environment, and time is it relevant?
* Has it become stale?
* Can it still be inspected?
* Has it been transformed?
* Is it independent from other Evidence?
* Is it valid?
* How strong is its relevance?
* Who or what produced it?
* Can it be reproduced?
* Has it been superseded or invalidated?

The purpose of this specification is not to manufacture certainty.

It is to make the basis of engineering belief inspectable.

# 4. Core Evidence Model

Conceptually:

```text
Evidence
 ├── identity
 ├── type
 ├── subject matter
 ├── source
 ├── representation
 ├── provenance
 ├── scope
 ├── temporal semantics
 ├── revision binding
 ├── integrity information
 ├── lifecycle
 └── relationships to Claims
```

A minimal Evidence record conceptually requires:

```text
identity
type
source or representation
```

A consequential Evidence record SHOULD additionally preserve:

```text
provenance
scope
time
revision
integrity
lifecycle
```

# 5. Core Evidence Invariants

Conforming implementations MUST preserve the following distinctions.

## 5.1 Evidence ≠ Claim

Evidence is not itself the proposition being asserted.

Example:

```text
Claim:
    Login succeeds with valid credentials.

Evidence:
    Passing integration-test execution.
```

The passing test does not become the Claim.

---

## 5.2 Evidence ≠ proof

Evidence may strongly support a Claim without constituting mathematical or logical proof.

Monad MUST NOT describe ordinary empirical Evidence as proof unless the underlying domain justifies that terminology.

---

## 5.3 Evidence ≠ truth

The existence of Evidence does not make the associated Claim true.

Evidence may be:

* mistaken,
* stale,
* corrupted,
* irrelevant,
* incomplete,
* misleading,
* contradicted.

---

## 5.4 Evidence ≠ authority

Evidence does not possess authority merely because it exists.

Authority determines who or what may legitimately establish state.

Evidence informs belief about that state.

---

## 5.5 Evidence ≠ provenance

Provenance explains where Evidence came from and how it was produced.

Evidence is the material itself.

---

## 5.6 Evidence ≠ verification

Verification evaluates an expectation against Evidence.

Evidence is an input to Verification.

---

## 5.7 Evidence ≠ approval

An approval may rely on Evidence.

An approval is not itself automatically Evidence that the underlying engineering state is correct.

---

## 5.8 Validity ≠ relevance

Evidence may be authentic and correctly captured while still being irrelevant to a particular Claim.

---

## 5.9 Freshness ≠ validity

Old Evidence is not automatically invalid.

Fresh Evidence is not automatically correct.

---

## 5.10 Absence of Evidence ≠ Evidence of absence

Unless the observation process is known to be sufficiently complete, failure to observe something MUST NOT automatically be treated as proof that it does not exist.

---

# 6. First-Class Evidence

Evidence that materially affects:

* verification,
* governance,
* release,
* incident analysis,
* reconciliation,
* architecture decisions,
* compliance,
* AI reasoning,
* historical reconstruction

SHOULD be representable as a first-class semantic entity.

First-class Evidence SHOULD support stable identity and durable reference.

---

# 7. Evidence Identity

Evidence requiring durable reference MUST possess stable semantic identity.

Evidence identity MUST NOT depend solely on:

* current file path,
* database row number,
* UI location,
* array index,
* temporary URL,
* native-system display text.

---

# 8. Evidence Identity Across Movement

Moving an Evidence Representation MUST NOT automatically create new Evidence identity when the underlying Evidence remains semantically unchanged.

Example:

```text
artifacts/test-results/run-42.json
```

moved to:

```text
evidence/tests/run-42.json
```

does not by itself create new Evidence.

---

# 9. Evidence Identity Across Regeneration

Regenerating materially identical Evidence MAY preserve identity if the domain defines the regenerated material as the same Evidence instance.

Otherwise, regeneration SHOULD create a new Evidence instance linked through provenance.

The distinction MUST be domain-defined rather than accidental.

---

# 10. Evidence Representation

Evidence MAY have one or more Representations.

Examples:

```text
raw JSON result
human-readable HTML report
database record
compressed archive
rendered chart
```

Multiple Representations MUST NOT automatically create multiple Evidence Subjects.

---

# 11. Evidence Content

Evidence MAY contain:

* structured data,
* unstructured text,
* binary artifacts,
* references,
* measurements,
* images,
* logs,
* traces,
* metadata,
* collections.

The Evidence model MUST NOT assume Evidence is textual.

---

# 12. Evidence Type

Every Evidence instance SHOULD have a type sufficiently precise for consumers to interpret its meaning.

Candidate Evidence types include:

```text
test-result
runtime-observation
build-result
deployment-record
source-snapshot
configuration-snapshot
benchmark-result
experiment-result
review-finding
log-extract
trace
metric-sample
human-observation
ai-analysis
external-reference
documentary-record
artifact-hash
```

The final vocabulary belongs to the Schema Catalog.

---

# 13. Evidence Type Extensibility

Domains MAY define additional Evidence types.

Unknown extension types SHOULD remain preservable during import/export.

Implementations MUST NOT silently coerce unknown Evidence types into misleading generic types.

---

# 14. Evidence Source

Evidence SHOULD identify the source from which it was obtained or generated.

A source MAY be:

* file,
* native-system object,
* API response,
* database query,
* execution,
* Actor observation,
* external document,
* device,
* instrumentation system.

---

# 15. Source ≠ Evidence

The source that produced Evidence is not necessarily the Evidence itself.

Example:

```text
Source:
    CI system

Evidence:
    test execution result
```

---

# 16. Evidence Provenance

Consequential Evidence MUST preserve provenance sufficient to explain:

* producer,
* source,
* collection method,
* transformation,
* time,
* relevant execution,
* imported origin.

---

# 17. Producer

Evidence provenance SHOULD identify the producer.

A producer MAY be:

* human,
* AI model,
* test runner,
* compiler,
* CI system,
* telemetry agent,
* integration adapter,
* external provider.

---

# 18. Requested Origin vs Resolved Origin

Where Evidence is retrieved through a reference, provenance SHOULD preserve the distinction between:

```text
requested origin
```

and:

```text
resolved origin
```

when redirects, aliases, mirrors, provider mappings, or repository movement affect interpretation.

---

# 19. Evidence Transformation

If Evidence is transformed after collection, the transformation SHOULD preserve derivation provenance.

Examples:

```text
raw log → filtered log extract
trace set → aggregate latency report
JSON results → HTML test report
```

---

# 20. Lossy Transformation

Lossy transformation MUST be identifiable.

A summarized Evidence artifact MUST NOT be treated as informationally equivalent to the complete source unless that equivalence is justified.

---

# 21. Evidence Scope

Evidence MUST support sufficient scope to determine where it applies.

Scope MAY include:

```text
Subject
Claim
Requirement
revision
commit
branch
repository
workspace
release
environment
deployment
execution
platform
configuration
time interval
```

---

# 22. Revision Binding

Evidence whose meaning depends on system revision SHOULD explicitly identify that revision.

Example:

```text
Test result:
    revision: commit abc123
```

A passing result for `abc123` MUST NOT automatically verify `def456`.

---

# 23. Environment Binding

Evidence MAY be valid only in a particular environment.

Example:

```text
environment: production
```

or:

```text
environment: linux-x86_64-ci
```

Environment-dependent Evidence MUST NOT silently be generalized beyond its supported scope.

---

# 24. Configuration Binding

Where configuration materially affects results, Evidence SHOULD preserve the relevant configuration identity or snapshot.

---

# 25. Input Binding

Evidence produced by execution SHOULD preserve relevant inputs where necessary for interpretation or reproduction.

---

# 26. Tool Binding

Where tool versions affect Evidence, the tool identity and version SHOULD be retained.

---

# 27. Temporal Semantics

Evidence SHOULD distinguish at least:

```text
observedAt
recordedAt
```

when those times may differ.

---

# 28. Valid Time

Evidence MAY describe a state that was valid during a time interval independent of when the Evidence was collected.

For example:

```text
configuration snapshot valid at deployment time T
```

---

# 29. Evidence Freshness

Evidence freshness describes whether Evidence remains sufficiently current for a particular use.

Freshness MUST be evaluated relative to:

* Claim,
* scope,
* risk,
* revision,
* policy,
* domain.

---

# 30. Freshness Is Contextual

Evidence that is stale for release authorization MAY remain perfectly valid for historical analysis.

Therefore, Evidence SHOULD NOT possess one universal freshness boolean.

---

# 31. Freshness Policy

Policies MAY specify maximum acceptable age or revision distance for particular Evidence uses.

Example:

```text
Production deployment verification requires security scan Evidence
from the release candidate revision.
```

---

# 32. Evidence-to-Claim Relationship

Evidence MUST be able to bear an explicit semantic relationship to a Claim.

Common relationships include:

```text
supports
contradicts
qualifies
corroborates
weakens
```

---

# 33. Support

`supports` means the Evidence increases justification for accepting a Claim within a defined scope.

It MUST NOT mean:

```text
Claim proven true
```

unless the domain explicitly establishes proof semantics.

---

# 34. Contradiction

`contradicts` means the Evidence provides material reason to reject or weaken a Claim.

Contradictory Evidence MUST remain representable alongside supporting Evidence.

---

# 35. Qualification

Evidence MAY qualify a Claim by narrowing the scope in which it should be accepted.

Example:

```text
Claim:
    Search latency is below 100 ms.

Evidence:
    benchmark shows <100 ms only for datasets below 1M records.
```

This Evidence may qualify rather than simply support or contradict the Claim.

---

# 36. Corroboration

Independent Evidence MAY corroborate another Evidence-backed conclusion.

Corroboration SHOULD preserve source independence information where material.

---

# 37. Evidence Relevance

Evidence relevance describes how directly Evidence bears on a particular Claim.

Relevance MAY depend on:

* subject matter,
* revision,
* environment,
* time,
* causal proximity,
* measurement method,
* domain assumptions.

---

# 38. Relevance Is Claim-Specific

An Evidence instance MAY be highly relevant to one Claim and irrelevant to another.

Therefore relevance SHOULD be represented in the relationship between Evidence and Claim where appropriate rather than solely as a property of Evidence.

---

# 39. Evidence Validity

Evidence validity concerns whether the Evidence itself can be relied upon as an accurate record of what it purports to represent.

Validity may depend on:

* collection integrity,
* source reliability,
* corruption,
* measurement correctness,
* tool defects,
* transformation errors.

---

# 40. Invalid Evidence

Invalid Evidence SHOULD remain historically inspectable where it influenced consequential engineering decisions.

Invalidation SHOULD NOT normally delete the Evidence.

---

# 41. Evidence Strength

Evidence strength MAY be evaluated using multiple independent dimensions.

Possible dimensions include:

```text
directness
relevance
freshness
source reliability
reproducibility
independence
coverage
precision
integrity
```

Monad SHOULD avoid reducing all Evidence quality to one universal scalar score unless a domain-specific model justifies it.

---

# 42. Direct Evidence

Direct Evidence bears on a Claim without requiring substantial intermediate inference.

Example:

```text
Claim:
    Endpoint returns HTTP 200 for valid request.

Direct Evidence:
    captured integration-test response.
```

---

# 43. Indirect Evidence

Indirect Evidence requires additional reasoning.

Example:

```text
source code inspection suggests endpoint should return 200
```

when runtime behavior has not been observed.

Indirect Evidence MAY still be valuable.

It MUST remain distinguishable from direct observation where the distinction matters.

---

# 44. Primary Evidence

Primary Evidence originates directly from the event, execution, observation, or source being evaluated.

Examples:

* raw trace,
* raw test result,
* original source snapshot.

---

# 45. Derived Evidence

Derived Evidence is produced from other Evidence.

Examples:

* summary report,
* aggregated metric,
* statistical analysis,
* AI synthesis,
* filtered log extract.

Derived Evidence SHOULD retain derivation provenance.

---

# 46. Primary ≠ superior in all cases

Primary Evidence may be more direct but difficult to interpret.

Derived Evidence may provide essential analytical value.

Monad MUST NOT assume all primary Evidence is automatically stronger than all derived Evidence.

---

# 47. Independent Evidence

Evidence sources SHOULD be considered independent only when their production paths are sufficiently separate.

Two reports generated from the same underlying test output are not necessarily independent corroboration.

---

# 48. Evidence Dependence

Derived Evidence SHOULD preserve links to upstream Evidence so consumers can identify correlated or duplicated support.

---

# 49. Duplicate Evidence

Equivalent Evidence imported from multiple Representations SHOULD not automatically be counted as multiple independent confirmations.

---

# 50. Negative Evidence

Evidence MAY indicate failure, nonconformance, absence, contradiction, or inability to satisfy an expectation.

Negative Evidence is first-class Evidence.

---

# 51. Evidence of Absence

Evidence MAY legitimately support absence when the observation method establishes sufficient coverage.

Example:

```text
Claim:
    No unauthorized dependencies exist.

Evidence:
    complete dependency scan over declared dependency graph.
```

The completeness assumption MUST be inspectable.

---

# 52. Observation Failure

Failure to collect Evidence MUST remain distinguishable from Evidence showing failure of the system being observed.

Example:

```text
monitor unavailable
```

does not mean:

```text
service unavailable
```

---

# 53. Unknown Evidence State

If the system cannot determine whether expected Evidence exists, the state SHOULD remain:

```text
unknown
```

rather than being coerced into:

```text
missing
```

or:

```text
passed
```

---

# 54. Evidence Completeness

Evidence MAY have known coverage boundaries.

Example:

```text
tests cover:
    Linux
    Node 24
    PostgreSQL 18
```

The Evidence MUST NOT be generalized to unsupported environments without justification.

---

# 55. Evidence Coverage

Coverage MAY refer to:

* requirements,
* branches,
* code paths,
* environments,
* scenarios,
* systems,
* time intervals,
* input classes.

Coverage metrics SHOULD identify what was actually measured.

---

# 56. Evidence Bundle

Multiple Evidence instances MAY be grouped into an Evidence Bundle.

Example:

```text
Release Evidence Bundle
 ├── unit-test results
 ├── integration-test results
 ├── security scan
 ├── build provenance
 ├── deployment rehearsal
 └── review findings
```

---

# 57. Evidence Bundle Identity

A consequential Evidence Bundle SHOULD have stable identity.

---

# 58. Bundle Membership

Bundle membership MUST NOT imply that every contained Evidence item supports the same Claim.

Each Evidence-to-Claim relationship remains independently meaningful.

---

# 59. Bundle Completeness

An Evidence Bundle MAY declare expected Evidence classes.

Example:

```text
required:
    test-result
    security-scan
    reproducibility-check
```

Missing members SHOULD be observable.

---

# 60. Evidence Aggregation

Aggregated Evidence SHOULD preserve sufficient links to its constituent Evidence to explain how the aggregate was produced.

---

# 61. Evidence Lifecycle

Evidence MAY have lifecycle states such as:

```text
captured
validated
active
stale
superseded
invalidated
archived
unavailable
```

Lifecycle state MUST remain distinct from Evidence content.

---

# 62. Captured Evidence

Evidence MAY enter the system before full validation.

Such Evidence SHOULD be distinguishable from validated Evidence.

---

# 63. Evidence Validation

Validation MAY determine:

* structural integrity,
* source resolution,
* digest verification,
* required provenance,
* schema validity,
* expected scope.

Validation does not establish Claim truth.

---

# 64. Stale Evidence

Evidence MAY become stale when relevant system state changes.

Staleness SHOULD preserve the Evidence rather than destroy it.

---

# 65. Superseded Evidence

New Evidence MAY supersede older Evidence for current decision-making.

The earlier Evidence remains historically relevant.

---

# 66. Evidence Invalidation

Evidence SHOULD be invalidated when it is discovered that:

* source was corrupted,
* wrong revision was tested,
* measurement instrument failed,
* execution was misconfigured,
* provenance was incorrect,
* transformation was defective.

---

# 67. Evidence Correction

Metadata errors SHOULD be correctable while preserving correction history where consequential.

---

# 68. Evidence Retraction

An Actor MAY retract an Evidence submission.

Retraction SHOULD preserve:

* original submission,
* retracting Actor,
* time,
* reason.

---

# 69. Evidence Deletion

Permanent Evidence deletion SHOULD be exceptional when the Evidence has participated in consequential:

* release decisions,
* approvals,
* incidents,
* verification,
* governance.

Retention and privacy requirements may nevertheless require deletion or redaction.

---

# 70. Evidence Integrity

Evidence MAY carry integrity metadata such as:

```text
content hash
digest algorithm
size
signature
timestamp
```

---

# 71. Integrity ≠ truth

A matching hash demonstrates content stability relative to the hashed artifact.

It does not demonstrate that the Evidence is true, relevant, or correctly interpreted.

---

# 72. Signatures

Cryptographic signatures MAY establish properties such as origin or tamper evidence.

Signatures MUST NOT automatically confer semantic authority.

---

# 73. Evidence Immutability

Evidence content representing an observed historical event SHOULD normally be immutable.

Corrections SHOULD generally create:

* corrected metadata,
* new Evidence,
* explicit invalidation,

rather than rewriting the original observation invisibly.

---

# 74. Content-Addressed Evidence

Evidence MAY use content-addressed storage.

Content addressing MUST remain distinct from Evidence semantic identity.

Two Evidence instances with identical bytes MAY have different provenance and therefore remain distinct semantic Evidence records.

---

# 75. Evidence Provenance Graph

Derived Evidence MAY form provenance chains.

Example:

```text
raw test output
    →
parsed test result
    →
coverage summary
    →
release dashboard
```

Consumers SHOULD be able to inspect material upstream derivation.

---

# 76. Evidence and Claims

One Evidence instance MAY bear on multiple Claims.

One Claim MAY be supported or contradicted by multiple Evidence instances.

---

# 77. Conflicting Evidence

Monad MUST support simultaneous presence of:

```text
supporting Evidence
```

and:

```text
contradicting Evidence
```

for the same Claim.

The model MUST NOT require premature forced consensus.

---

# 78. Evidence Resolution

Resolving conflicting Evidence is an interpretive or governance process.

The storage layer MUST NOT automatically discard lower-priority Evidence solely because another Evidence item is considered stronger.

---

# 79. Evidence and Authority

Authority MAY determine:

* who may submit Evidence,
* which Evidence classes satisfy obligations,
* who may validate Evidence,
* what Evidence is required for authorization.

Authority MUST NOT determine factual truth merely by declaration.

---

# 80. Evidence and Policy

Policies MAY define Evidence obligations.

Example:

```text
Before production deployment:
    require passing integration-test Evidence
    require security-scan Evidence
    require release approval
```

---

# 81. Evidence Obligation

An Evidence Obligation SHOULD identify:

* required Evidence type,
* applicable Claim or operation,
* scope,
* freshness,
* revision binding,
* minimum validation state.

---

# 82. Missing Required Evidence

Missing required Evidence SHOULD produce a distinct governance result.

It SHOULD NOT be silently treated as:

```text
policy denied because system failed
```

when the actual state is:

```text
required Evidence unavailable
```

---

# 83. Evidence and Approval

An approval MAY reference the Evidence considered by the approving Actor.

That relationship SHOULD remain revision-aware.

---

# 84. Approval Freshness

An approval based on Evidence for revision A SHOULD NOT automatically authorize revision B if material changes invalidate the Evidence basis.

---

# 85. Evidence and Verification

Verification MUST identify the Evidence used to evaluate an expectation.

A verification result without inspectable Evidence SHOULD be considered weaker than one with durable Evidence unless the verification mechanism itself provides equivalent inspectability.

---

# 86. Verification Result ≠ Evidence Set

The Verification result and the Evidence used by the Verification MUST remain conceptually distinct.

Example:

```text
Verification:
    PASSED

Evidence:
    test run 241
    benchmark 83
    static analysis 912
```

---

# 87. Evidence Reuse

Existing Evidence MAY be reused across multiple Verification activities if:

* applicable scope matches,
* revision remains relevant,
* freshness requirements are satisfied,
* integrity remains valid.

---

# 88. Evidence Invalidating Verification

If Evidence underlying a Verification is invalidated, affected Verification results SHOULD become discoverable as potentially invalid or stale.

---

# 89. Evidence and Requirements

Evidence MAY support Claims that requirements have been satisfied.

Evidence MUST NOT directly convert a requirement into a satisfied state without applicable Verification or policy semantics.

---

# 90. Evidence and Specifications

Specifications MAY define required Evidence for conformance.

Example:

```text
SPEC-X clause 12:
    implementation MUST preserve deterministic serialization.

Required Evidence:
    conformance test output.
```

---

# 91. Evidence and Decisions

Architecture or engineering decisions SHOULD be able to reference Evidence supporting or challenging their rationale.

---

# 92. Decision Reassessment

New Evidence MAY weaken or invalidate assumptions underlying an existing Decision.

Monad SHOULD preserve this connection without automatically superseding the Decision unless governance rules require it.

---

# 93. Evidence and Experiments

Experiment results SHOULD be represented as Evidence rather than only embedded in narrative conclusions.

---

# 94. Experiment Evidence

Experiment Evidence SHOULD preserve:

* experiment identity,
* hypothesis,
* method,
* inputs,
* environment,
* observations,
* producer,
* time,
* relevant raw data.

---

# 95. Negative Experiment Results

Evidence that falsifies a hypothesis is valuable and MUST remain first-class.

---

# 96. Evidence and Reviews

Reviews MAY generate Evidence such as:

* findings,
* annotations,
* inspected artifact snapshots,
* reviewer observations.

A Review itself SHOULD remain distinct from the Evidence it produces.

---

# 97. Review Finding

A Finding MAY serve as Evidence for a Claim.

Its relevance, authority, and validity remain separately evaluable.

---

# 98. Evidence and Execution

Executions SHOULD be able to produce durable Evidence.

Examples:

```text
command result
tool output
changed-state observation
artifact hash
exit status
deployment receipt
```

---

# 99. Execution Success ≠ Verification Evidence Sufficiency

An execution returning success MAY be Evidence that the tool completed successfully.

It MUST NOT automatically prove that the intended engineering outcome was achieved.

---

# 100. Post-Execution Evidence

Consequential operations SHOULD collect post-execution Evidence where necessary to verify actual outcome.

---

# 101. Evidence and Reconciliation

Reconciliation SHOULD be able to use Evidence from:

* canonical model,
* native observations,
* execution results,
* historical state.

---

# 102. Reconciliation Evidence

Evidence used in reconciliation SHOULD preserve observation freshness and source authority.

A stale observation SHOULD NOT silently override newer authoritative state.

---

# 103. Evidence and Releases

A Release SHOULD be able to reference an Evidence Bundle supporting its release state.

---

# 104. Release Evidence

Release Evidence MAY include:

```text
build provenance
test results
verification results
security scans
compatibility checks
artifact digests
approvals
reproducibility checks
```

---

# 105. Release Evidence Binding

Release Evidence MUST be bound to the actual release candidate or immutable artifact set where required.

Evidence for a previous candidate MUST NOT silently transfer to a materially different candidate.

---

# 106. Evidence and Deployment

Deployment Evidence MAY include:

* provider deployment identifier,
* deployed artifact digest,
* target environment,
* resulting runtime revision,
* health observations.

Release Evidence and Deployment Evidence MUST remain distinguishable.

---

# 107. Runtime Evidence

Runtime Evidence MAY include:

```text
logs
metrics
traces
health probes
events
state snapshots
```

---

# 108. Runtime Observation Scope

Runtime Evidence SHOULD preserve enough context to identify:

* service,
* environment,
* deployment,
* time interval,
* instance population where relevant.

---

# 109. Metrics as Evidence

Metrics MAY serve as Evidence when the collection methodology and scope are sufficiently understood.

A dashboard visualization alone SHOULD NOT be assumed to contain all required Evidence provenance.

---

# 110. Logs as Evidence

Logs MAY serve as Evidence.

Log content SHOULD preserve or reference:

* source,
* time,
* system identity,
* collection method,
* relevant retention information.

---

# 111. Traces as Evidence

Distributed traces MAY provide Evidence about runtime interactions.

A trace showing:

```text
A → B
```

may support a runtime-call Claim.

It does not automatically establish architectural necessity or causality.

---

# 112. Source Code as Evidence

Source code MAY be Evidence for Claims about implementation structure.

Source code MAY be weaker Evidence for runtime behavior than direct runtime observation.

The system SHOULD preserve that distinction.

---

# 113. Documentation as Evidence

Documentation MAY serve as Evidence of:

* intended behavior,
* declared architecture,
* historical decision,
* documented process.

Documentation MUST NOT automatically be treated as Evidence of actual runtime behavior.

---

# 114. Git History as Evidence

Git history MAY provide Evidence of:

* revision ancestry,
* authored changes,
* commit timestamps,
* repository state.

Git history MUST NOT be treated as a complete record of semantic truth.

---

# 115. Issue Tracker Records as Evidence

Issue comments, state transitions, and assignments MAY serve as Evidence of coordination history.

An issue marked closed is not automatically Evidence that its underlying requirement is satisfied.

---

# 116. CI Evidence

CI results MAY provide Evidence about:

* test execution,
* build success,
* scans,
* checks.

A green CI run does not automatically prove release readiness.

---

# 117. External Evidence

Evidence MAY originate from external systems or documents outside Monad's control.

External Evidence SHOULD preserve durable citation information where possible.

---

# 118. Broken External References

If external Evidence becomes unavailable, Monad SHOULD preserve:

* historical reference,
* last-known metadata,
* provenance,
* unavailable state.

It MUST NOT pretend the Evidence remains inspectable if it no longer is.

---

# 119. Evidence Snapshotting

Policies MAY require snapshotting externally hosted Evidence when long-term inspectability matters.

---

# 120. Evidence Retention Strategy

Retention MAY choose among:

```text
full content
immutable snapshot
content-addressed artifact
provider reference
metadata-only record
```

The chosen strategy SHOULD be explicit.

---

# 121. Evidence Availability

Evidence availability is distinct from Evidence validity.

Valid Evidence may temporarily be unavailable.

---

# 122. AI Output as Evidence

AI-generated output MAY be stored as Evidence of:

```text
what an AI system concluded
```

It MUST NOT automatically be treated as Evidence that the conclusion itself is correct.

---

# 123. AI Evidence Provenance

AI-produced Evidence SHOULD preserve, where material and available:

* model identity,
* provider,
* model version or revision,
* prompt or task identity,
* context references,
* tool outputs,
* time,
* generation parameters where relevant.

---

# 124. AI Analysis

An AI analysis MAY constitute derived Evidence.

Its relationship to source Evidence SHOULD remain inspectable.

---

# 125. AI Confidence

AI confidence values MUST NOT be treated as universal Evidence-strength scores.

---

# 126. AI Hallucination Risk

Policies SHOULD permit AI-generated Evidence to require corroboration before use in consequential governance decisions.

---

# 127. Human Observation as Evidence

A human observation MAY be valid Evidence.

It SHOULD preserve:

* Actor identity,
* observation time,
* subject matter,
* scope,
* statement,
* relevant provenance.

---

# 128. Human ≠ authoritative by default

Human-produced Evidence does not automatically become authoritative or correct solely because a human produced it.

---

# 129. Testimony and Observation

The system MAY distinguish between:

```text
direct observation
reported testimony
interpretation
```

where that distinction matters.

---

# 130. Evidence Confidentiality

Evidence MAY contain sensitive information.

Evidence records SHOULD support classification such as:

```text
public
internal
confidential
restricted
```

when required.

---

# 131. Secret Material

Secret values SHOULD NOT be copied into portable Evidence artifacts unless explicitly required and safely protected.

Evidence SHOULD prefer redacted content or secret references where possible.

---

# 132. Redacted Evidence

Redacted Evidence MUST identify that redaction occurred.

Redaction SHOULD preserve enough provenance to understand what class of information was removed.

---

# 133. Evidence Access Control

Systems MAY independently control:

```text
discover
read metadata
read content
create
validate
invalidate
export
delete
```

Evidence operations.

---

# 134. Evidence Authority and Access

Technical permission to create or alter Evidence MUST NOT be treated as authority to determine the Claim outcome.

---

# 135. Evidence Portability

Core Evidence semantics SHOULD remain portable across:

* repositories,
* machines,
* storage engines,
* hosting providers,
* CI providers,
* AI providers.

---

# 136. Portable Evidence Record

A portable Evidence record SHOULD preserve:

```text
identity
type
source
scope
provenance
time
revision binding
integrity metadata
lifecycle
Claim relationships
```

where applicable.

---

# 137. Provider-Specific Evidence

Provider-specific metadata MAY be preserved as extensions.

Provider-specific fields MUST NOT become required to interpret the canonical Evidence meaning unless the Evidence itself is inherently provider-specific.

---

# 138. Import

Evidence imports SHOULD validate:

* identity,
* schema version,
* source references,
* digests where available,
* provenance,
* scope,
* duplicate identity,
* extension preservation.

---

# 139. Export

Evidence exports SHOULD declare:

* format version,
* schema version,
* included Evidence,
* relationship closure,
* redactions,
* external dependencies,
* integrity metadata.

---

# 140. Round-Trip Preservation

Supported export/import paths SHOULD preserve canonical Evidence semantics without silent loss.

---

# 141. Lossy Export

If an export format cannot preserve all Evidence semantics, loss MUST be explicitly reported.

---

# 142. Local-First Evidence

Core Evidence metadata SHOULD remain inspectable locally where Monad operates local-first.

---

# 143. Offline Evidence Capture

Evidence MAY be captured offline.

Later synchronization MUST preserve:

* original capture time,
* producer,
* local provenance,
* any subsequent reconciliation.

---

# 144. Evidence Synchronization

Evidence synchronization MUST NOT alter Evidence identity merely because storage location changes.

---

# 145. Duplicate Import

Importing the same Evidence repeatedly SHOULD support duplicate detection or idempotent behavior where practical.

Duplicate detection MUST account for provenance and identity rather than relying only on byte equality.

---

# 146. Evidence Querying

Monad SHOULD support queries such as:

* What Evidence supports Claim C?
* What Evidence contradicts Claim C?
* Which Evidence applies to revision R?
* Which Evidence is stale?
* Which Evidence has been invalidated?
* What produced Evidence E?
* Which Verifications used E?
* Which releases depend on E?
* Which Evidence came from AI?
* Which Evidence is unavailable?
* Which Evidence lacks provenance?
* Which Evidence was derived from E?

---

# 147. Historical Query

Monad SHOULD support:

> What Evidence was available when Decision D was made?

where historical retention permits.

---

# 148. Evidence Explanation

Consequential Evidence SHOULD be explainable in a human-readable form.

Example:

```text
Evidence: EVD-0042
Type: integration-test-result
Revision: abc123
Environment: linux-ci
Observed: 2026-08-30T04:13:00Z
Producer: test-runner
Supports: CLM-0102
Status: valid
Freshness: current for revision abc123
```

---

# 149. Evidence Graph Traversal

Evidence MAY participate in graph queries.

Traversal MUST preserve relationship semantics.

For example:

```text
Requirement
  verified-by
Verification
  supported-by
Evidence
  produced-by
Execution
  executed-by
Actor
```

must not be flattened into anonymous adjacency.

---

# 150. Evidence Indexes

Derived indexes MAY accelerate queries by:

* type,
* Claim,
* revision,
* time,
* producer,
* lifecycle,
* environment,
* integrity state.

Indexes SHOULD be reconstructable.

---

# 151. Evidence Projection

Read models MAY project Evidence summaries.

Projected summaries MUST NOT replace canonical Evidence when details are necessary for audit or interpretation.

---

# 152. Evidence Summarization

AI or deterministic tools MAY summarize Evidence.

Summaries MUST retain derivation links to source Evidence.

---

# 153. Evidence Compression

Compression MAY alter representation but MUST NOT alter semantic Evidence identity if content remains semantically unchanged.

---

# 154. Evidence Deduplication

Storage systems MAY deduplicate identical Evidence content.

Semantic Evidence records with distinct provenance MUST remain distinguishable.

---

# 155. Evidence Ranking

User interfaces MAY rank Evidence.

Ranking criteria SHOULD be explainable if ordering could materially influence engineering decisions.

---

# 156. No Universal Evidence Score

Monad SHOULD NOT define one universal scalar indicating “how true” Evidence is.

Evidence quality is multidimensional and Claim-specific.

---

# 157. Evidence Confidence

Confidence MAY describe an Evidence-producing process or interpretation.

Confidence MUST remain distinct from:

* validity,
* authority,
* relevance,
* Claim truth.

---

# 158. Evidence Uncertainty

Uncertainty SHOULD be preserved rather than converted into unjustified certainty.

Example:

```text
coverage incomplete
source reliability unknown
collection interval partial
```

---

# 159. Evidence Gaps

Monad SHOULD be able to identify Evidence gaps.

Examples:

```text
Requirement has no Verification Evidence.
Release lacks reproducibility Evidence.
Claim has only AI-generated support.
```

---

# 160. Evidence Obligations Query

The system SHOULD support:

> What Evidence is still required before Operation O can be authorized?

---

# 161. Evidence Economy

Monad SHOULD collect Evidence that materially improves:

* confidence,
* traceability,
* governance,
* verification,
* incident analysis,
* reproducibility.

It SHOULD NOT retain expensive Evidence indefinitely without a justified purpose.

---

# 162. Cost-Aware Evidence Retention

Retention MAY consider:

* storage cost,
* regeneration cost,
* audit value,
* legal obligations,
* security sensitivity,
* reproducibility value.

---

# 163. Regenerable Evidence

Evidence that can be cheaply and deterministically regenerated MAY use different retention rules than irreproducible Evidence.

The ability to regenerate MUST itself be justified.

---

# 164. Irreplaceable Evidence

Evidence of ephemeral events SHOULD receive stronger retention consideration.

Examples:

```text
incident trace
production failure snapshot
external API response
human observation
```

---

# 165. Evidence Reproducibility

Evidence SHOULD identify whether reproduction is:

```text
exact
approximate
possible
unknown
impossible
```

where materially relevant.

---

# 166. Reproducible Evidence

Reproducible Evidence SHOULD preserve enough execution context to repeat the relevant observation when practical.

---

# 167. Non-Reproducible Evidence

Evidence need not be reproducible to be valid.

For example, an incident occurring once in production may produce unique Evidence.

Non-reproducibility MUST NOT cause automatic rejection.

---

# 168. Evidence Lineage

Consumers SHOULD be able to traverse upstream and downstream Evidence derivation.

Example:

```text
raw benchmark samples
    →
aggregated statistics
    →
performance report
    →
release verification
```

---

# 169. Circular Evidence

The system SHOULD detect or avoid circular Evidence reasoning where:

```text
Claim A supported by Evidence B
Evidence B exists solely because Claim A was assumed
```

and no independent basis exists.

---

# 170. Evidence Independence Audit

High-risk decisions SHOULD allow reviewers to inspect whether multiple Evidence items truly originate from independent sources.

---

# 171. Evidence Quality Policy

Domains MAY define quality criteria for specific Evidence types.

Example:

```text
security scan Evidence:
    scanner version must be recorded
    scan must target release artifact digest
    database definitions must be <= 24h old
```

---

# 172. Native-System Evidence

Monad SHOULD preserve native-system Evidence without unnecessarily reimplementing the native capability that produced it.

---

# 173. Native Evidence Reference

A Native Reference MAY locate Evidence stored externally.

The canonical Evidence record SHOULD distinguish:

```text
Evidence identity
```

from:

```text
native location
```

---

# 174. Native Evidence Drift

If native Evidence changes after import, Monad SHOULD be able to detect or represent:

* changed content,
* new revision,
* deletion,
* access loss,
* provider mutation.

---

# 175. Mutable Native Evidence

Mutable external Evidence SHOULD be revision-bound or snapshotted when reproducibility or auditability requires stable content.

---

# 176. Evidence Correction Provenance

Corrections to Evidence metadata MUST preserve:

* previous value,
* corrected value,
* Actor,
* reason,
* time

when consequential.

---

# 177. Evidence Replacement

Replacing Evidence MUST NOT silently rewrite downstream historical decisions that relied on the earlier Evidence.

---

# 178. Downstream Impact

Invalidating Evidence SHOULD make affected downstream entities discoverable.

Possible downstream entities include:

```text
Claims
Verifications
Approvals
Releases
Decisions
Reviews
```

---

# 179. Evidence Dependency

A Verification or Decision MAY depend materially on Evidence.

Such dependency SHOULD be explicit rather than inferred only from document co-location.

---

# 180. Evidence Falsifiability

Evidence-backed Claims SHOULD remain open to contradicting Evidence unless the domain legitimately establishes proof.

Monad MUST NOT encode “supported” as permanent unquestionable truth.

---

# 181. Evidence Neutrality

The Evidence model MUST support Evidence that:

* supports,
* contradicts,
* weakens,
* qualifies,

without privileging Evidence that confirms the current canonical Claim.

---

# 182. Confirmation Bias Resistance

Interfaces SHOULD avoid hiding contradictory Evidence merely because a canonical Claim already exists.

---

# 183. Evidence Interpretation

Evidence interpretation MAY itself be represented as a Claim.

Example:

```text
Evidence:
    CPU usage spike

Claim:
    The spike indicates resource exhaustion.
```

The interpretation MUST remain distinguishable from the raw Evidence.

---

# 184. Causal Evidence

Evidence used to support causal Claims SHOULD preserve the reasoning or method connecting observation to causality.

Temporal correlation alone SHOULD NOT be labeled causal Evidence without additional justification.

---

# 185. Statistical Evidence

Statistical Evidence SHOULD preserve relevant method metadata where needed, such as:

```text
sample size
population
confidence interval
test method
aggregation
```

The final statistical schema is domain-specific.

---

# 186. Benchmark Evidence

Benchmark Evidence SHOULD preserve:

* workload,
* environment,
* dataset,
* runtime versions,
* warmup policy,
* sample count,
* measurement method.

---

# 187. Security Evidence

Security Evidence SHOULD preserve sufficient provenance to determine:

* scanner/tool,
* ruleset/database version,
* artifact scanned,
* configuration,
* time.

---

# 188. Build Evidence

Build Evidence MAY include:

```text
source revision
dependency lock state
toolchain identity
artifact digest
build environment
build logs
```

---

# 189. Reproducibility Evidence

Reproducibility Evidence SHOULD distinguish:

```text
same inputs produced identical artifact
```

from:

```text
same inputs produced functionally equivalent behavior
```

Those are not identical claims.

---

# 190. Migration Evidence

Migration operations SHOULD be able to produce Evidence for:

* pre-migration state,
* migration plan,
* execution,
* post-migration validation,
* reconciliation,
* rollback capability.

---

# 191. Evidence Availability Failure

When Evidence required for a consequential operation cannot be retrieved, the system SHOULD surface:

```text
Evidence unavailable
```

rather than fabricating or silently omitting it.

---

# 192. Evidence Retrieval Failure

Retrieval failure SHOULD preserve diagnostic information separately from the Evidence record.

---

# 193. Evidence Parsing Failure

A parser failure does not imply the underlying Evidence is invalid.

The system SHOULD distinguish:

```text
Evidence unreadable by current parser
```

from:

```text
Evidence content invalid
```

---

# 194. Schema Evolution

Evidence schemas MUST support explicit versioning.

Older Evidence SHOULD remain interpretable or migratable according to the compatibility policy.

---

# 195. Unknown Fields

Importers SHOULD preserve unknown fields when possible to avoid destructive round-trip loss.

---

# 196. Evidence Migration

Evidence schema migrations SHOULD preserve:

* identity,
* provenance,
* Claim relationships,
* lifecycle,
* integrity information,
* historical meaning.

---

# 197. Migration Provenance

Material Evidence migrations SHOULD be traceable.

---

# 198. Evidence Validation Layers

Evidence validation SHOULD occur in distinct layers.

## Level 1 — Structural

Verify:

* required fields,
* identifiers,
* schema structure.

## Level 2 — Referential

Verify:

* source references,
* Claim references,
* revision references,
* Representation references.

## Level 3 — Integrity

Verify:

* digests,
* sizes,
* expected immutable content.

## Level 4 — Provenance

Verify required origin and producer information.

## Level 5 — Scope

Verify:

* revision,
* environment,
* time,
* Subject applicability.

## Level 6 — Semantic

Verify Evidence-type-specific invariants.

## Level 7 — Policy

Verify Evidence satisfies required governance criteria for intended use.

---

# 199. Validation Result

Evidence validation results MUST remain distinct from Evidence itself.

---

# 200. Structurally Valid ≠ Valid Evidence

Evidence that matches a schema may still be:

* fabricated,
* stale,
* irrelevant,
* corrupted upstream,
* collected from wrong revision.

Schema validation alone MUST NOT imply evidentiary validity.


# 211. Anti-Patterns

## 211.1 Claim embedded as Evidence

```text
Evidence:
    "The system is secure."
```

This is a Claim, not sufficient Evidence by itself.

---

## 211.2 Green CI means release ready

A CI status is treated as definitive release Evidence without understanding which checks ran.

---

## 211.3 Newest Evidence always wins

More recent Evidence automatically overrides older Evidence regardless of scope or authority.

---

## 211.4 Screenshot without provenance

A screenshot is retained without identifying:

* source,
* time,
* environment,
* subject.

Its evidentiary value becomes difficult to establish.

---

## 211.5 Summary replaces source

An AI summary replaces raw logs and the original Evidence is discarded.

---

## 211.6 Same bytes means same Evidence

Two artifacts with identical content but different provenance are collapsed into one semantic Evidence instance.

---

## 211.7 Evidence equals proof

A passing test is interpreted as proving all relevant behavior correct.

---

## 211.8 Evidence by authority

A senior engineer's assertion is treated as Evidence solely because of organizational rank.

---

## 211.9 Provider URL as Evidence identity

A mutable CI URL becomes the semantic Evidence identifier.

---

## 211.10 Silent stale Evidence

Evidence remains displayed as current after underlying revision changes.

---

## 211.11 Supporting Evidence hidden by verdict

Only the final `PASS` result is retained; underlying Evidence disappears.

---

## 211.12 Contradictory Evidence discarded

Evidence that challenges the preferred conclusion is deleted.

---

## 211.13 AI output treated as observation

An AI-generated explanation is represented as if it directly observed runtime state.

---

## 211.14 Dashboard equals raw Evidence

A dashboard screenshot is treated as equivalent to the complete metric dataset from which it was generated.

---

## 211.15 Missing Evidence treated as failure Evidence

Failure to retrieve a test result is interpreted as a failing test.

---

# 212. Non-Requirements

This specification does **not** require:

* all Evidence to be stored directly inside Monad,
* cryptographic signing of every Evidence artifact,
* indefinite Evidence retention,
* one universal Evidence-strength score,
* every Claim to have Evidence,
* every Evidence item to be independently reproducible,
* formal proof,
* automatic Claim resolution,
* AI analysis,
* graph database storage,
* content-addressable storage,
* immutable external providers,
* storing every log indefinitely,
* treating native systems as untrusted,
* treating human observations as inherently superior to machine observations.

# 213. Conformance Scenario — Passing Test

Given:

```text
Requirement R
```

and:

```text
test T passes against revision abc123
```

Monad MAY create Evidence representing the passing test.

It MUST NOT automatically mark R satisfied unless an applicable Verification establishes that conclusion.

---

# 214. Conformance Scenario — Changed Revision

Given passing Evidence for revision `abc123` and a materially changed revision `def456`,

the original Evidence MUST remain bound to `abc123`.

Policy or Verification MAY determine whether it remains relevant to `def456`.

---

# 215. Conformance Scenario — Conflicting Results

Given:

```text
Test run A: pass
Test run B: fail
```

for the same revision and environment,

Monad MUST preserve both results.

It MUST NOT silently retain only the newest or preferred outcome.

---

# 216. Conformance Scenario — Invalid Test Setup

Given a passing test run later discovered to have used the wrong database configuration,

the Evidence SHOULD be invalidated or qualified.

Any Verification materially dependent on it SHOULD become discoverable as affected.

---

# 217. Conformance Scenario — External Deletion

Given externally hosted Evidence is deleted,

Monad SHOULD retain:

* Evidence identity,
* former reference,
* provenance,
* last-known metadata,
* unavailable lifecycle state.

---

# 218. Conformance Scenario — AI Analysis

Given an AI system analyzes logs and concludes:

```text
Database exhaustion likely caused the incident.
```

Monad MAY record the analysis as derived Evidence.

It MUST preserve the underlying logs separately and MUST NOT automatically establish the causal Claim as canonical truth.

---

# 219. Conformance Scenario — Duplicate Derivation

Given two reports are generated from the same underlying benchmark dataset,

Monad SHOULD NOT treat the two reports as independent corroborating Evidence merely because they are separate files.

---

# 220. Conformance Scenario — Historical Evidence

Given Evidence became stale after a new release,

historical queries concerning the earlier release MUST still be able to retrieve it where retention permits.

---

# 221. Conformance Scenario — Redaction

Given Evidence contains credentials,

an exported Evidence package MAY redact the credentials.

The export MUST indicate that redaction occurred.

---

# 222. Conformance Scenario — Offline Capture

Given a developer captures Evidence while offline,

later synchronization MUST preserve the original observation and capture time rather than replacing it with synchronization time.

---

# 223. Conformance Scenario — Native Evidence Move

Given a CI provider migrates an artifact from one storage URL to another,

Evidence identity SHOULD remain stable if the underlying Evidence remains the same.

---

# 224. Conformance Scenario — Evidence of Absence

Given an observation mechanism does not guarantee complete capture,

no matching observation MUST NOT automatically establish a negative Claim.

---

# 225. Conformance Scenario — Release Candidate

Given Evidence Bundle B applies to release candidate RC1,

and RC2 differs materially,

B MUST NOT automatically satisfy RC2's release Evidence obligations.

---

# 226. Conformance Scenario — Signature

Given a signed Evidence artifact passes signature validation,

Monad MAY conclude that the artifact matches the signed content and signer identity according to the signature system.

It MUST NOT infer that the Evidence content is factually correct solely from the signature.

---

# 227. Conformance Scenario — Evidence Transformation

Given raw trace data is summarized into a latency report,

the report MUST preserve derivation links sufficient to locate or identify the trace Evidence when retention policy permits.

---

# 228. Conformance Scenario — Review Finding

Given a reviewer reports:

```text
Authentication code appears to bypass policy check.
```

the finding MAY be stored as Evidence.

The finding does not automatically establish the defect as confirmed.

---

# 233. Definition of Ready

This specification is ready for baseline acceptance when:

* Evidence is explicitly separated from Claims,
* Evidence is explicitly separated from Verification,
* Evidence is explicitly separated from Authority,
* Evidence provenance is representable,
* scope and revision binding are explicit,
* validity and relevance are distinguished,
* freshness is contextual,
* contradictory Evidence can coexist,
* derived Evidence retains upstream provenance,
* AI-generated Evidence remains epistemically constrained,
* native Evidence can remain externally stored,
* lifecycle and invalidation semantics preserve history,
* Evidence can be related to Verification and release decisions,
* portability and integrity concerns are represented.

# 234. Definition of Done

The Evidence specification is complete for the foundational specification baseline when:

* every major `REQ-EVD-*` obligation maps to normative behavior,
* Claims can reference supporting and contradicting Evidence,
* Verification can consume explicit Evidence sets,
* governance can express Evidence obligations,
* execution can emit durable Evidence,
* reconciliation can reason over observed Evidence,
* releases can assemble revision-bound Evidence Bundles,
* AI output can participate without being mistaken for truth,
* native-system Evidence can remain first-class,
* Evidence invalidation can propagate downstream impact,
* schema work can proceed without redefining Evidence semantics,
* conformance scenarios cover primary epistemic failure modes.

# 235. Requirement Traceability

This specification primarily operationalizes `REQ-EVD-*`.

It also defines Evidence behavior needed by:

| Requirement family | Evidence concern                                          |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `REQ-CLM-*`        | Claims supported, contradicted, and qualified by Evidence |
| `REQ-PROV-*`       | origin and derivation of Evidence                         |
| `REQ-SEM-*`        | Evidence as a first-class semantic concept                |
| `REQ-ID-*`         | stable Evidence identity independent of location          |
| `REQ-AUTH-*`       | authority over Evidence submission and use                |
| `REQ-GOV-*`        | Evidence obligations and authorization gates              |
| `REQ-EXE-*`        | Evidence produced by executions                           |
| `REQ-REC-*`        | observed-state Evidence used in reconciliation            |
| `REQ-VER-*`        | Evidence evaluated by Verification                        |
| `REQ-PORT-*`       | Evidence preservation across providers and migrations     |

Exact requirement-to-clause mapping SHOULD be produced during specification baseline review.

# 237. Governing Principle

Evidence exists to make engineering belief inspectable.

Monad must therefore preserve the difference between:

```text
"We believe this."
```

and:

```text
"Here is what we observed."
```

between:

```text
"The test passed."
```

and:

```text
"The requirement is satisfied."
```

between:

```text
"This artifact has a valid hash."
```

and:

```text
"This artifact is correct."
```

between:

```text
"An AI system concluded this."
```

and:

```text
"This is true."
```

between:

```text
"We found no evidence."
```

and:

```text
"We have evidence of absence."
```

The governing principle is:

> **Engineering Claims should be as strong as the Evidence supporting them—and no stronger.**

Monad should make Evidence durable enough to inspect, precise enough to scope, connected enough to trace, and humble enough not to pretend that observation alone eliminates uncertainty.
