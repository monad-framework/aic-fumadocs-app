# Relationships Specification

**Specification ID:** `SPEC-REL-001`
**Status:** Draft
**Version:** `0.1.0`
**Normative:** Yes
**Primary requirements:** `REQ-SEM-*`
**Related requirements:** `REQ-ID-*`, `REQ-CLM-*`, `REQ-EVD-*`, `REQ-PROV-*`, `REQ-AUTH-*`, `REQ-REC-*`, `REQ-PORT-*`
**Parent specification:** `SPEC-SEM-001 — Semantic Kernel Specification`

## 1. Purpose

This specification defines how Monad represents meaningful relationships among semantic entities.

Relationships are necessary because engineering systems are not merely collections of isolated artifacts.

They contain structures such as:

```text
Requirement
    implemented-by
Component
```

```text
Decision
    constrains
Specification
```

```text
Claim
    supported-by
Evidence
```

```text
Release
    contains
Artifact
```

```text
Component A
    depends-on
Component B
```

```text
Claim C2
    corrects
Claim C1
```

A relationship is not merely an untyped graph edge.

The central principle is:

> **A relationship must preserve the meaning of the connection, not merely the fact that two things are connected.**

A useful relationship model therefore needs to represent distinctions among:

* dependency,
* containment,
* implementation,
* derivation,
* provenance,
* causality,
* chronology,
* support,
* contradiction,
* authority,
* ownership,
* supersession,
* rationale,
* verification,
* deployment,
* representation,
* identity binding.

Monad MUST NOT reduce these materially different semantics to an undifferentiated `related-to` edge when the distinction affects engineering reasoning.

* provenance,
* authority,

# 4. Core Relationship Model

Conceptually:

```text
Relationship
 ├── identity
 ├── type
 ├── source
 ├── target
 ├── scope
 ├── provenance
 ├── temporal semantics
 ├── lifecycle
 └── optional properties
```

A minimal relationship can be expressed conceptually as:

```text
source
predicate
target
```

For example:

```text
Component A
    depends-on
Component B
```

A richer relationship may contain:

```text
Relationship {
    identity
    type
    source
    target
    scope
    validTime
    observedTime
    provenance
    authority
    confidence
    lifecycle
    properties
}
```

The final serialized representation is deferred.

---

# 5. Core Relationship Invariants

Conforming implementations MUST preserve the following distinctions.

## 5.1 Relationship ≠ endpoint

A relationship is not reducible to either connected entity.

Deleting or changing a relationship MUST NOT implicitly change endpoint identity unless another rule explicitly requires it.

---

## 5.2 Relationship ≠ Claim

A relationship may itself be asserted by a Claim.

For example:

```text
Claim:
    Component A depends on Component B.
```

The proposition concerns a semantic relationship.

The Claim and relationship remain conceptually distinct.

---

## 5.3 Relationship ≠ Evidence

Evidence may support the existence or properties of a relationship.

Evidence is not the relationship itself.

---

## 5.4 Relationship ≠ provenance

A provenance relationship may be represented using the general relationship model.

However, provenance semantics remain distinct from arbitrary semantic association.

---

## 5.5 Dependency ≠ causality

If:

```text
A depends on B
```

Monad MUST NOT infer:

```text
B caused A
```

or:

```text
A was caused by B
```

---

## 5.6 Chronology ≠ causality

If event A occurred before event B, Monad MUST NOT infer that A caused B.

---

## 5.7 Similarity ≠ equivalence

Two entities that are semantically or textually similar MUST NOT automatically be treated as equivalent.

---

## 5.8 Connection ≠ authority

The existence of a relationship does not confer authority over either endpoint.

---

## 5.9 Derived ≠ authoritative

A derived relationship does not become authoritative solely because an engine inferred or materialized it.

---

## 5.10 Unknown ≠ unrelated

Absence of a known relationship MUST NOT generally imply that no relationship exists.

---

# 6. First-Class Relationships

Relationships whose identity, provenance, properties, history, or lifecycle materially matter SHOULD be representable as first-class semantic entities.

First-class relationships may need:

* stable identity,
* timestamps,
* provenance,
* authority,
* confidence,
* lifecycle,
* properties,
* Evidence,
* revision history.

Not every simple relationship must necessarily receive an independent first-class identifier.

---

# 7. Relationship Identity

A relationship requiring durable reference MUST possess stable semantic identity.

Relationship identity MUST NOT depend solely on:

* array position,
* database row order,
* source file line number,
* textual wording,
* current endpoint location.

---

# 8. Relationship Identity and Endpoints

Changing an endpoint generally changes the relationship's semantic meaning.

For example:

```text
A depends-on B
```

and:

```text
A depends-on C
```

are ordinarily distinct relationships.

A specification MAY define exceptions for relationships whose target changes as part of an explicit revision lifecycle.

---

# 9. Relationship Identity and Type

Changing a relationship type generally constitutes a material semantic change.

For example:

```text
A depends-on B
```

is not a revision-equivalent form of:

```text
A caused-by B
```

unless a domain-specific specification explicitly defines such equivalence.

---

# 10. Relationship Identity Across Representation Movement

Moving or renaming endpoint Representations MUST NOT create a new semantic relationship if endpoint Subjects and relationship semantics remain unchanged.

Identity must follow semantic entities rather than mutable paths.

---

# 11. Relationship Type

Every relationship MUST possess semantics sufficient to distinguish its meaning from materially different relationship types.

Relationship type MAY be identified by:

* stable identifier,
* vocabulary entry,
* schema identifier,
* domain-defined predicate.

---

# 12. Relationship Vocabulary

Monad SHOULD maintain a controlled vocabulary for foundational relationship types.

The vocabulary SHOULD be small enough to remain understandable while extensible enough to support domain-specific semantics.

---

# 13. Foundational Relationship Families

The core model SHOULD support relationship families including:

```text
structural
dependency
traceability
provenance
epistemic
governance
lifecycle
identity
representation
execution
deployment
historical
organizational
```

These families are organizational concepts and do not necessarily define final predicate names.

---

# 14. Directionality

Relationship types MUST explicitly define whether they are:

```text
directed
symmetric
undirected
```

Implementations MUST NOT assume all graph edges are interchangeable in direction.

---

# 15. Directed Relationship

For:

```text
A depends-on B
```

the direction matters.

The relationship does not necessarily imply:

```text
B depends-on A
```

---

# 16. Symmetric Relationship

A relationship type MAY be symmetric.

For example, a domain might define:

```text
A incompatible-with B
```

as symmetric.

If so:

```text
A incompatible-with B
```

semantically implies:

```text
B incompatible-with A
```

without requiring two independent semantic assertions.

---

# 17. Inverse Relationships

A relationship type MAY define an inverse.

For example:

```text
Requirement implemented-by Component
```

may have inverse:

```text
Component implements Requirement
```

The inverse SHOULD be defined semantically rather than represented as unrelated duplicate data.

---

# 18. Materialized Inverses

Implementations MAY materialize inverse relationships for efficient querying.

Materialized inverses MUST remain derivable from the authoritative relationship.

They MUST NOT drift independently.

---

# 19. Reflexivity

Relationship definitions SHOULD specify whether self-relationships are valid.

For example:

```text
A depends-on A
```

may indicate:

* an invalid cycle,
* intentional recursion,
* modeling error.

The meaning is domain-specific.

---

# 20. Cardinality

A relationship type MAY define cardinality constraints.

Examples:

```text
one-to-one
one-to-many
many-to-one
many-to-many
```

Cardinality constraints SHOULD be enforced where violating them would create semantic ambiguity.

---

# 21. Relationship Properties

A relationship MAY possess properties that describe the relationship itself rather than either endpoint.

Example:

```text
Service A
    calls
Service B

protocol = HTTP
criticality = high
environment = production
```

These values belong to the relationship.

They MUST NOT be incorrectly assigned to A or B solely because the persistence model lacks relationship properties.

---

# 22. Typed Relationship Properties

Relationship properties SHOULD preserve meaningful value types.

Examples:

```text
boolean
integer
duration
version
enum
timestamp
Subject reference
```

---

# 23. Relationship Scope

Relationships MAY apply only within a defined scope.

Possible scopes include:

```text
workspace
repository
branch
release
environment
deployment
tenant
architecture view
execution
time interval
```

---

# 24. Environment-Specific Relationships

The following may both be valid:

```text
Service A depends-on Redis
scope: development
```

```text
Service A depends-on DynamoDB
scope: production
```

Scope MUST be considered before declaring these relationships contradictory.

---

# 25. Revision-Specific Relationships

A relationship MAY apply only to a specific revision.

Example:

```text
Requirement R
    implemented-by
Commit C
```

A later implementation revision may change the relevant relationship.

Revision scope MUST remain explicit where necessary.

---

# 26. Temporal Relationships

Relationships MAY possess valid-time semantics.

Conceptually:

```text
validFrom
validUntil
```

A relationship that was true historically SHOULD remain queryable where history is retained.

---

# 27. Observation Time

The time at which Monad observed a relationship MUST remain distinguishable from the time during which the relationship existed.

For example:

```text
observedAt: 14:05
```

does not necessarily mean:

```text
validFrom: 14:05
```

---

# 28. Relationship Provenance

Consequential relationships SHOULD preserve provenance sufficient to answer:

> Why does Monad believe this relationship exists?

Provenance may identify:

* authored declaration,
* source code,
* native API,
* dependency scanner,
* AI inference,
* human assertion,
* import,
* reconciliation,
* transformation,
* verification.

---

# 29. Explicit Relationship

An explicit relationship is intentionally declared by an actor or authoritative source.

Example:

```text
architecture manifest:
    api depends-on database
```

Explicit declaration does not automatically establish universal authority.

---

# 30. Observed Relationship

An observed relationship is discovered from native state.

Example:

```text
package.json dependency:
    application → library
```

This observation MAY support a semantic dependency relationship.

The native relationship and interpreted semantic relationship MAY remain distinct.

---

# 31. Inferred Relationship

A relationship MAY be inferred from available Evidence or Claims.

For example:

```text
AI analysis:
    module A likely implements requirement R
```

Inferred relationships MUST remain distinguishishable from confirmed or authoritative relationships.

---

# 32. Relationship Confidence

An inferred relationship MAY carry confidence.

Confidence MUST NOT substitute for:

* authority,
* Evidence,
* approval,
* truth.

---

# 33. Relationship Authority

A relationship MAY be authoritative within a defined scope.

Authority SHOULD identify who or what may legitimately establish the relationship.

For example:

```text
architecture specification
```

may be authoritative for:

```text
logical-component dependency
```

while:

```text
package manager
```

may be authoritative for:

```text
declared package dependency
```

These are not necessarily the same proposition.

---

# 34. Native Authority

Native systems MAY own authoritative relationship semantics for their own domain.

Examples:

```text
Git:
    commit parent relationships
```

```text
package manager:
    declared package dependencies
```

```text
issue tracker:
    parent-child issue relationships
```

Monad MUST preserve native meaning rather than translating all native relations into one generic semantic form.

---

# 35. Relationship Claim

Where epistemic treatment matters, the existence of a relationship SHOULD be expressible through a Claim.

Conceptually:

```text
Claim C:
    proposition:
        A depends-on B
```

The relationship may then participate in:

* Evidence,
* conflict,
* authority,
* correction,
* verification.

---

# 36. Relationship Evidence

Evidence MAY support, contradict, or qualify a relationship Claim.

Example:

```text
Claim:
    Service A calls Service B.

Evidence:
    distributed trace showing A → B.
```

---

# 37. Multiple Sources

The same semantic relationship MAY be supported by multiple independent sources.

Example:

```text
source code import
runtime trace
architecture specification
```

Monad SHOULD preserve source provenance rather than flattening all support into one anonymous edge.

---

# 38. Relationship Conflict

Two relationships MAY conflict when they express incompatible semantic states within overlapping scope.

Examples:

```text
Component A
    owned-by Team X
```

and:

```text
Component A
    owned-by Team Y
```

may conflict if ownership is defined as single-valued.

If multiple ownership is permitted, no conflict exists.

Conflict depends on relationship semantics.

---

# 39. Relationship Conflict Preservation

Conflicting relationships MUST remain representable until resolved.

A conforming system MUST NOT delete one merely to maintain graph consistency.

---

# 40. Relationship Correction

A relationship later determined to be incorrect SHOULD be corrected without destroying relevant historical provenance.

For example:

```text
R1:
    Component A depends-on B

R2:
    Component A depends-on C

R2 corrects R1
```

where appropriate.

---

# 41. Relationship Retraction

An actor MAY retract a relationship assertion.

Retraction MUST NOT imply the assertion never existed.

---

# 42. Relationship Supersession

Relationships MAY supersede previous relationships.

Example:

```text
Service A
    deployed-to Cluster Old
```

superseded by:

```text
Service A
    deployed-to Cluster New
```

Supersession SHOULD preserve temporal semantics.

---

# 43. Relationship Invalidation

A relationship MAY be invalidated when:

* endpoint identity was wrong,
* source data was corrupted,
* inference was flawed,
* scope was incorrect,
* native binding was mistaken.

Invalidation SHOULD preserve provenance.

---

# 44. Relationship Lifecycle

Relationship lifecycle MAY include:

```text
candidate
active
contested
superseded
invalidated
historical
archived
```

Lifecycle state MUST remain distinct from truth and authority.

---

# 45. Dependency Relationships

Dependency relationships MUST be modeled with sufficient semantic precision for their intended use.

The generic phrase:

```text
A depends on B
```

may refer to materially different concepts such as:

```text
build dependency
runtime dependency
deployment dependency
data dependency
configuration dependency
organizational dependency
semantic dependency
```

Monad SHOULD preserve those distinctions where they affect engineering action.

---

# 46. Direct Dependency

A direct dependency is explicitly present without relying on transitive traversal.

Example:

```text
A → B
```

---

# 47. Transitive Dependency

If:

```text
A depends-on B
B depends-on C
```

then:

```text
A transitively depends-on C
```

MAY be derived if the relationship type is defined as transitively composable.

The derived relationship MUST remain distinguishable from a direct dependency.

---

# 48. Transitivity Is Type-Specific

Monad MUST NOT assume all relationship types are transitive.

For example:

```text
A reviewed-by B
B reviewed-by C
```

does not imply:

```text
A reviewed-by C
```

---

# 49. Dependency Cycles

Dependency cycles SHOULD be detectable where the domain considers them significant.

A cycle does not automatically mean an error.

Possible meanings include:

* architecture defect,
* recursive structure,
* mutually dependent services,
* modeling artifact.

Domain rules determine validity.

---

# 50. Causal Relationships

Causal relationships require stronger semantics than dependency or chronology.

A Claim that:

```text
Event A caused Event B
```

SHOULD retain Evidence or rationale sufficient to distinguish causal analysis from simple temporal ordering.

---

# 51. Causal Confidence

Causal relationships MAY be:

```text
hypothesized
probable
confirmed
contested
```

The epistemic status SHOULD remain explicit.

---

# 52. Chronological Relationships

Relationships such as:

```text
before
after
concurrent-with
```

describe temporal ordering.

They MUST NOT automatically imply causality.

---

# 53. Rationale Relationships

Relationships MAY express reasoning such as:

```text
Decision
    justified-by
Evidence
```

or:

```text
Decision
    addresses
Problem
```

Rationale relationships MUST remain distinguishable from technical dependency.

---

# 54. Containment Relationships

Containment relationships MAY describe semantic inclusion.

Examples:

```text
Release contains Artifact
```

```text
Repository contains Module
```

```text
Milestone contains Epic
```

Different containment concepts SHOULD use distinct relationship semantics where necessary.

---

# 55. Physical and Semantic Containment

Filesystem containment and semantic containment MUST remain distinguishable.

A file being inside a directory does not necessarily mean its Subject is semantically owned by that directory's conceptual component.

---

# 56. Membership Relationships

Membership MAY be represented separately from strict containment.

Example:

```text
Component member-of Subsystem
```

may not imply ownership or physical containment.

---

# 57. Ownership Relationships

Ownership SHOULD define what ownership means in the applicable domain.

Possible meanings include:

* responsible team,
* repository owner,
* service steward,
* legal owner,
* authoritative maintainer.

Monad MUST NOT assume these concepts are equivalent.

---

# 58. Responsibility Relationships

Responsibility SHOULD remain distinguishable from authority.

For example:

```text
Engineer responsible-for Service
```

does not necessarily mean the engineer has authority to approve production deployment.

---

# 59. Representation Relationships

Monad MUST support the semantic relationship between a Subject and its Representations.

Conceptually:

```text
Representation
    represents
Subject
```

This relationship MUST NOT collapse Representation identity into Subject identity.

---

# 60. Multiple Representations

A Subject MAY have multiple Representations.

Example:

```text
Subject: ADR-0042

Representations:
    Markdown file
    generated HTML page
    search index entry
    API projection
```

These are not duplicate Subjects merely because they express the same semantic entity.

---

# 61. Native Reference Relationships

A Native Reference MAY bind an external or local native-system object to a Subject or Representation.

Conceptually:

```text
NativeReference
    references
Subject
```

or:

```text
NativeReference
    locates
Representation
```

The precise relationship depends on the native system and semantic model.

---

# 62. Identity Relationships

Monad MAY support relationships such as:

```text
alias-of
same-as
split-from
merged-into
supersedes
derived-from
```

These MUST have precisely defined semantics.

---

# 63. `same-as`

A `same-as` relationship is semantically strong.

It SHOULD mean that two identifiers refer to the same semantic entity within the defined identity model.

Similarity MUST NOT be represented as `same-as`.

---

# 64. `alias-of`

An alias MAY identify another label or identifier that resolves to the same semantic entity.

Alias semantics SHOULD preserve:

* alias identifier,
* canonical target,
* provenance,
* validity interval where relevant.

---

# 65. Split Relationships

When one semantic identity splits into multiple successors, Monad SHOULD represent that transition explicitly.

Conceptually:

```text
Subject A
    split-into
Subject B
Subject C
```

The system MUST NOT pretend one successor is automatically identical to the predecessor.

---

# 66. Merge Relationships

When multiple prior Subjects are consolidated:

```text
A
B
    merge-into
C
```

Monad SHOULD preserve the lineage rather than deleting historical identity.

---

# 67. Supersession Relationships

Supersession means that a newer entity or assertion takes the governing role formerly held by another.

Supersession MUST NOT imply identity equivalence.

Example:

```text
ADR-002
    supersedes
ADR-001
```

Both remain distinct decisions.

---

# 68. Derivation Relationships

`derived-from` indicates that one entity was produced using another as an input.

Derivation SHOULD preserve sufficient provenance to distinguish:

```text
copy
transformation
compilation
generation
aggregation
inference
```

where relevant.

---

# 69. Generation Relationships

Generated artifacts SHOULD remain traceable to their source inputs.

Example:

```text
Generated API documentation
    generated-from
OpenAPI specification
```

The generated artifact MUST NOT automatically become authoritative merely because it is newer.

---

# 70. Traceability Relationships

Monad MUST support typed traceability relationships across the engineering reasoning chain.

Conceptually:

```text
Problem
    motivates
Goal

Goal
    refined-into
Requirement

Requirement
    addressed-by
Feature

Requirement
    specified-by
Specification

Requirement
    implemented-by
Implementation

Requirement
    verified-by
Verification

Verification
    supported-by
Evidence

Release
    contains
Verified Implementation
```

The exact vocabulary may evolve, but the semantics MUST remain inspectable.

---

# 71. Traceability Is Not Generic Association

A relationship such as:

```text
Requirement related-to File
```

is insufficient when the intended meaning is:

```text
Requirement implemented-by Component
```

Loss of predicate semantics reduces the usefulness of traceability.

---

# 72. Requirement Relationships

Requirements MAY participate in relationships such as:

```text
refines
depends-on
conflicts-with
derived-from
satisfied-by
specified-by
verified-by
supersedes
```

These relationships MUST remain semantically distinct.

---

# 73. Decision Relationships

Decision relationships MAY include:

```text
addresses
resolves
constrains
supersedes
rejected-alternative
supported-by
motivated-by
```

---

# 74. Specification Relationships

Specifications MAY participate in relationships such as:

```text
specifies
implements-requirement
constrained-by
references
supersedes
verified-by
```

---

# 75. Evidence Relationships

Evidence SHOULD use explicit semantics such as:

```text
supports
contradicts
qualifies
corroborates
weakens
```

when related to Claims.

These relationships MUST NOT collapse into generic attachment semantics.

---

# 76. Claim Relationships

Claims MAY relate through:

```text
supports
contradicts
corrects
refines
supersedes
derived-from
weakens
qualifies
equivalent-proposition
```

The Claims Specification defines their epistemic interpretation.

---

# 77. Governance Relationships

Governance relationships MAY express:

```text
Actor holds Authority
Policy applies-to Operation
Approval authorizes Operation
Obligation required-by Policy
Exception overrides Policy
```

These relationships SHOULD preserve scope and temporal validity.

---

# 78. Authority Relationships

Authority relationships MUST not be inferred merely from:

* ownership,
* authorship,
* technical permission,
* tool capability,
* responsibility.

Authority requires explicit authority semantics.

---

# 79. Actor Relationships

Actors MAY have relationships such as:

```text
member-of
responsible-for
approved
authored
executed
reviewed
owns
delegated-to
```

These predicates MUST remain distinct.

---

# 80. Capability Relationships

An Actor or tool MAY possess a Capability relationship.

Example:

```text
Agent A
    capable-of
UpdateRepository
```

This MUST NOT imply:

```text
Agent A
    authorized-to
UpdateRepository
```

---

# 81. Execution Relationships

Execution may generate relationships such as:

```text
Operation
    executed-by
Actor

Execution
    used
Tool

Execution
    consumed
Input

Execution
    produced
Artifact
```

Execution lineage SHOULD preserve provenance.

---

# 82. Deployment Relationships

Deployment semantics MAY include:

```text
Release
    deployed-to
Environment

Deployment
    deployed
Release

Deployment
    produced
RuntimeState
```

Release and Deployment MUST remain distinct semantic entities.

---

# 83. Verification Relationships

Verification MAY relate:

```text
Verification
    evaluates
Requirement

Verification
    evaluated
Revision

Verification
    supported-by
Evidence
```

A passing verification relationship MUST remain bound to the applicable scope and revision.

---

# 84. Review Relationships

Reviews MAY produce relationships such as:

```text
Review
    examines
Artifact

Finding
    produced-by
Review

Finding
    concerns
Subject
```

Review MUST NOT be collapsed into approval or verification.

---

# 85. Release Relationships

A Release MAY relate to:

```text
artifact
revision
verification
approval
deployment
migration
compatibility declaration
```

These relationships SHOULD be typed rather than represented as an unstructured list.

---

# 86. Historical Relationships

Monad SHOULD preserve historically valid relationships where necessary for:

* auditability,
* incident analysis,
* release reconstruction,
* provenance,
* decision history.

---

# 87. Current Relationship

A relationship may be designated current under applicable temporal, authority, and lifecycle rules.

`current` MUST NOT mean merely:

```text
most recently stored
```

---

# 88. Relationship Canonicality

A relationship MAY be canonical within a defined scope.

Canonicality SHOULD be derived from or governed by:

* authority,
* provenance,
* Evidence,
* policy,
* reconciliation,
* temporal validity.

---

# 89. Competing Relationships

Multiple relationship assertions MAY coexist even if only one is currently canonical.

Example:

```text
Architecture document:
    Service A depends-on B

Runtime observation:
    Service A depends-on C
```

The divergence may be meaningful engineering information.

---

# 90. Reconciliation

Relationships concerning native or observable state SHOULD participate in reconciliation.

Potential reconciliation states include:

```text
agreement
divergence
stale
missing
ambiguous
unauthorized
unobservable
```

---

# 91. Missing Relationship

If a canonical relationship is absent from observed native state, reconciliation SHOULD distinguish among possibilities such as:

* actual deletion,
* observation failure,
* incomplete integration,
* changed identity,
* stale projection,
* unauthorized drift.

---

# 92. Unexpected Relationship

If native observation reveals an unmodeled relationship, Monad SHOULD preserve it as an observation rather than immediately deleting or promoting it.

Governance determines whether remediation is appropriate.

---

# 93. Relationship Inference Rules

Domains MAY define inference rules.

For example:

```text
A contains B
B contains C
```

may imply:

```text
A transitively-contains C
```

if containment semantics support transitivity.

Inference rules MUST be explicit and versionable.

---

# 94. Inference Provenance

Every material inferred relationship SHOULD retain:

* rule or algorithm,
* inputs,
* producer,
* time,
* confidence where relevant.

---

# 95. Inference Does Not Rewrite Source

Derived relationships MUST NOT alter the source relationships from which they were inferred.

---

# 96. Materialized Derived Relationships

Implementations MAY materialize derived relationships for performance.

Such materialization SHOULD be reconstructable and MUST remain distinguishable from source declarations.

---

# 97. Transitive Closure

The system MAY compute transitive closure for selected relationship types.

Closure computation MUST be relationship-type-aware.

Universal graph closure across arbitrary edge types is semantically invalid.

---

# 98. Mixed-Type Traversal

Queries MAY traverse multiple relationship types.

Example:

```text
Requirement
    implemented-by
Component
    depends-on
Library
    contains
Vulnerable Package
```

Mixed traversal MUST preserve each predicate rather than treating the path as homogeneous.

---

# 99. Path Semantics

A graph path does not automatically imply a valid higher-level relationship between its endpoints.

Path interpretation MUST depend on the predicates involved.

---

# 100. Relationship Distance

Graph distance MAY be useful for navigation.

It MUST NOT automatically be interpreted as:

* semantic similarity,
* causal importance,
* organizational closeness,
* engineering risk.

---

# 101. Impact Analysis

Impact analysis MAY use relationships such as:

```text
depends-on
implements
contains
deployed-to
consumes
produces
```

Impact engines SHOULD distinguish:

* direct impact,
* transitive impact,
* inferred impact,
* speculative impact.

---

# 102. Blast Radius

A blast radius MAY be derived from relationship traversal.

Derived blast radius MUST preserve:

* relationship paths,
* traversal rules,
* excluded relationship types,
* confidence or uncertainty where applicable.

---

# 103. Relationship Density

Monad MUST avoid treating maximum graph density as a goal.

A dense graph with weak semantics may be less useful than a sparse graph with precise predicates.

---

# 104. Relationship Economy

Relationships SHOULD be added when they materially improve:

* explanation,
* navigation,
* impact analysis,
* traceability,
* reconciliation,
* governance,
* verification,
* AI context.

Incidental or low-value relationships SHOULD NOT be formalized merely because they can be.

---

# 105. Generic `related-to`

A generic `related-to` relationship MAY exist for low-specificity associations.

It SHOULD NOT replace a more precise predicate when that precision affects reasoning.

---

# 106. Promotion of Generic Relationships

A generic relationship MAY later be refined into a more precise predicate.

The refinement SHOULD preserve provenance and historical traceability where the earlier generic association mattered.

---

# 107. Semantic Similarity

Semantic similarity MAY produce candidate relationships.

Example:

```text
Document A similar-to Document B
```

Similarity MUST NOT automatically produce:

```text
same-as
implements
supersedes
duplicate-of
```

---

# 108. AI-Generated Relationships

AI systems MAY propose relationships.

Such relationships SHOULD default to candidate or inferred status unless authoritative confirmation exists.

---

# 109. AI Relationship Provenance

Consequential AI-generated relationships SHOULD preserve:

* model/provider identity where material,
* context used,
* producer,
* timestamp,
* confidence if available,
* source artifacts,
* reasoning inputs where representable.

---

# 110. AI Authority

An AI model's ability to discover a likely relationship does not grant authority to establish canonical engineering state.

Capability ≠ authority.

---

# 111. Human-Generated Relationships

Human-authored relationships likewise require appropriate authority when they establish canonical state.

Human authorship alone is not universal authority.

---

# 112. Native Relationship Preservation

Provider-specific relationships SHOULD preserve provider semantics even when Monad maps them to broader canonical relationship types.

Example:

```text
GitHub "blocks"
```

must not be silently treated as identical to every possible generic dependency relationship.

---

# 113. Native Relationship Mapping

A native relationship MAY map to:

```text
canonical relationship
+
provider-specific metadata
```

when the mapping is sufficiently faithful.

Lossy mappings MUST be identifiable.

---

# 114. Mapping Uncertainty

If the system cannot determine a safe canonical mapping, it SHOULD preserve the native relationship without inventing stronger semantics.

---

# 115. Portability

Canonical relationship semantics SHOULD survive:

* repository movement,
* provider migration,
* storage migration,
* serialization changes,
* operating-system changes.

Relationship identity MUST NOT depend unnecessarily on provider-specific identifiers.

---

# 116. Provider Migration

When a native system changes, historical native relationships SHOULD remain traceable where possible.

Example:

```text
GitHub issue relation
```

migrated to:

```text
GitLab issue relation
```

should not erase the historical GitHub binding.

---

# 117. Serialization

Relationship serialization MUST preserve enough information to reconstruct:

* identity,
* type,
* endpoints,
* required scope,
* provenance,
* relevant temporal semantics.

---

# 118. Unknown Relationship Types

Importers SHOULD preserve unknown extension relationship types where possible.

They MUST NOT silently coerce unknown predicates into `related-to`.

---

# 119. Schema Versioning

Relationship representations MUST identify applicable schema or vocabulary versions where interpretation may vary across versions.

---

# 120. Local-First Operation

Core relationship state SHOULD remain locally inspectable in local-first deployments.

Remote provider availability SHOULD NOT be required to understand canonical relationship semantics already stored locally.

---

# 121. Offline Creation

Relationships MAY be created or inferred offline.

Later synchronization MUST support reconciliation of:

* duplicates,
* conflicting relationships,
* stale endpoints,
* identity changes,
* divergent authority.

---

# 122. Relationship Security

Access control MAY independently govern:

```text
read
create
modify
approve
delete
export
```

relationship operations.

Technical access MUST remain distinct from semantic authority.

---

# 123. Sensitive Relationships

Some relationships may expose sensitive engineering information.

Examples:

```text
service-to-database topology
security control dependencies
incident causality
organizational responsibility
```

Systems SHOULD support appropriate classification and access control.

---

# 124. Relationship Retention

Retention policy SHOULD consider whether relationships participate in:

* releases,
* incidents,
* decisions,
* provenance,
* audit,
* approvals,
* ownership,
* verification,
* identity transitions.

---

# 125. Relationship Deletion

Deleting a relationship MUST NOT implicitly delete either endpoint.

Deletion consequences for derived relationships SHOULD be explicit.

---

# 126. Dangling Relationships

Persisted relationships SHOULD NOT silently reference nonexistent endpoints unless the model explicitly supports unresolved or externally referenced entities.

Dangling references SHOULD be detectable.

---

# 127. External Endpoints

A relationship MAY reference an external entity not fully represented as a Monad Subject.

Such references SHOULD use a durable Native Reference or external identifier representation sufficient to preserve meaning.

---

# 128. Unresolved Endpoints

Monad MAY represent unresolved relationship endpoints when source material refers to an entity that cannot yet be identified.

Unresolved state MUST remain explicit.

---

# 129. Endpoint Resolution

Later resolution of an unresolved endpoint SHOULD preserve the provenance of how the binding was established.

---

# 130. Ambiguous Endpoint

If multiple possible Subjects match a relationship endpoint, Monad MUST NOT arbitrarily choose one without justified resolution.

Ambiguity SHOULD remain represented.

---

# 131. Relationship Validation

Relationship validation SHOULD occur in layers.

## Level 1 — Structural

Verify:

* required fields,
* valid relationship identifier,
* source and target presence,
* syntactic type validity.

## Level 2 — Referential

Verify:

* source resolves,
* target resolves,
* referenced type exists.

## Level 3 — Type

Verify:

* allowed source type,
* allowed target type,
* relationship property types.

## Level 4 — Cardinality

Verify:

* multiplicity constraints,
* uniqueness constraints.

## Level 5 — Semantic

Verify:

* valid scope,
* temporal coherence,
* relationship-specific invariants.

## Level 6 — Provenance

Verify required origin and derivation information.

## Level 7 — Authority

Verify authority where establishing canonical state requires it.

---

# 132. Structural Validity ≠ Semantic Truth

A relationship may pass schema validation while still being:

* false,
* stale,
* unauthorized,
* contradictory,
* inferred,
* semantically inappropriate.

Schema validation MUST NOT be presented as proof of truth.

---

# 133. Relationship Type Constraints

A relationship vocabulary SHOULD permit constraints such as:

```text
source types
target types
symmetry
transitivity
inverse
cardinality
required properties
allowed scopes
```

---

# 134. Invalid Predicate Use

If a predicate defined for:

```text
Requirement → Verification
```

is used between:

```text
Repository → Human
```

the system SHOULD reject or flag the relationship unless the vocabulary permits such usage.

---

# 135. Relationship Uniqueness

Some relationship types MAY require semantic uniqueness.

Example:

```text
Release has-version "1.2.0"
```

might be single-valued within a scope.

Other relationship types may legitimately have multiple targets.

Uniqueness MUST be type-specific.

---

# 136. Relationship Canonicalization

Canonicalization MAY normalize:

* predicate identifiers,
* endpoint identifiers,
* property ordering,
* serialization form.

Canonicalization MUST NOT change semantic meaning.

---

# 137. Relationship Hashing

A deterministic representation MAY be hashed for:

* integrity,
* deduplication,
* caching.

Hash equality MUST NOT substitute for semantic identity unless explicitly defined.

---

# 138. Duplicate Relationship Assertions

Two independent sources MAY assert the same relationship.

Monad SHOULD preserve independent provenance even if the read model presents one consolidated relationship.

---

# 139. Consolidated Relationship View

A read model MAY display:

```text
A depends-on B
```

once while aggregating multiple supporting assertions.

The underlying provenance MUST remain recoverable.

---

# 140. Relationship Projection

Read models MAY project relationship state for performance or usability.

Projection freshness SHOULD be available when stale data could affect engineering decisions.

---

# 141. Projection Failure

A failed or stale relationship projection MUST NOT alter canonical semantic state.

---

# 142. Relationship Indexes

Indexes MAY accelerate:

* outbound lookup,
* inbound lookup,
* type filtering,
* traversal,
* temporal queries,
* provenance lookup.

Indexes SHOULD be reconstructable from canonical relationship state.

---

# 143. Search and Discovery

Search MAY help discover relationship candidates.

Search result proximity or keyword co-occurrence MUST NOT establish a semantic relationship by itself.

---

# 144. Relationship Querying

Monad SHOULD support queries such as:

* What does Subject A depend on?
* What depends on A?
* Which requirements does Component C implement?
* Which Evidence supports Claim X?
* Which decisions constrain Specification S?
* What superseded Artifact A?
* What was deployed with Release R?
* Which relationships are inferred?
* Which relationships conflict?
* Which relationships are stale?
* Which relationships existed at time T?

---

# 145. Predicate-Specific Querying

Clients SHOULD be able to request exact relationship semantics rather than receiving all adjacent graph edges indiscriminately.

---

# 146. Directional Querying

Queries SHOULD distinguish:

```text
outgoing
incoming
```

relationships where direction matters.

---

# 147. Historical Relationship Query

Where history is retained, Monad SHOULD support:

> What relationships were considered valid at time T?

---

# 148. Relationship Provenance Query

The system SHOULD support:

> Why does Monad believe A depends on B?

A useful answer may include:

* source declaration,
* observation,
* producer,
* Evidence,
* inference path,
* authority.

---

# 149. Relationship Explanation

Consequential relationships SHOULD be explainable in human-understandable form.

Example:

```text
API Service depends on Identity Service

Basis:
- declared in architecture specification
- observed in runtime traces
- confirmed in source dependency analysis

Status:
- canonical
- current
```

---

# 150. AI Context Construction

When relationships are supplied to AI systems, context SHOULD preserve:

* predicate semantics,
* direction,
* scope,
* provenance,
* authority,
* confidence,
* temporal state,
* whether relationship is direct or derived.

Flattening all relationships into generic prose SHOULD be avoided when doing so would mislead reasoning.

---

# 151. Relationship Falsifiability

Relationships asserting observable engineering reality SHOULD be defined precisely enough to identify possible contradicting Evidence.

Example:

```text
Service A calls Service B at runtime
```

can be challenged by runtime observations.

---

# 152. Relationship Complexity Budget

Monad MUST NOT require a universal ontology for every possible engineering relationship before the model becomes useful.

The initial vocabulary SHOULD prioritize relationships required for:

* identity,
* traceability,
* provenance,
* governance,
* execution,
* verification,
* reconciliation.

---

# 153. Minimal Useful Relationship Model

A minimal durable relationship requires:

```text
relationship type
source
target
```

When consequential, it SHOULD additionally support:

```text
identity
provenance
scope
time
lifecycle
```

```yaml
relationship:
  id: rel:01K...

  type: depends-on

  source:
    subject: service:api

  target:
    subject: service:identity
```

# 155. Claim-Backed Relationship Example

```yaml

  id: claim:01K...

  proposition:
    relationship:
      type: implements
      source: component:auth
      target: requirement:REQ-042

  epistemic_status: inferred

  provenance:
    producer: ai-analysis

  confidence: 0.84
```

The relationship is not automatically canonical.

---

# 156. Native Relationship Example

```text
Native observation:
    package.json
    app → @monad/core

Native relationship:
    declared-package-dependency

Potential semantic mapping:
    Application Component
        build-depends-on
    Core Library
```

The native relationship and semantic interpretation remain distinguishable.

---

# 157. Temporal Example

```text
R1:
    Service A deployed-to Cluster Blue
    valid: Jan 1 – Feb 15

R2:
    Service A deployed-to Cluster Green
    valid: Feb 15 onward
```

Both may be historically correct.

---

# 158. Conflict Example

```text
R1:
    Component A owned-by Team Alpha

R2:
    Component A owned-by Team Beta
```

If ownership is single-valued in the relevant scope:

```text
conflict = true
```

Monad SHOULD retain both until authority and reconciliation resolve the state.

---

# 159. Inference Example

Given:

```text
Requirement R
    specified-by
Specification S

Specification S
    implemented-by
Component C
```

Monad MUST NOT automatically infer:

```text
Requirement R
    implemented-by
Component C
```

unless an explicit inference rule authorizes this semantic composition.

---

# 160. Dependency Impact Example

```text
Service A → Library B → Package C
```

A vulnerability in C may create transitive impact on A.

The impact result SHOULD retain the path:

```text
A
  depends-on
B
  depends-on
C
```

rather than reporting an unexplained direct dependency.

---

# 161. Anti-Patterns

## 161.1 Everything is `related-to`

```text
A related-to B
```

for every connection.

This destroys semantic precision.

---

## 161.2 Edge direction ignored

Treating:

```text
Requirement implemented-by Component
```

as equivalent to:

```text
Component implemented-by Requirement
```

is invalid.

---

## 161.3 Dependency implies causality

A dependency edge is interpreted as proof that one component caused an incident.

This is non-conforming.

---

## 161.4 Similarity implies identity

Two similar documents are connected using `same-as`.

Similarity is not semantic identity.

---

## 161.5 Filesystem hierarchy equals architecture

Directory containment is automatically treated as component containment.

Physical organization and semantic architecture may differ.

---

## 161.6 Inferred edge becomes canonical

AI proposes:

```text
Requirement implemented-by Module
```

and the relationship is immediately stored as authoritative.

Inference ≠ authority.

---

## 161.7 Duplicate edges destroy provenance

Two independent observations are collapsed into one edge and all source provenance is discarded.

This reduces epistemic value.

---

## 161.8 New edge overwrites old edge

A new relationship replaces historical state without temporal or lifecycle history.

Historical truth is lost.

---

## 161.9 Universal transitivity

The engine computes transitive closure across every relationship type.

This produces semantically invalid conclusions.

---

## 161.10 Graph density as quality metric

More edges are treated as inherently better.

Relationship usefulness depends on semantic precision and engineering value.

---

# 162. Non-Requirements

This specification does **not** require:

* a graph database,
* RDF,
* OWL,
* SPARQL,
* one global ontology,
* every relationship to possess first-class identity,
* automatic inference,
* automatic transitive closure,
* every native relationship to map to a Monad core predicate,
* graph visualization,
* embedding-based relationship discovery,
* AI relationship extraction,
* universal causal inference,
* formal proof systems.

# 163. Conformance Scenario — Rename

Given:

```text
Subject A
    depends-on
Subject B
```

and B's primary file moves from:

```text
src/core.ts
```

to:

```text
packages/core/src/index.ts
```

Then the semantic dependency MUST remain the same relationship if Subject identity and semantics remain unchanged.

---

# 164. Conformance Scenario — Dependency vs Causality

Given:

```text
Service A depends-on Database B
```

and B fails before A,

Monad MUST NOT automatically create:

```text
Database B caused-failure-of Service A
```

without separate causal Evidence or analysis.

---

# 165. Conformance Scenario — AI Inference

Given an AI model proposes:

```text
Requirement R implemented-by Module M
```

Then:

* the relationship MAY be stored as a candidate,
* AI provenance MUST remain inspectable,
* confidence MAY be recorded,
* canonical acceptance requires applicable authority or confirmation.

---

# 166. Conformance Scenario — Native Mapping

Given GitHub reports:

```text
Issue A blocks Issue B
```

Monad MAY map this to an internal blocking relationship.

The original GitHub relationship semantics and native reference SHOULD remain inspectable.

---

# 167. Conformance Scenario — Conflicting Ownership

Given two authoritative systems declare different owning teams,

Monad MUST:

* preserve both assertions,
* identify the overlapping scope,
* surface the conflict,
* avoid resolving solely by recency.

---

# 168. Conformance Scenario — Historical Deployment

Given Service A moved from Cluster X to Cluster Y,

Then:

* current queries MAY return Y,
* historical queries MAY return X for the earlier interval,
* X MUST NOT be erased merely because it is no longer current.

---

# 169. Conformance Scenario — Unknown Relationship

Given Monad has no recorded dependency between A and B,

Monad MUST NOT conclude:

```text
A does not depend on B
```

unless the dependency domain explicitly guarantees complete knowledge.

---

# 170. Conformance Scenario — Direct vs Transitive

Given:

```text
A depends-on B
B depends-on C
```

Then a query MAY report:

```text
A transitively depends-on C
```

if the relationship semantics permit.

It MUST NOT report C as a direct dependency of A unless that direct relationship independently exists.

---

# 171. Conformance Scenario — Representation

Given one Subject has:

```text
Markdown Representation
HTML Representation
Database Projection
```

Then these SHOULD all relate to the same Subject without being collapsed into one Representation identity.

---

# 172. Conformance Scenario — Supersession

Given:

```text
ADR-002 supersedes ADR-001
```

Then:

* both Subjects remain independently addressable,
* the relationship is directional,
* ADR-001 remains historically inspectable,
* supersession MUST NOT imply `same-as`.

# 176. Definition of Ready

This specification is ready for baseline acceptance when:

* relationship identity is separated from endpoint identity,
* relationship type is normative,
* directionality is explicit,
* dependency is distinguished from causality,
* chronology is distinguished from causality,
* similarity is distinguished from identity,
* relationship provenance is preserved,
* inferred relationships remain epistemically distinct,
* relationship scope and time are representable,
* first-class relationship properties are supported where needed,
* conflicts can coexist without destructive resolution,
* native relationship semantics can be preserved,
* traceability relationships are sufficiently precise,
* transitivity is explicitly type-specific.

---

# 177. Definition of Done

The Relationships specification is complete for the foundational specification baseline when:

* every semantic-kernel relationship obligation maps to a normative clause,
* downstream provenance work can express derivation safely,
* Claims can assert relationship propositions,
* Evidence can support or contradict relationship Claims,
* Identity can express alias, split, merge, and continuity relationships,
* traceability can connect requirements through implementation and verification,
* reconciliation can compare canonical and observed relationships,
* AI interaction can create candidate relationships without silently establishing truth,
* schema work can define predicates without redefining relationship semantics,
* conformance scenarios cover primary semantic failure modes.

# 180. Governing Principle

Monad should model relationships precisely enough to preserve the reasoning structure of an engineering system.

It must know the difference between:

```text
depends on
```

and:

```text
caused by
```

between:

```text
similar to
```

and:

```text
same as
```

between:

```text
authored by
```

and:

```text
authorized by
```

between:

```text
occurred before
```

and:

```text
produced
```

between:

```text
associated with
```

and:

```text
implements
```

The governing principle is:

> **A graph becomes useful not when everything is connected, but when the meaning of each consequential connection is preserved.**

Monad should therefore prefer a smaller number of trustworthy, typed, explainable relationships over a large graph of ambiguous edges.
