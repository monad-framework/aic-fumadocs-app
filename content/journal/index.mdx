The Engineering Journal is the **chronological working record of Monad**.

It captures engineering activity close to when it happens: investigations, implementation notes, experiments, failures, discoveries, revisions, unresolved questions, and changes in understanding.

<Callout type="info" title="Historical record, not current authority">

Journal entries preserve what was observed, attempted, or believed at a particular point in the project.

They are intentionally allowed to become outdated. When a journal entry conflicts with an accepted requirement, specification, ADR, verification result, or current [System documentation](/docs/system), the authoritative artifact takes precedence.

</Callout>

## What belongs in Journal?

Journal is deliberately closer to the work than the rest of the public knowledge base.

| Record | Belongs here? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A question being investigated | Yes | The uncertainty itself is part of the engineering record. |
| Implementation progress and debugging discoveries | Yes | They explain how the system evolved. |
| An experiment and its result | Yes | Journal can preserve the activity before evidence is formalized elsewhere. |
| A failed approach | Yes | Failure is historical information, not noise. |
| A decision still being worked out | Yes | Use `decision-in-progress`; do not present it as an accepted ADR. |
| An accepted architectural decision | Not as authority | Record the transition, then link to the authoritative ADR. |
| A normative system requirement | No | Requirements belong under [Artifacts](/docs/artifacts/requirements). |
| The current explanation of how Monad works | No | That belongs under [System](/docs/system). |
| A polished interpretation of a period of work | Usually not | That belongs in [Building Monad](/docs/building-monad) or Articles. |

## Journal and Building Monad are related, but different

The Journal and Building Monad describe some of the same project history at different levels of interpretation.

**Journal asks:** What happened while we were doing the work?

**Building Monad asks:** What does that work mean in the larger engineering story?

A useful relationship is:

```text
engineering work
      ↓
journal entry
      ↓
formal artifacts + evidence
      ↓
current system state
      ↓
Building Monad synthesis
```

The flow is not a promotion pipeline in which the Journal disappears after publication. The journal entry remains a historical record even after its observations become formal artifacts or are synthesized into an installment or essay.

Cross-links preserve the relationship without making Journal part of `/docs`.

## The four public record types

The site now maintains four intentionally different kinds of publication:

### Journal — what happened while working?

Chronological, immediate, and allowed to age.

### Building Monad — how did the project evolve?

Curated, retrospective enough to form a coherent engineering narrative, but still historical rather than normative.

### Artifacts — what can be inspected and verified?

Requirements, specifications, ADRs, schemas, evidence, reviews, verification results, and other formal engineering records.

### System — what is true now?

The current authoritative explanation of Monad.

Keeping those roles separate prevents history from masquerading as current truth and prevents current documentation from erasing the path that produced it.

## Chronology

Journal entry filenames begin with an ISO date and a three-digit sequence number:

```text
YYYY-MM-DD-NNN-short-slug.mdx
```

For example:

```text
2026-08-30-001-establishing-the-engineering-journal.mdx
```

The corresponding durable entry ID is:

```text
JRN-2026-08-30-001
```

The sidebar lists entries in **reverse chronological order**, so the newest work stays nearest the top while URLs and IDs remain stable.

If several entries are published on the same date, increment the sequence number rather than relying on timestamps.

## Entry states

A journal entry can be:

- **working** — the matter is active or its conclusions remain provisional;
- **resolved** — the immediate question or work item reached a conclusion;
- **superseded** — later work materially replaced the entry's position or conclusion;
- **retrospective** — the entry was intentionally written after the period it describes.

Status describes the **state of the journal record**, not the authority of its claims. Journal remains non-authoritative regardless of status.
